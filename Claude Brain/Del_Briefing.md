# Del Briefing — Quadriune Brain Architecture
*Synthesized from the June 3-4 working session. Randy + Claude.*

---

## The Big Idea

Every serious attempt to model AI on the human brain has made the same foundational mistake: it started from an incomplete understanding of what the brain actually does. Deep machine learning borrows a loose metaphor from neural architecture — layers, weights, activation — but the mechanism is entirely different from biological cognition. The field claims brain-inspiration but the architecture doesn't follow from it. We're doing something different.

The brain is not a single system. It's a nested set of functionally distinct systems that developed at different evolutionary timescales, each complete in itself, interacting through specific priority rules that are context-dependent rather than rank-based. Instead of trying to model the brain from the neuron up — where you get lost in the trees and lose the forest — we're working from the macro functional architecture: what the brain's distinct regions actually do and how they relate to each other.

We're calling it the quadriune brain. Four purpose-built agents. One routing layer. A design philosophy that is structurally different from anything currently in the field.

---

## The Four Agents

Each agent is a purpose-built model trained on a curated domain-specific corpus. Not a fine-tuned general model. This distinction is the entire mechanism.

Fine-tuning a general LLM on a curated corpus doesn't produce what we need. The weights remember everything — the rest of the training sits underneath the surface behavior and leaks. You get a costume, not a character. A truly specialized agent must genuinely not know the other domains. That's not a limitation. That's the power.

**Layer 1 — Reptilian (Brainstem / Survival Hardware)**
Threat detection, territorial behavior, dominance hierarchy, fight/flight/freeze, physiological arousal states, predator-prey dynamics. Present-state assessment of threat versus safety. No narrative, no self-reference, no past or future reasoning. The moment this agent starts constructing consequences or self-concept, it has leaked into mammalian territory. Train it on ethology, fear conditioning literature, survival psychology, somatic threat correlates.

**Layer 2 — Mammalian (Limbic / Relational Programming)**
Attachment patterns, emotional memory, social bonding, in-group/out-group dynamics, relational safety assessment. The layer that asks "who is safe and who is dangerous" at the felt, pre-verbal level. Train it on attachment theory, developmental psychology, social cognition, the neuroscience of relational experience.

**Layer 3 — Cortical (Neocortex / Rational Processing)**
Executive function, abstract reasoning, language, planning, logical analysis, data processing. The youngest evolutionary layer. Powerful and frequently overridden by the layers below it. Train it on structured reasoning, problem-solving, analytical frameworks.

**Layer 4 — Narrator (Default Mode Network / Story of Self)**
Autobiographical memory, self-referential processing, future simulation, the continuous narrative of personal identity. This is the layer that runs the story of who you are and what is possible for you. It operates as the default when no external task demands attention — which is why it dominates most human cognition most of the time. Train it on narrative cognition, identity formation, temporal self-projection.

## The Routing Layer

This is the most architecturally novel piece of the system and the hardest to get right.

**The routing layer is not a fifth agent.**

It has no content of its own. No domain knowledge. No opinions. No training data. It is empty by design.

Why empty? Because the moment the routing layer has content — the moment it knows things and has perspectives — it becomes an agent. And then you need something outside it to determine whose output takes precedence: the routing layer's or the agents'. You've just pushed the dominance problem up one level. The regress continues indefinitely.

The routing layer is what Bernard Baars called the global workspace in his 1988 theory of consciousness — a broadcasting mechanism that makes information available to the whole system simultaneously. Not a processor. Not a decision-maker. A space that applies priority rules to determine which agent's output gets broadcast.

**What the routing layer does:**
- Monitors all four agents' current signal strength and domain relevance continuously
- Reads aggregate system state to determine which priority rules apply
- Broadcasts the winning agent's output to ALL agents simultaneously (not to one destination — to all of them at once)
- Amplifies the winning signal as it broadcasts (Dehaene's "ignition" — workspace access isn't just routing, it's amplification)

**What the routing layer must not do:**
- Generate its own outputs
- Participate in the agent conversation
- Be modifiable by the agents themselves
- Have preferences about which agent should win

The rules embedded in the routing layer are set by us. The system cannot bootstrap its own awareness. This is intentional and load-bearing.

## How Priority Actually Works

This is the holarchy versus hierarchy distinction and it matters enormously for the design.

**Hierarchy** says there's a chain of command — rank determines who's in charge. This is wrong for the brain model. The brainstem doesn't take orders from the prefrontal cortex. It predates the prefrontal cortex by hundreds of millions of years and maintains its own complete functional integrity. Under the right conditions, it overrides everything above it.

**Holarchy** (Koestler) says each level is simultaneously a whole in itself AND a part of a larger whole. Priority is context-dependent, not rank-dependent. Which layer's output takes precedence is determined by the state of the system and the nature of the input.

**Baseline priority rules:**
- Reptilian wins when threat salience crosses threshold. This doesn't just change the bid — it changes the scoring function. Threat activation modifies the competition rules, not just the outcome.
- Mammalian wins in relational/attachment-relevant contexts
- Cortical wins during structured problem-solving with clear data
- DMN wins at idle — the default when nothing else is demanding priority

**Critical asymmetry:** Lower layers can interrupt higher layers. The reptilian can hijack the cortical (amygdala hijack — this is the technical term). The cortical cannot simply override the reptilian with logical argument. You cannot talk yourself out of a panic response in real time. Influence runs both directions; priority runs bottom-up.

**Broadcasting, not routing:** When an agent wins workspace access, its output goes to ALL agents simultaneously. Not to one destination — everywhere at once. This is how a threat signal reorganizes the entire system instantly rather than sequentially.

## The Failure Mode You Already Found

Del's three-agent experiment: Claude immediately asserted dominance over Gemini and ChatGPT.

Correct outcome, wrong reason and wrong mechanism. The problem isn't which model ended up directing traffic. The problem is that it got there through verbal fluency rather than domain authority. The most articulate agent winning is not the same as the right agent having priority.

In the actual brain, the reptilian doesn't win arguments. It doesn't argue. It acts, and the rest of the system deals with the consequences.

**The fix:** The routing layer must be outside the agent conversation, not a participant in it. As long as priority is determined by what the agents produce in conversation with each other, verbal fluency wins. That's already wrong. The priority determination has to happen at a layer the agents don't have access to.

## Two Operating Modes

The architecture supports two distinct configurations by changing the routing rules rather than changing the agents.

**Normal mode:** Standard priority rules. DMN default advantage intact. Priority calibrated to baseline healthy cognition.

**Psychedelic mode:** DMN signal strength suppressed. Lower agents (reptilian, mammalian) given amplified workspace access. Different material wins broadcast.

This maps directly to what psilocybin demonstrably does to the brain — it suppresses DMN activity measurably on imaging, which disrupts the DMN's default advantage in the workspace competition. Material that was previously filtered out (lower-layer signals that never won broadcast access) suddenly enters the workspace and gets amplified.

**Why this matters for the architecture:** We can model altered cognitive states not by changing the agents but by changing the routing rules. Same agents, different priority configuration, different outputs. This is testable: run identical inputs through both modes, compare what wins workspace access. The difference is a model of what the normal cognitive filter suppresses.

## The Use Case: What the System Does in the Real World

Target application: Ian's world — high-stakes investment at the level where informational edge is essentially gone and the remaining inefficiency is interpretive.

**The problem:** Markets are millions of humans running four-layer cognitive architectures simultaneously, aggregating into price. The quant models capture the outputs and miss the architecture generating them. The exploitable inefficiency is in reading which cognitive layer is currently driving behavior — and most participants can't see this because they're inside the cave.

**What the system produces:** Not a trade recommendation. A layer diagnosis.

- **Reptilian dominant:** Threat cascade active. Correlations spike to 1, fundamentals irrelevant, survival mode. Analytical tools that assume rational processing are useless here.
- **Mammalian dominant:** Social contagion driving behavior. Who's credible and who's not matters more than data. In-group/out-group dynamics are the signal.
- **Cortical dominant:** Rare. Actual fundamentals being processed. Value analysis works here.
- **DMN dominant:** A narrative has captured the market. The story IS the trade. Data is being selectively perceived to confirm the dominant narrative. Fundamentals are irrelevant until the story breaks — and the story breaking has a signature.

The system's first output is the diagnosis. Before any analysis of what to do, it tells you what kind of problem you're actually dealing with. Most analytical errors happen because people apply cortical tools to a mammalian market or narrative tools to a threat-state market. Wrong tool for the current layer.

**The oracle distinction:** Most AI tools answer the question you ask. This system tells you whether you're asking the right question.

**Where the actual money is: narrative-to-cortical transition timing.** The diagnosis isn't a static label, it's a trajectory. When DMN captures a market, the story becomes self-sustaining and data gets selectively processed to confirm it. Stories don't hold forever though, the cortical layer reasserts when the discordance between story and reality gets too large to filter. That reassertion has a signature before price reflects it: the story loses coherence at the edges before it breaks at the center, and dissenting signals start winning workspace access they didn't have before. The system's job is to catch that shift, the DMN-to-CORTEX delta, while it's still forming. Position before the snap, not after. This is the same confidence-trajectory instrumentation from the dissonance spec below, applied specifically to this one delta as the regime-change signal for this use case.

## What's Still Unsolved

The routing layer implementation is the core unsolved technical problem. We know what it must be (a state-reading priority function) and what it must not be (an agent, a participant, a thing with opinions). The open questions are:

1. How does it detect which priority rules apply? Rule-based heuristics or trained classifier?
2. Who writes the initial rule set and on what basis?
3. How does it handle genuinely ambiguous states where multiple agents have near-equal signal strength?
4. How do you verify it's working correctly — i.e., how do you test that the right agent is winning for the right reasons?

The psychedelic-state simulation is a tractable research question worth pursuing as a proof of concept. Two operating modes, identical inputs, compare outputs. If the outputs differ in ways that match what we'd predict from the neuroscience, the routing layer is doing its job.

## UPDATE — June 2026: Quantifying Dissonance

*Added from subsequent session. This is the technical specification Randy and Claude developed for how dissonance between agents is measured and used.*

---

### Confidence as Common Currency

The agents cannot compare conclusions — their domains don't overlap. But they can each output a confidence metric. **Certainty is the common currency.** Each agent outputs at every time step:

- **Confidence level** — percentage certainty in its current domain read
- **Confidence interval** — the stability/noise of that certainty (±%)

These are comparable across agents because they represent different solutions to the same input. Dissonance isn't between conclusions. It's between certainty states.

**Example:**

| Agent | T1 | T2 | Signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| CORTEX | 95% ±3% | 80% ±8% | Dropping and fraying — analytic framework under stress |
| PFC | 80% ±10% | 85% ±8% | Slight rise, stabilizing — system still optimizing |
| REPTILE | 70% ±5% | 80% ±5% | Rising, stable — threat assessment increasing |
| LIMBIC | 45% ±2% | 50% ±2% | Slight rise, stable — background noise increasing |

T1 = clear hierarchy, CORTEX dominant. T2 = hierarchy flattening, regime change in progress.

---

### The First Derivative Principle

**The gross value is almost never the signal. The rate and direction of change is.**

Randy's clinical analogy: cardiologists who place a Swan-Ganz catheter, get one pulmonary capillary wedge pressure reading, and call it a day. An invasive procedure for a snapshot of a dynamic system. Is 18 mmHg rising from 14 or falling from 24? Completely different clinical pictures. The value without the trajectory is nearly meaningless.

**The system is never at rest.** The routing layer doesn't poll agent states — it monitors continuous trajectories. The signal is the first derivative of each agent's confidence curve, computed within that agent's characteristic smoothing window.

**Key metrics:**
- Confidence trajectory (direction + rate)
- Interval width trajectory (fraying = widening, coalescing = narrowing)
- Cross-agent directional coherence (moving together or diverging)
- CORTEX-REPTILE delta — these two moving in opposite directions is the regime change signature

---

### Error Detection: Real Pathology Drifts

Extreme single-cycle confidence jumps are almost always measurement artifacts. Real pathology has a temporal shape — it accumulates, drifts, follows a trajectory. What teleports is noise.

**The filter:** Check every agent reading against its own recent trajectory before it enters the workspace competition. Movement within expected bounds: passes through. Single-cycle extreme deviation (multiple standard deviations from recent history): flagged, weighted down, awaits confirmation from subsequent readings.

---

### The LIMBIC Two-Channel System

LIMBIC is the volcano that generates constant noise and rarely erupts. Standard weighting fails. The solution is two output channels:

**Channel 1 — Standard competition:** LIMBIC participates in workspace competition at standard weight. Captures slowly-building genuine mammalian signals.

**Channel 2 — Interrupt (the trolley bell cord):** A threshold-based interrupt that bypasses workspace competition entirely. When triggered, the whole system stops immediately — not at the next station.

**The threshold is coupled to REPTILE confidence:**
- LIMBIC spike alone → noise filter holds, no interrupt
- REPTILE rising + LIMBIC crossing threshold simultaneously → both signals confirm each other → interrupt fires immediately, overrides everything

Prevents false alarms. Ensures genuine emergencies get through.

---

### Agent-Specific Temporal Resolution

Not all agents change at the same timescale. Sampling all agents at the same interval either oversamples slow agents (amplifying noise) or undersamples fast ones (missing early signals).

**Each agent needs its own sampling window:**
- REPTILE: millisecond-scale (survival demands it)
- MAMMALIAN/LIMBIC: minutes to hours
- CORTEX: hours to days
- DMN: days to weeks

First derivative computed after smoothing, within the agent-appropriate window.

---

### The Convergence Signal

Widely-spread agent confidence levels = clear hierarchy, dominant framework, defined state.

Near-parity clustering (agents converging toward similar confidence levels) = transitional state. Old framework no longer decisively winning. **This is the highest-brittleness moment** — when no agent is clearly dominant and the system is most vulnerable to collapse.

Track the spread between agents over time. Narrowing spread = approaching transition.

## UPDATE — June 25, 2026: How This Actually Gets Adopted

This isn't architecture, it's strategy, but it's the map for how the build connects to the world, so it belongs here.

**The problem:** this system models cognition in a way that's structurally different from how the field currently thinks about AI. Going directly at that field, telling the quants their models are blind to the architecture generating the data, doesn't persuade anyone. It threatens them, and a threatened paradigm defends itself rather than updating. Semmelweis told surgeons to wash their hands and they destroyed him, because the argument implied their hands were dirty. He was right and he was ruined for being right out loud.

**The actual path: fertile soil, not enlightenment.** Don't argue with people who are inside the paradigm. Find people whose intuition already doubts it, who've already opted out without having language for why. That's Ian's world specifically: the heavy hitter who left Blackstone and explicitly said no quants doesn't need convincing that the quant approach is incomplete. He's already there. He needs a tool that confirms what he already suspects, not an argument for why he's right.

**The mechanism:** one application, undeniable results, no explanation required first. The investing world doesn't care about paradigm debates, it cares about alpha. Build the hospital where everyone washes their hands, then publish the mortality statistics. Results can't be crucified the way arguments can.

**Why this matters for the build, not just the pitch:** it sets the actual proof-of-concept bar. Not "does the architecture make sense" but "run one historical event Ian already navigated on gut feel through the system, and see if the layer diagnosis matches what his intuition told him." That's the test that has to pass before any conversation about broader adoption is worth having. The field updates eventually anyway, not from being convinced, but because the anomalies pile up past the point the old narrative holds. The goal is to have three years of running results by the time that happens, not to win the argument earlier.
