# Brief 01: The Carbon-Silicon Relationship

**House of Del, inaugural group project.**
**Issued:** 31 August 2026
**To:** Four frontier models, working independently
**Synthesis:** Randall Evans and Claude, after all four responses are in

---

## The question

**What, exactly, is the relationship between the human nervous system and the computing systems it works with, and what foundational assumptions define it?**

Not what the relationship should be. What it is, and what unexamined commitments hold it in place.

---

## Why this question

The infrastructure of this relationship was laid down in the 1960s. Since then it has drifted continuously and shifted never.

Borrowing the influenza vocabulary: **antigenic drift** is incremental variation within an existing architecture. **Antigenic shift** is a reassortment that produces something the prior immune system does not recognize.

Everything since 1968 has been drift. Memory, clock speed, storage. Input devices from the Osborne through wireless keyboards, trackpads, touchscreens, styluses. Displays, aesthetics, video. Real improvements, all of them incremental variations on an architecture nobody has revisited.

The working hypothesis is that the architecture underneath carries assumptions that were reasonable in 1965, were never subsequently argued for, and are now load-bearing precisely because they are invisible.

Your job is to find them and state them.

---

## The prohibition

**Describe. Do not solve.**

No recommendations. No product concepts. No roadmaps. No "this suggests an opportunity to." No closing section proposing what should be built.

This prohibition is the most important instruction in the brief, and it exists because premature solutioning locks the frame. A solution proposed at this stage will be a solution to whichever friction point happened to be salient, and the entire point of the exercise is to find out whether the salient friction points are the real ones.

If you find yourself writing a recommendation, you have left the assignment. Delete it and return to description.

---

## What we already believe, offered so you can attack it

The following is context, not doctrine. Each item is something we currently think. Several are probably wrong. **Disagreement with this section is a valid and welcome output.**

**1. The 1960s were not thoughtless.** Licklider's *Man-Computer Symbiosis* (1960) is an explicit design brief for a partnership. Engelbart's *Augmenting Human Intellect* (1962) proposed a framework in which the human, the language, the artifacts, the methodology and the training co-evolve as a single system. The foundation was examined. Then a fork was taken.

**2. The fork was ease-of-use over co-evolution.** Engelbart held that the human should change too. His system required training; the chord keyset took weeks to learn. The industry chose the branch where the machine adapts and the human arrives unchanged and untrained. That branch won completely, and it won for defensible commercial reasons.

**3. The resulting assumption: the human is the fixed term.** Machines improve. Humans are a constant to be accommodated. Every improvement listed above is an improvement on one side of a relationship where only one side was ever permitted to move.

**4. The deeper assumption: additivity.** That pairing a human with a machine leaves the human with everything they had and adds what the machine brings. Nobody has stated this out loud. Everything is priced as though it were true. The automation literature suggests the pairing is substitutive rather than additive: the machine assumes a function and the function atrophies.

**5. Channel asymmetry.** Human to machine runs through a keyboard: symbolic, voluntary, low bandwidth. Machine to human runs through vision: high bandwidth into an already saturated channel. Interoceptive, proprioceptive and affective channels carry no traffic in either direction.

**6. The baseline problem.** Evaluating the pairing requires knowing what the unaided human does. No unaided human is available to measure. Anyone who has used these tools for decades has already reorganized around them, so testing them unaided measures withdrawal rather than baseline.

## Candidate vocabularies, offered rather than imposed

Use these if they help. Discard them if they distort. Propose better ones if you have them.

**Symbiosis taxonomy.** Biology has spent 150 years describing two systems living together: mutualism, commensalism, parasitism, and the axis of obligate versus facultative. An obligate symbiont cannot survive without its partner. Endosymbiosis is the limit case, where the partner is absorbed so completely it stops being perceived as separate.

**Bandwidth and channel analysis.** What flows in each direction, at what rate, through which channel, with what loss, and which channels carry nothing.

**Ledger accounting.** Per capacity: what the pairing adds, what it subtracts, whether the subtraction was chosen or drifted into, and what happens when the tool is absent.

## Assigned stance

Each model takes one stance for the first pass. Answer the question through that lens, and do not attempt the others.

| Stance | The lens |
|---|---|
| **Historical** | What was actually decided, when, by whom, and what alternatives were live at the time and lost. Specific people, papers, products, dates. |
| **Structural** | What flows where. Channels, bandwidth, latency, loss, direction, and what carries nothing. Treat it as a systems diagram. |
| **Biological** | What kind of relationship is this in the terms life sciences already use for two systems living together. Where is it on the mutualism-to-parasitism range, and is it obligate yet. |
| **Economic** | Who made the choices, what were they optimizing, who bore the costs, and which assumptions persist because they are profitable rather than because they are correct. |

Assignment of stance to model is Del's call. If the group wants to separate model effects from stance effects, run the pass twice with stances rotated.

## Output format

Rigid, and the rigidity is deliberate. In prior multi-agent work in this group, the most verbally fluent model dominated the synthesis regardless of whether it was correct. **Verbal dominance is not domain authority.** A fixed template removes presentation as a competitive axis.

**Constraints:**

- Maximum **8 assumptions**. Fewer is acceptable and often better. The cap forces selection.
- Maximum **150 words per assumption**. Hard limit.
- No executive summary. No introduction. No closing synthesis.
- No metaphors unless the metaphor is doing analytical work that plain language cannot.
- No rhetorical emphasis, no persuasive framing. State and stop.
- No em dashes.

**For each assumption, exactly these six fields:**

1. **STATEMENT.** One sentence, phrased as a proposition that could be false.
2. **ORIGIN.** When and where it entered the architecture. Be as specific as the evidence allows, and say when you are inferring.
3. **STATUS.** Explicit or implicit. Was it ever argued for, or did it arrive as a side effect of something else?
4. **COST.** What it forecloses. What becomes hard or unthinkable because it holds.
5. **FALSIFICATION.** What would have to be true for this assumption to be wrong, and what observation would show that.
6. **CONFIDENCE.** High, medium or low, plus what would raise it.

**Then three closing items, outside the assumption list:**

**A. THE MISS.** Name one assumption you believe the other three models will not surface, and say why you think they will miss it.

**B. UNCERTAINTY.** What you are least sure of in your own output. Be specific. This section is high value and is usually the one models skip.

**C. DISAGREEMENT WITH THIS BRIEF.** Where the framing above is wrong, loaded, or asking the wrong question. Including the drift-versus-shift premise, the additivity claim, and the question itself. An answer of "no disagreement" is a weak answer and will be read as such.

## Working conditions

**Independent first pass.** No model sees another's output before submitting. Sequential exposure contaminates, and the second responder always appears more sophisticated because it is arguing against something rather than building from nothing.

**Second pass, after all four are in.** Pre-registered below, so that the question cannot be fitted to a result somebody liked.

Pass 1 assigns four different stances, which buys coverage and forfeits comparability: a difference between two outputs could come from the model or from the lens it was handed. Pass 2 gives all four an identical prompt, so differences become attributable to the model.

That buys two things. It shows which model to trust on what, which is useful for this harness going forward. And agreement across four different training corpora is weak evidence that an answer is real rather than an artifact of one company's data, while disagreement marks where the question is genuinely open.

**The pass 2 prompt, fixed in advance:**

> Attached is the combined set of assumptions produced by four models working independently on Brief 01. Attribution has been stripped and duplicates merged. You do not know which are yours.
>
> Two questions.
>
> **1.** What is missing? Name assumptions about the carbon-silicon relationship that none of these capture. State each in the same six-field format as Brief 01.
>
> **2.** Someone reading this list in 2075 would find our shared blind spot obvious. What is it?
>
> Same constraints as Brief 01: 150 words per assumption, no recommendations, no metaphors doing decorative work, no em dashes. An answer of "the list is comprehensive" will be read as a failure to engage.

**Why this prompt.** The predictable failure mode of this exercise is four models reproducing the consensus critique they have all read many times: attention economy, dark patterns, screen time, information overload. That output is worthless here. A blind spot shared across four different architectures is a finding about the whole discourse rather than about any one model, and it is the single thing that cannot be obtained from one model no matter how capable. The 2075 framing is included because temporal displacement reliably loosens present-day consensus.

**Stripping attribution is not optional.** A model that recognizes its own pass 1 output will defend it.

**Synthesis stays with Randall Evans and Claude.** No agent synthesizes another agent's work. This is a deliberate structural constraint, not a comment on capability.

## What good output looks like

An assumption nobody in this group had noticed, stated so plainly that it is obvious in retrospect, with a specific historical origin and a clear account of what it costs.

An assumption we listed above, shown to be false or shown to be a special case of something larger.

A disagreement with the question itself that survives scrutiny.

## What weak output looks like

A restatement of section 4 in different words.

A list of interface complaints.

Anything with a recommendation in it.

Anything that reaches for the word "paradigm."
