## Read this part first

Three findings change decisions already made in this folder.

**1. The instruments question has an answer, and it is not about timing.** It is about whether the learner commits to a guess before the reading appears. Detail in section 3.

**2. The system already exists, in medicine, with 12-month outcome data.** Blood Glucose Awareness Training. Section 4.

## 1. The correction was right, and now it is empirically grounded

The decision to strip semantic authority from the silicon was made on philosophical grounds. It turns out to have a hard empirical basis.

**There are no autonomic fingerprints for discrete emotions.** Siegel et al. 2018, *Psychological Bulletin*, 202 studies, 739 effect sizes, six emotion categories. [META]

> "The pattern of effect sizes did not clearly distinguish 1 emotion category from another."

Autonomic changes during emotion are "less like a bodily fingerprint and more like a population of variable, context sensitive instances." Experimental moderators explained minimal variance, so this is not a methods artifact.

There is no physiological ground truth against which any machine could check whether a person's label is correct. A system that says "your wearable indicates you are actually feeling X" is making a claim the physiology cannot support. The correction in this folder was not merely good epistemics. It was the only defensible design.

Note the irony worth holding: this comes from Barrett's own research programme, the same one that produced emotional granularity. On constructionist theory, granularity is a skill of *constructing* useful categories, not of *detecting* pre-existing ones.

## 2. What actually produces gains

Answering the first half of open question 2.

### The dissociation that organizes everything

| Dimension | Trainable? | Best evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Sensibility** (self-reported interoception) | **Yes, reliably** | g = 0.31, 29 RCTs, N = 2,191, no publication bias [META] |
| **Accuracy** (objective detection) | **Acutely yes, durably no** | η² = 0.278 after one session, null at 3 weeks [RCT]; null in the N = 102 InMe trial [RCT]; null in expert meditators [CORR] |
| **Awareness** (metacognitive calibration) | **Unknown** | The construct does not converge across tasks (r = 0.09, ns) [META] |

Eight weeks of mindfulness moves MAIA-type self-report by about a third of a standard deviation. It does not durably move detection ability. Khalsa et al. 2020 found meditation is not associated with better heartbeat perception at all, which means thousands of practice hours do not produce it either.

**What these traditions demonstrably teach is a changed relationship to bodily sensation, not a measurably sharper sensor.** That is worth having. It should be what gets claimed.

### The measurement instrument this field runs on is broken

Heartbeat counting is the dominant objective measure. Desmedt et al. 2022, 133 studies, N = 11,524 [META]:

- Trait anxiety: r = 0.03, ns
- Depression: r = −0.04, ns
- Alexithymia: r = −0.01, ns
- Heart rate: r = −0.17, p < .001
- BMI: r = −0.11, p = .002
- Sex: r = −0.14, p < .001

Null on every psychological construct it was adopted to measure. Significant on exactly the physiological and demographic variables that determine how well a stereotyped guess of roughly 70 bpm happens to match reality.

Desmedt et al. 2018 [CORR] closed it: under standard instructions, reported heartbeats correlated with time estimation (r = .41) and with knowledge of one's own heart rate (r = .27). Under instructions saying "only count beats you really feel, without guessing," those correlations collapsed to .08 and .02.

The task is reliable (ICC = 0.88). It measures something stable. That something is a belief about heart rate.

There is a live rebuttal literature (Zimprich, Ainley) defending the task's clinical validity, and it complicates the scoring-artifact argument. It does not answer the instruction manipulation or the empty nomological network.

**Do not use heartbeat counting as an outcome measure.**

### Interoception is probably not one thing

N = 241, hierarchical Bayesian, cardiac and respiratory axes: **no significant cross-domain correlation** in sensitivity, precision, or metacognitive efficiency [CORR]. The one thing that did correlate across domains was confidence (r = 0.510).

What generalizes is how sure you feel, not how accurate you are. A global "interoceptive score" is not defensible.

### What the traditions agree on

Six traditions developed in near-total isolation from each other (Feldenkrais from judo and physics, MBSR from Theravada Buddhism, Hakomi from Reichian work, SE from ethology, IE from behaviorism, MABT from massage and mindfulness). Where they converge, the convergence carries weight.

**Nobody says "notice your body."** Every tradition supplies an amplifier or an anchor first. MABT applies manual pressure. Feldenkrais uses floor contact and movement. MBSR imposes a scripted serial route. Interoceptive exposure provokes the sensation chemically or mechanically. Hakomi uses "taking over" to raise signal-to-noise. The unadorned instruction to attend inward is treated as inadequate by all six.

**Start where the signal is strongest, not where the problem is.** MABT starts at the upper chest. Feldenkrais at contact points with the floor. IE with the lowest-anxiety exercise in the hierarchy. Nobody starts at the trauma or the pain.

**Vocabulary is explicitly taught.** MABT hands the client a word list when they cannot find words. IE hands them three rating dimensions. Language scaffolding is treated as prerequisite to noticing, not as its byproduct.

**Contrast beats absolute detection.** Feldenkrais does one side, then compares. MABT trains noticing of shifts rather than states. MBSR brackets each session with a before and after scan. A beginner who cannot answer "what do you feel" can usually answer "is this different from that."

**Sensation is separated from its interpretation, and both are trained.** IE rates sensation intensity, anxiety intensity, and panic-similarity on three separate scales, explicitly teaching the distinction. MABT's Stage 3 appraisal is the same move from the other direction. Perceive, then appraise.

**Every session ends with verbal review.** No tradition leaves the experience unprocessed.

**Dose converges on 8 weekly sessions over 8 to 12 weeks.** MABT 8, MBSR 8, IE about 12, SE 12 to 15, Feldenkrais 10 to 30. Nothing credible claims durable capability in under two weeks.

**Scaffolding and fading is documented where the tradition is manualized, and absent where it is not.** MBSR removes the guided recording at week 7. MABT moves therapist touch to self-touch to props to unaided practice. IE moves supervised to logged homework to deliberate solo practice. Somatic Experiencing and Hakomi document no fading path at all. That correlates almost perfectly with which traditions have trial evidence.

### Evidence quality by tradition

| Tradition | Best evidence | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Interoceptive exposure** | 72-RCT component network meta-analysis, N = 4,064, OR 7.69; 132-study safety review, zero serious adverse events | Strongest efficacy and safety base. Different goal. |
| **MBSR body scan** | 29-RCT meta-analysis, g = 0.31; N = 96 prospective harm study | Modest, reliable, non-specific. Best-documented dose and fading. Real harm rates. |
| **MABT** | 3 RCTs (N = 187, N = 303, N = 22 imaging) | Only tradition with both a manualized beginner sequence and trials. Trains the construct (d = 0.62); transfer modest (d ≈ 0.3); best-powered trial missed its primary endpoint on a ceiling effect. |
| **Feldenkrais** | 16-RCT meta-analysis; N = 53 interoception RCT | Good functional evidence. No evidence of specific interoceptive benefit over active control. Excellent pedagogy regardless. |
| **Somatic Experiencing** | 1 small waitlist RCT (n = 63); 1 mixed; 1 clean null at n = 114 | Practitioner-rich, evidence-poor. Pedagogy not documented to a reimplementable standard. |
| **Hakomi** | Dissertations, theses, a 3-case series | No RCTs exist. Valuable technique library, zero outcome evidence. |

**MABT is the model to study.** Three stages across 8 sessions: Awareness (body literacy, therapist applies pressure, supplies vocabulary when the client cannot find words), Accessing (breath flow, tissue softening, region-specific attention, starting at the upper chest), Appraisal (sustained attention on one region, coaching the noticing of shifts, closing verbal review). It is felt-sense literacy with the trial infrastructure Focusing never built.

## 3. Where the instrument goes

Answering the second half of open question 2. This section overturns the framing in `_START_HERE.md`.

### Frequency is the wrong variable

McKay et al. 2022, *Psychology of Sport and Exercise*, 61 papers, k = 75 effect sizes, N = 2,228 [META]:

| Timepoint | Hedges' g | 95% CI |
|---|---|---|
| Acquisition | .19 | [−.11, .49] |
| Immediate retention | .01 | [−.30, .32] |
| Delayed retention | .19 | [−.05, .43] |

The reversal the guidance hypothesis predicts, worse in acquisition and better in delayed retention, was tested directly and was absent (difference −.0008, CI [−.32, .32]). Faded schedules and bandwidth feedback failed as moderators. The authors: "The guidance hypothesis is not supported by the extant research," and scientists should "abstain from making substantive recommendations regarding feedback frequency to practitioners."

Self-controlled feedback fares no better. Naive estimate g = .44; published-only .54; **unpublished-only .003**; bias-corrected .054, ns [META].

### The variable is whether the learner commits first

Guadagnoli & Kohl 2001, 2×2 factorial, 64 participants [QUASI with delayed retention]. Error estimation required or not, crossed with feedback frequency 100% or 20%. Retention ordering:

1. **Best: 100% feedback WITH required error estimation**
2. Middle: 20% feedback, either way
3. **Worst: 100% feedback WITHOUT error estimation**

Read that twice. Frequent feedback was simultaneously the best and the worst condition. What flipped the sign was whether the learner had to commit to an internal judgment before seeing the answer.

This reconciles the whole literature. Frequency was never causal. It was a noisy proxy for whether learners were doing their own error estimation, which is why frequency effects meta-analyze to zero.

**The System Test's probe-first ordering is correct. The reason given for it in this folder was wrong.** The reason is not that instruments in the room invite intellectualizing. The reason is that a reading arriving before a committed guess removes the only event that produces learning.

### The competing theory gives the same advice for a better reason

Specificity of practice (Proteau et al. 1992) [QUASI]: with more practice under vision, movements became *more* dependent on vision, not less. Learning builds a representation specific to the sensory conditions of acquisition and does not migrate out of them with repetition.

Guidance says remove the display to force an internal reference. Specificity says practice in the condition you intend to perform in. Both converge: **if the goal state is unaided interoception, a substantial share of practice must occur unaided.** Specificity is the better-supported of the two.

### The complexity caveat

Sigrist et al. 2013 [META] documents a clean sign reversal. Concurrent feedback degrades retention on simple tasks and *benefits* learning on complex ones, via cognitive load reduction.

The felt sense is motorically trivial and perceptually very hard. The moderator was derived from motor complexity. Whether perceptual difficulty triggers the same load-reduction benefit is an open extrapolation.

The defensible reading: a display may be needed early to solve the discrimination problem, which sensation is the target. That job is short. It is a poor tool for the longer job of learning to scale the sensation.

### Fade on measured accuracy, never on a schedule

The expertise reversal effect: support that helps novices actively harms experts, because an expert cannot decline to process redundant guidance and must cross-reference it against their own schema, consuming working memory [TRAD reviewing multiple QUASI experiments].

Reisslein et al.: withdrawal pace must match expertise. High-knowledge learners did better with fast transitioning, low-knowledge learners with slow. Same fading, opposite optimal rate.

Kalyuga and Sweller's rapid diagnostic method classifies expertise 5 times faster than full testing. Adaptive fading beat fixed schedules on knowledge gains [QUASI].

**This system generates its own fading signal for free: the running error between the learner's committed estimate and the sensor reading.** Fade on that. Collect subjective effort ratings alongside accuracy, which is what distinguishes genuine schema development from performance that merely looks adequate.

### The finding that should most change the prior

Casner et al. 2014, 16 airline pilots, 747-400 simulator [QUASI]:

**Retained:** manual aircraft control and instrument scanning, "surprisingly resistant to forgetting, even after four months of inactivity."

**Collapsed:** the interpretive layer. Without the map display, 44% missed the missed-approach point, 38% flew incorrect headings, only 1 of 16 navigated error-free, and 94% approached stall conditions in an unreliable-airspeed scenario. Performance correlated with task-unrelated mind-wandering during automated flight.

The skills that survived automation were psychomotor. The skills that collapsed were interpretive: reading a state from raw indications, knowing what it means, deciding what follows.

**The skill this project trains is interpretive.** It sits in the category automation reliably degrades.

The mind-wandering correlation adds a second mechanism. A display does not only substitute for the skill, it withdraws attention from the signal, so nominal practice opportunities are not cognitively taken up. **Passive exposure with a display running does not count as practice.**

### Retention and deployment are separate problems

Van den Berghe et al. 2024, running gait retraining with faded music-based biofeedback, 6-month follow-up [QUASI]:

- **Spontaneous retention: none.** No significant change from baseline at follow-up.
- **With a single verbal cue** ("run as at the end of the program"): peak tibial acceleration −32% (p = 0.018), vertical impact loading rate −34% (p = 0.006).

The internal representation was intact and durable for 6 months. The learner simply never invoked it.

Fading the display addresses retention. Only cue and context design addresses deployment. Standard HRV biofeedback protocols contain neither a fading stage nor a transfer-to-daily-life stage (Lehrer et al. 2023 [META]), and should not be copied wholesale.

### The FAA's position, which cuts against a naive fading rule

SAFO 17007 recommends manual flight practice across all automation combinations and encourages hand flying "when conditions permit." It explicitly **discourages blanket prohibitions** and explicitly **prohibits arbitrary flight-hour quotas without scientific justification**.

Unaided practice should be frequent, deliberate, and conditioned on the situation being low-stakes enough to absorb failure. Not scheduled by quota.

## 4. The system already exists

**Blood Glucose Awareness Training.** People with type 1 diabetes are trained to estimate their own blood glucose from internal cues, then verify against a meter.

The training procedure (Cox et al. 2001, *Diabetes Care*, N = 73, three sites, 6 months pre-intervention monitoring, 8-week training, 12-month follow-up) [QUASI, repeated baseline]:

Using handheld computers, 50 trials over 3 weeks, participants **(1) estimated their own blood glucose from internal cues, (2) recorded the action they would take, and only then (3) measured actual glucose to receive feedback.**

Estimate, commit, verify. Terminal feedback on a self-generated hypothesis. It is Guadagnoli and Kohl's winning condition, implemented clinically, a decade before the lab result.

Outcomes at 12 months:

| Measure | Baseline | Follow-up |
|---|---|---|
| Low BG detection | 34% | 44% |
| Accurate estimates | 38% | 45 to 46% |
| Severe hypoglycemia | 1.6/month | 1.1 to 1.2/month (−31%) |
| Motor vehicle violations | 0.09/month | 0.03/month (−67%) |
| Diabetic ketoacidosis | 6 episodes | **0** |
| HbA1c | 10.2% | 10.2% (stable) |

Supported by an RCT in Switzerland and Germany (BGAT III, 2005) and an internet-delivered version.

**Three honest caveats.** The 2001 study is a repeated-baseline design, not randomized against an alternative feedback schedule. Detection gains are real but modest in absolute terms. And **no BGAT trial has ever tested estimate-then-verify against a real-time continuous display.** That comparison is the single largest gap in the evidence for this project's central decision.

**And the ending is the whole argument in one sentence.** Continuous glucose monitoring has since largely displaced the trained skill. A capacity that was teachable, durable, and measurably protective was replaced by a screen. That is the Google Maps problem, realized, in this project's own discipline.

## 5. What this folder got wrong

`_START_HERE.md`, section "Where The Instruments Sit," offered scaffold-then-fade as the possible resolution and cited the biofeedback tradition as the counter-case.

**The scaffold-and-fade framing rested on the guidance hypothesis, which failed meta-analysis in 2022.** Reduced feedback frequency shows no benefit at any timepoint, and faded schedules specifically failed as a moderator across 75 effect sizes.

The instinct that produced the hypothesis survives, by a different and better-supported route. But the file overstated a settled answer, and the flag on it was correct.

## 6. Nobody has instrumented Focusing. Gendlin specified how in 1978.

This answers open question 7, and the answer is more interesting than expected.

### The verified absence [ABSENT]

No study places physiological monitoring on a Focusing session or on Focusing-Oriented Psychotherapy to characterize the process. No study combines biofeedback with Focusing. No fMRI, no EEG, no HRV, no facial EMG, no voice analysis. Searched in English and Japanese, across the International Focusing Institute's own research pages, the Gendlin Center's roughly 100-dissertation database spanning 13 countries and 1971 to 2024, and the adjacent psychophysiology literatures.

Confirmed from the other side: Kleinbub 2017's systematic review of interpersonal physiology in psychotherapy (19 studies since the 1950s) **does not mention Gendlin, Focusing, or experiential therapy at all.**

The two literatures have essentially never intersected.

### Gendlin wrote the instrument specification

From *Focusing*, 1978, describing the felt shift:

> "You often can **see it and hear it when it happens in somebody else**. There may be a **long audible sigh of relief**, a **sudden loosening of some tight facial grimace**, a **quick, comfortable relaxing in the posture**."

Three externally observable modalities, named in 1978: respiration, facial muscle activity, postural tone. They map onto a respiratory belt, facial EMG or video-based action coding, and pressure-mat or IMU posture sensing.

He described the instrument and nobody built it.

### He also ran the study, in 1961

**Gendlin & Berlin (1961), *Journal of Clinical Psychology* 17(1), 73 to 77.** N = 17, zinc sulfate palmar electrodes, climate controlled. Three conditions: silences of continuous reference to directly felt experiencing, discontinuous or externally-directed silences, and speech.

**Result: a > b > c on both GSR indices, p < .01.** The Focusing mode of attention produces measurable, monotonic autonomic quieting. Gendlin concluded that GSR reflected **how** the person was processing, not **what** they were thinking about.

This 65-year-old study is the strongest existing precedent for this project, and it has apparently never been replicated with modern instrumentation.

**The companion study carries a warning.** Berlin & Gendlin 1961 (unpublished, N = 13) measured GSR, skin temperature and heart rate during interactions with a close versus a distant partner. Tension peaks were significantly **higher** in the *better* relational condition. Interpretation: good relationships involve more eventful physiological experiencing; poor ones involve withdrawal and damped experiencing.

**A system that naively rewards calm would reward disengagement and mistrain the thing it means to teach.** The target must be the transition, not a tonic arousal level.

### Candidate signals, ranked

**1. Respiration and sigh detection.** Gendlin's own primary descriptor is "exhaling after holding your breath" and "a long audible sigh of relief." Independently, Vlemincx et al. 2017 [QUASI, N = 29] found sigh frequency significantly higher during relief transitions than control transitions, with higher sigh frequency accompanying steeper cortisol declines. **Two literatures describing the same event, and neither cites the other.** Cheapest and least intrusive to instrument.

**2. Electrodermal.** Two precedents with opposite signs. Gendlin & Berlin 1961: rising skin resistance, meaning quieting, during felt-sense referencing. Shen et al. 2018 [QUASI]: increased skin conductance response at the aha moment. The sign is a genuinely open empirical question, and resolving it would be a contribution.

**3. Facial EMG and posture.** Named by Gendlin as observable, never measured.

Adjacent support: insight has a measurable neural precursor roughly 300 ms before the subject reports it (Jung-Beeman et al. 2004), and pupil dilation precedes conscious recognition by 658 to 1500 ms (Suzuki et al. 2018) [QUASI]. A subjectively sudden event can have a detectable signature.

### Two people to contact

**Rieko Kurino** (Kogakkan University, Japan). As far as this search can determine, the only active researcher on earth instrumenting Focusing. Her doctoral dissertation is the sole physiological entry in the entire TIFI dissertation database. Much of her output is Japanese-language conference proceedings that will not surface in an English literature search.

**Antonio Pascual-Leone** (Windsor). Owns the Experiencing Scale meta-analysis. TIFI's own diagnosis is that EXP lacks temporal resolution, because random session sampling misses infrequent focusing episodes and averaging hides them. Continuous physiological monitoring is precisely the fix. He would understand immediately.

## 7. What Focusing solved, and what it never did

### Solved. Do not rebuild.

**The teaching sequence**, with 45 years of field refinement and two distinct entry paths: Gendlin's six steps, and Cornell's body-first, greeting-first sequence for beginners who cannot find a felt sense at all.

**A catalogue of failure modes with matched interventions.** Roughly a quarter of Gendlin's founding text is troubleshooting. Two full chapters titled "If You Can't Find a Felt Sense" and "If You Can't Make Anything Shift." He expected most beginners to fail on first attempt.

**A peer dyad with explicit safety architecture.** Equal halves, hard clock, listener prohibited from advice and even from clarifying questions about content, no post-session reference. Gendlin's stated safety mechanism for putting depth work in non-professional hands is reciprocity plus a time boundary. Asserted, never tested [ABSENT on validation].

**A discriminating criterion for real versus fake progress.** "If you get a quick answer without a shift in the felt sense, just let that kind of answer go by." An answer without a body shift is not an answer.

**A low-cost credentialing ladder.** The Proficiency in Focusing Partnership award requires no training hours and no mandated session count. A certified professional attests to three competencies. $25.

### Never solved

**Only about half of naive subjects can follow the instructions at all.** Focusing ability correlated with 9 of 14 personality factors, and with anxiety at **r = −0.58** [CORR]. The population most likely to need this is the population least able to learn it.

**It does not develop spontaneously.** Across 38 cases, successful clients gained 0.592 stages on the seven-stage scale; failures −0.055. Only 5 of 38 low-starting clients eventually succeeded. Gendlin concluded focusing is a **prerequisite** for therapeutic benefit, not a product of it. That finding is why the method exists.

**Whether the teaching rescues the other half has never been tested against a control** [ABSENT].

**Scale.** Roughly 1,100 certified teachers and 2,600 dues-paying members worldwide, after 50 years, for a method whose delivery mechanism is free peer dyads. The tradition solved peer-transmission pedagogy and did not solve distribution.

**The recognition problem.** The entire method hinges on the learner recognizing an event they have never knowingly experienced, verified only by their own report. This is the exact failure point a physiological channel could address.

### And the honest note on evidence

There are no RCTs of Focusing. TIFI says so itself. The Experiencing Scale meta-analysis (Pascual-Leone & Yeryomenko 2017, 10 studies, N = 406, r = −.19 to −.25) is a process-outcome correlation across therapies of all orientations. It is not evidence that Focusing works. Anyone calling Focusing evidence-based is over-reading.

## 8. Threats to the granularity method

This folder chose granularity because it uses the person as their own control. That property holds. Three problems do not.

**The index may not measure what we assumed.** Nook et al. 2018, *Psychological Science*, N = 143, ages 5.8 to 25.9 [CORR]: differentiation follows a U-shape, bottoming at about 15.7 years. **Children score high.** Vocabulary accounts cannot explain that. The mediator was single-emotion experience: children tend to feel one emotion at a time, which mechanically inflates an ICC-based score.

The standard index may be measuring emotional co-occurrence structure rather than descriptive precision. Thompson et al. 2021, reviewing 82 studies, add that the index **penalizes people who report multiple simultaneous emotions**, scoring them as low differentiators, even though co-occurring specific emotions is arguably more granular.

**The vocabulary versus experience question is unresolved** [ABSENT on any clean dissociation]. The ICC is computed from ratings on researcher-supplied labels. It measures the covariance structure of responses to a fixed word list. It cannot distinguish feeling distinct things from using words distinctly. Kashdan, Barrett and McKnight's own recommendation is to assess differentiation *without* closed-ended word lists, via think-aloud or open-ended description. That is not standard practice.

**Nobody has validated granularity against physiology**, and section 1 gives the principled reason nobody can.

**What survives.** Granularity is trainable, with the best trainability evidence in this survey. Vedernikova et al. 2021 [RCT, N = 120]: an emotion-knowledge intervention raised negative differentiation, persisting at follow-up, though measured on a lab scenario task rather than in real life. Widdershoven et al. 2019 [RCT, N = 79 clinical]: experience sampling raised negative differentiation d = 0.43, with the serious confound that the intervention is repeated practice at the exact rating task the outcome is computed from.

Direct causal support for the underlying move comes from elsewhere: Kircanski, Lieberman & Craske 2012 [RCT], spider-phobic participants trained in **affect labeling** showed less anxiety and greater approach than reappraisal or distraction.

**Recommendation: keep granularity as a within-subject measure, treat the ICC as provisional, and consider open-ended handles scored for differentiation rather than a fixed word list.** Gendlin's insistence on idiosyncratic handles and Kashdan's methodological recommendation point the same direction, from different centuries.

## 9. Two design constraints that were not on the list

### Alexithymia is not one phenotype

*Frontiers in Psychiatry* 2020, N = 76 [CORR plus small RCT]: the relationship between alexithymia and interoceptive accuracy is **quadratic, not linear.** High alexithymia is associated with *either* relatively high *or* relatively low interoceptive accuracy. Some people who cannot label emotions are hypersensitive to bodily signals and catastrophize them. Others are genuinely hypo-perceptive.

In the same paper, a 10-minute body scan produced no advantage over control. The authors: "a 'one size fits all' approach seems inappropriate given the heterogeneous interoceptive profiles within high-alexithymia populations."

**Screening and branching is not optional.** Hypo-perceivers need amplification: MABT touch, Feldenkrais contrast, IE provocation. Hyper-perceivers need the opposite: appraisal work, discrimination between sensation and alarm, reduced attentional dwelling. The same 45-minute body scan will help one group and destabilize the other.

### The harm data are real

Britton et al. 2021, *Clinical Psychological Science*, N = 96, 92% completion, prospective active monitoring [QUASI]:

- **83%** reported at least one meditation-related side effect
- **58%** experienced effects with negative valence
- **37%** reported effects that negatively impacted daily functioning
- **6 to 14%** experienced lasting bad effects
- **6.6%** had effects lasting 1 to 5 months or ongoing
- Traumatic memory re-experiencing: **10 to 25%**
- Dissociative symptoms occurred in under 5% but carried **5 to 10 times greater risk** for lasting bad effects

Two findings that are design requirements rather than cautions. **Open-ended questioning underestimated the true rate by nearly 70%**, so active systematic monitoring is mandatory. And teachers must be trained to recognize dissociative symptoms as **indicators for intervention rather than material to be met with non-judgmental awareness.** A system that tells a user reporting derealization to "just notice it, without judgment" is doing the wrong thing.

Also: those with lasting effects practised informally far more often afterward (13.5 versus 4.3 times per week). More practice was not protective.

Interoceptive exposure, by contrast, has the best safety record here: 132 studies, no serious adverse events, dropout and severe complications at or under 1%, and clinicians significantly overestimate its risks. Its real contraindications are physical: seizure disorders, cardiac conditions, asthma.

## 10. Two traps this design will fall into unless built against

### Trap 1: apparent skill that is not perceptual

Murphy & Bird 2025 enumerate 7 mechanisms by which interoceptive accuracy scores improve **without any genuine perceptual learning**. The one this design specifically invites is **inference from learned base rates**.

A real-time display teaches the learner what the signal typically does under given conditions. The learner can then score well by reasoning: quiet room, 10 minutes seated, probably around X. No perception involved. That produces a display-derived inference model that passes every training test and **fails precisely in the novel situations where the skill matters.**

Countermeasures: vary conditions so base rates are uninformative, include trials where the state is manipulated unpredictably, measure the underlying physiological parameter directly, and use validated alternative tests.

### Trap 2: measurement is itself the intervention

An RCT of sampling frequency, N = 313, randomized to 3 versus 9 prompts per day for 7 days [RCT]: momentary emotional clarity rose 0.05 per day, 0.28 across the week, **in both groups, independent of frequency.**

Optimistic reading: repeated self-monitoring is a mild active ingredient. Being asked is part of the treatment.

Cautionary reading: an outcome that drifts upward simply from being measured is contaminated as an outcome. **If this system both trains and measures felt-sense literacy through the same prompts, improvement will appear whether or not the intervention works.** A sampled-but-untrained control arm is required to claim anything causal.

Note also that tripling the prompts bought nothing. Frequency is not the lever here either.

## 11. Practical parameters, where the evidence supports them

From the EMA meta-analysis, 477 articles, 496 samples, N = 677,536 [META]:

- Mean compliance **79.19%**; 56.8% of studies hit 80% or better
- Mean **6.53 prompts per day**
- Mean duration **12.4 days**, median 7
- Monetary incentives helped (82.21% versus 76.20%)
- **What did not predict compliance:** prompts per day, total assessments, study duration, age, gender, or health status

That last line is useful. Within the studied range, burden did not drive attrition the way designers assume.

Defensible starting parameters:

- **5 to 7 signal-contingent prompts per day.** Signal-contingent, not event-contingent, for anything feeding a granularity index: the ICC is a covariance statistic and is distorted by a non-representative sample of occasions. Event-contingent reports skew pleasant and higher-arousal.
- **At least 7 days**, 12 to 14 as the field norm.
- **80% compliance as a design constraint**, monitored.
- **Negative and positive differentiation scored separately**, never pooled, with a pre-specified rule for negative ICCs.
- **A sampled-but-untrained control arm**, for the reason in section 10.

One tension to resolve deliberately. Event-contingent sampling respects the world of no time by letting the body say when. Signal-contingent sampling is what the granularity statistic requires. These pull against each other. A parallel event-contingent stream, analyzed separately and never pooled into the index, is the compromise.

## 12. What this survey changes

**Confirmed, on better grounds than we had:**
- Silicon supplies no meaning. Now backed by 202 studies showing no autonomic fingerprints for emotion.
- Probe first, reveal second. Now backed by the error-estimation interaction and by BGAT's clinical durability.
- Person as their own control. Confirmed by the collapse of cross-domain interoceptive correlations.

**Overturned:**
- Scaffold-and-fade as a frequency prescription. The guidance hypothesis failed meta-analysis.
- Heartbeat counting as any kind of measure.

**Newly required:**
- Screening and branching for hypo- versus hyper-perceivers.
- Active systematic harm monitoring, with a dissociation protocol that overrides "just notice it."
- A sampled-but-untrained control arm.
- Explicit defense against base-rate inference.
- Cue and context training as a separate problem from retention.

**Newly available:**
- MABT as the manualized model to study and adapt.
- BGAT as the proven architecture, in medicine, with 12-month outcomes.
- The sigh as the first signal to chase.
- Gendlin & Berlin 1961 as a study to replicate with modern instruments.
- Two named collaborators.

**The decisive experiment has not been run, in any field.** Estimate-then-verify versus continuous display, same task, delayed unaided retention test. Not in motor learning, not in biofeedback, not in interoception. BGAT demonstrates the architecture works and lasts; it never tested it against a display.

That is a gap someone could fill.
