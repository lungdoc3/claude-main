# The Negotiated Codebook

*Working note for the carbon-silicon inquiry. 1 September 2026.*
*Follows from fence 2 in `information-theory-and-presentation.md`.*

---

## The claim

Every improvement to the carbon-silicon relationship since 1968 changed the machine's side of the mapping. Faster, prettier, more responsive, more channels of the same kind.

**Not one of them changed who holds authorship of the code itself.** The human has never been a co-author of the mapping between signal and meaning. They receive a table and learn it.

Negotiation moves authorship. That is a change in the structure of the relationship rather than an improvement within it, which is the drift-versus-shift distinction landing on something concrete and buildable.

---

## Why this is possible now, on these channels, and not before

Shannon requires sender and receiver to share a codebook. Carbon and silicon share one at the symbol level, which is why Level A works and characters arrive intact. That table was built for the machine's convenience and handed to the human as a condition of entry.

The idle channels have no table at all. Haptic, proprioceptive, interoceptive. Two reasons that matters, and the second is the structural one.

**Installed convention.** Visual carries 60 years of it. A red badge with a number means unread items to nearly everyone alive. Changing it breaks everything downstream, so the table is effectively frozen regardless of whether it is any good.

**No natural semantics.** Two taps on the wrist does not mean anything. It cannot be looked up. There is no dictionary and no possibility of one, because the signal has no referent to anchor against.

Which makes an imposed universal haptic codebook incoherent rather than merely undesirable. **Personalization is the only available option on that channel.** It arrives as a structural fact, not a design preference, and it is the first time in the history of this relationship that the imposed route was closed off by the physics rather than by an argument.

---

## The protocol

1. The machine proposes a pairing. Two taps for "something changed." Arbitrary, and known to be arbitrary.
2. The person uses it and reports back. **The question is not whether they memorized it. The question is whether it fit.**
3. If not, revise. Different rhythm, location, intensity envelope, duration.
4. Iterate until the pairing lands.

### What that loop already is

Propose a handle. Check it against the felt sense. Adjust until it fits. The body confirms.

**That is Gendlin's resonating step**, steps 3 and 4 of Focusing, run between a person and a machine rather than between a person and a word.

Which means the method already exists and has 48 years of field refinement behind it. It was never a technique for introspection specifically. It is a general procedure for establishing whether a symbol fits a wordless state, and the wordlessness is the whole reason it is needed.

See `gendlin-primer.md`.

## The machine can run this while knowing nothing

The acceptance criterion for a codebook entry is the felt shift.

Gendlin described the shift as observable from outside: a long audible sigh of relief, a sudden loosening of a tight facial grimace, a quick comfortable relaxing in the posture. Respiration, facial EMG, postural tone. All three are instrumentable.

So the machine can detect **that the pairing landed** while remaining blind to **what it means**.

Propose a symbol. Read the body's verdict. Keep or discard. **No semantics anywhere in the loop.**

That is Weaver's Level B approached without ever touching meaning, which is the move everyone assumed was unavailable.

It also gives the felt-shift detection work from `best-practice-survey.md` a job that is not research. Detecting the shift stops being an interesting open question and becomes the mechanism that makes codebook negotiation possible at all.

## What this satisfies

**Engelbart's abandoned branch.** He held that the human, the language, the artifacts, the methodology and the training co-evolve as one system, and that the human should change too. The industry chose ease of use, where the machine adapts and the human arrives untrained. A negotiated codebook has both sides moving, which is the branch that closed in the 1970s.

**The project's own no-semantic-authority rule.** The machine proposes symbols and reads physiological verdicts. It never decides what anything means.

**The data processing inequality.** The machine stays downstream and never claims to hold more information about the interior state than the person does.

---

## Four honest costs

**Negotiated codes do not transfer.** The vocabulary is one person's. No shared dashboards, no handing it to a colleague, no team display, no institutional standard. That forecloses certain products and opens others, and the choice has to be made deliberately rather than discovered.

**Negotiation costs time up front.** Imposed codes are instantly usable and permanently mediocre. This is exactly the trade Engelbart lost, and the commercial forces that beat him have not weakened.

**The channel is genuinely tiny.** A few bits per second at best. A beautifully negotiated codebook on a channel that still cannot carry much. Excellent for one-bit alarms, useless for anything rich.

**Meanings drift.** A pairing that fit in March may not fit in September, so it requires periodic re-negotiation. That makes it a maintained relationship rather than a configured setting.

The last cost is arguably the point. It is what relationships are, and it is the first item on this list that would make the carbon-silicon pairing resemble one.

---

## Open questions

1. **Does a negotiated pairing outperform a well-designed imposed one?** Untested, and the honest prior is uncertain. Imposed codes have the advantage of designer expertise and iteration across thousands of users.
2. **How many entries can a personal haptic codebook hold before collisions?** Small, probably. Under ten. Worth finding the ceiling before designing above it.
3. **Does the felt shift fire for an arbitrary symbol pairing**, or only for symbolizations of pre-existing interior states? Gendlin's shift responds to a handle that fits something already present. A codebook entry is a convention being established. Those may not be the same event, and if they are not, the acceptance criterion above does not work.

That third one is load-bearing. It should be tested before anything is built on this.

---

## Status

Nothing here is tested. One protocol, one mechanism that makes it possible, four costs, and three open questions of which the third could invalidate the whole approach.
