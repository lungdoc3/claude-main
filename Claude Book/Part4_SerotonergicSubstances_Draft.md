# Substances That Influence Serotonin
## Part 4: Companion section to the receptor pharmacology passage

---

Most people preparing for a ceremony know to ask about prescription medications. Almost nobody thinks to ask about what is sitting in the medicine cabinet or the supplement drawer. That gap is where the real risk lives, because several of the most common over-the-counter products and herbal supplements on the market are serotonergic, and none of them carry a warning label that would mean anything to a layperson.

**Direct reuptake inhibitors.** These work the same way an SSRI does, blocking the salesman from being pulled back off the street. St. John's Wort is the one most people have heard of. Its active compounds, hypericin and hyperforin, inhibit reuptake of serotonin, norepinephrine, and dopamine, the same mechanism class as a prescription antidepressant, sold without one. It also induces the liver enzymes CYP3A4 and CYP1A2, so it does not simply add serotonergic signal. It can change how the body metabolizes whatever else a person is taking at the same time. Case reports of serotonin syndrome from this interaction cluster most often around sertraline and paroxetine.

Dextromethorphan is the more dangerous one, and it is more dangerous precisely because it does not look the part. It is the active ingredient in nearly every cough syrup on the drugstore shelf, Robitussin DM, NyQuil, and their store-brand equivalents. It is a weak serotonin transporter inhibitor. Weak is not the same as harmless. Nobody screens a cough suppressant the way they screen a psychiatric medication, because nobody thinks of cough medicine as a psychiatric medication.

**Precursor loading.** 5-HTP and L-tryptophan work upstream of the receptor entirely. Rather than blocking the salesman's retrieval, they put more salesmen on the street to begin with. Both are sold openly as mood and sleep aids. Both feed directly into serotonin synthesis. The downstream effect lands in the same place a reuptake inhibitor lands, by a different route.

**MAO-inhibiting or MAO-adjacent.** Ginseng and nutmeg, at high enough doses, have been associated with serotonergic toxicity. The mechanism here is less clean than the categories above, and the evidence is thinner. Murky is not the same as absent.

**Multi-mechanism, lower-grade signal.** SAMe increases serotonin turnover and offers mild norepinephrine reuptake inhibition. Documented serotonin syndrome from SAMe alone is rare, essentially a single case report in the literature. Rare is not the same as zero. The standard precaution for any serotonergic combination still applies: start low, and watch closely for the first forty-eight hours.

Rhodiola rosea sits in the same family through a different door, leaning on 5-HT1A receptor activation and mild MAO inhibition rather than reuptake blockade. Lower signal, same neighborhood.

None of these substances are sold with a warning that says serotonergic. They are sold next to the vitamins, the cold medicine, the sleep aids. The label says mood support, or immune support, or cough relief. That is the actual danger. Someone walking into a ceremony involving an MAOI, ayahuasca or Syrian rue among them, needs to know what is already circulating in their system. A cough syrup taken three nights ago does not announce itself. The bloodstream does not care that the box looked harmless.
