**[INSERT AFTER "...not a feeling they are having." AND BEFORE "In each case..."]**

These examples all carry clinical names. But the mechanism does not wait for a diagnosis.

Body dysmorphia is in the DSM. What is not in the DSM is the vastly larger population of people who have never looked in a mirror without running an inventory of what is wrong. The story about the body was not chosen. Too much, too little, not right, not enough. It was written by a culture with strong opinions about what bodies should look like, reinforced by decades of small moments, and is now fed continuously by platforms engineered to make the story feel urgent. Social media is not a passive comparison experience. It is a machine designed to activate the adequacy story on a continuous loop, feeding it a stream of curated, aspirational content against which the self can measure itself and reliably come up short. No diagnosis required. No clinical threshold crossed. The story is still running the show. It is still mistaken, moment to moment, for a realistic assessment of how things are.

Eating disorders are the clinical name for the extreme end of something most people live somewhere on. The negotiation, the guilt, the identity organized around what was and was not consumed. None of that begins at the diagnostic threshold. The diagnostic threshold is where it becomes severe enough to name. Below it, in the vast middle ground that never gets labeled, the same story is running a quieter version of the same trap.

The diagnostic threshold is where impairment becomes visible enough to measure. It is not where the mechanism begins.

The target population for this work is not the formally diagnosed. It is anyone whose story has become a cage of any size. That is not a narrow category.
