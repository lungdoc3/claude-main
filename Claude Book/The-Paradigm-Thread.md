# A Different Model Entirely

*Working Draft | Healing with Psychedelics: A Physician's Perspective*

Step back from the two threads we have just covered and look at what they are describing together.

The mechanism of healing in this work is not solely molecular. The downstream events that produce the outcomes (the reconsolidation, the loosening of old patterns, the shifts in how a person relates to their own experience) are shaped profoundly by context, relationship, preparation, and integration. Strip those out and you have a genuinely different intervention.

Safety is not primarily a property of the molecule. It is a property of the container. The relational quality of the space, the adequacy of the preparation, the character of the guide, the depth of the integration: these are not soft add-ons to a pharmacological intervention. They are the intervention's load-bearing structure.

What these two things are describing, taken together, is a different model of what healing is and how it happens.

We established the theoretical frame for this earlier in this book: the Newtonian model and the territory it cannot map, the distinction between broken parts and faulty brain output, the argument that a different domain requires a different kind of understanding. What we have done in this chapter is show what that looks like at the level of mechanism and practice. The paradigm shift is not an abstract philosophical claim. It is a practical description of what this intervention actually is and what it actually requires.

This has consequences for everyone approaching this work, regardless of where they are coming from.

For the person considering their first experience: the preparation you do beforehand and the integration you commit to afterward are not supplementary to the medicine. They are part of it. The outcome you are looking for will not be delivered by the compound alone. It will emerge from the compound plus everything you bring to it and everything you do with it after. That is a different model entirely. One that asks something of you that a pill does not.

For the person with significant experience: what you have learned about yourself through this work: what preparation you need, what kind of container serves you, what integration looks like when it is actually happening. That is clinical information. Specific to you. More useful than any generalized protocol. The paradigm that honors this work honors that specificity.

For the facilitator: the relationship you hold with the people in your care is not the context for the medicine. It is part of the medicine. Your preparation, your ongoing accountability, your capacity for honest self-examination: professional requirements of the work itself, as essential as any technical skill.

For the physician or researcher approaching this territory from conventional medicine: the tools we have developed to evaluate pharmaceutical interventions will answer some of the relevant questions about these substances. They will not answer all of them, and the ones they cannot answer are not trivial. Knowing what the molecule does in isolation is genuinely useful. It is not the same as knowing what the intervention does when it is done well, and we should be careful about conflating the two.

None of this makes the pharmaceutical model wrong. It makes it incomplete for this particular territory — the same way Newtonian mechanics is not wrong, just incomplete when the domain it is applied to operates by different rules.

The map is not the territory. The framework is not the phenomenon. Where those two things diverge, it is always the phenomenon that has the final word.

What the evidence from this work is pointing toward, and what the ceremonial traditions that have worked with these medicines for centuries have always understood, is that healing at this level is participatory. The medicine initiates something. The person completes it. The relationship and the container and the integration make that completion possible. You cannot take any one of those elements out and call what remains the same treatment.

That is the paradigm, grounded in what is actually happening.
