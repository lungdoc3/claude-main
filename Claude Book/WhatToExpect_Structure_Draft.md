# What to Expect — And How This Book Is Built
## Book Introduction Section

---

*[Note: "What to expect / structure" section. Frames the four-part architecture as intentional sequence, not just chapters. Each part description answers what's in it and why it lives where it does. Randy's voice. First-pass draft.]*

---

This book is built around a single conviction: that understanding changes outcomes.

Not information. Not instructions. Understanding. The kind that stays with you when you are in the room, when the medicine is doing what medicine does, when no protocol or preparation checklist can reach you. The kind that was built before you arrived.

This book is structured to build that understanding in sequence. Each part earns the next. You could read them out of order. I would not recommend it.

---

**Part 1: The Framework**
*What psychedelics do — and why it matters*

Before the medicines, before the preparation, before any of the clinical specifics, there is a question worth sitting with: what does healing actually mean? Not managing. Not symptom reduction. Not the restoration of function as medicine typically defines it. Healing.

Part 1 builds the foundation. It covers what is happening in the brain when these medicines work, why certain people respond and others do not, and what the research — and the experience — is actually showing us. It introduces the framework this book uses to talk about healing: the four foundations that shape every person who sits in ceremony, and the five movements that characterize how real change happens. None of what follows makes full sense without this part. Read it first.

---

**Part 2: The Substances**
*What you're actually working with*

Psilocybin is not ayahuasca. Ayahuasca is not San Pedro. These are not interchangeable tools for interchangeable purposes. They have distinct pharmacological profiles, distinct durations, distinct interaction risks, and distinct ceremonial contexts that have shaped how they are used and what they ask of the people who work with them.

Part 2 is where the medicines get their individual chapters. Not as a buyer's guide. As a serious introduction to what each substance actually is, what it does in the body and the brain, and what a person needs to understand before they sit with it. By the end of this part, you will know what you are working with. That knowledge is the prerequisite for everything that comes next.

---

**Part 3: The Experience**
*Before and after*

The ceremony itself is not where most of the work happens. That is one of the things this field has learned, slowly and with some resistance, over the past two decades of serious research. What happens before you sit down — the preparation, the intention, the psychological readiness — shapes what becomes available in ceremony. What happens after you come home — the integration, the reentry, the period when the window is still open — is often where the change either takes root or dissipates.

Part 3 covers both ends of the experience. How to prepare in a way that actually prepares you. How to choose a facilitator and what that choice means. How to work with what the medicine surfaces rather than manage it from a distance. And how to integrate — not as a post-ceremony checklist, but as the continuation of the work that the ceremony opened.

---

**Part 4: The Medical Lens**
*Physician's toolkit*

This is the part of the book that does not exist anywhere else in quite this form.

Part 4 is where thirty-five years of clinical medicine and more than a decade of ceremonial experience converge. It covers the pharmacological interactions between plant medicines and prescription medications. The medical conditions that require serious clinical evaluation before anyone sits in ceremony. The contraindications that are real, the ones that are overstated, and how to tell the difference. The emergency protocols that every facilitator should have in place and almost none do. The preparation framework that closes the gap between western medicine and plant medicine — the gap that has already cost people who did not have this information.

If you are a participant preparing for serious work, this part is your clinical education. If you are a facilitator or practitioner, this part is your reference. Either way, it is the section I wish had existed when I started this work.

---

You do not need to agree with everything in this book. You do not need to arrive with certainty about what you believe or what you are looking for. What this book asks of you is honest engagement. Bring what is actually true for you. Let the framework do what frameworks are supposed to do.

The rest is yours.
