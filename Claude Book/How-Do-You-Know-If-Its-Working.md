# How Do You Know If It's Working

*Working Draft | Healing with Psychedelics: A Physician's Perspective*

After ceremony, the first question is usually some version of: what do I do now? That one has plenty of answers.

The harder question comes later, quieter, in the middle of an ordinary Tuesday. Is anything actually changing?

From inside the process it can look a lot like watching grass grow. Don't kid yourself. It can look that way from the outside too.

So how do you know?

## You Will Know

You will know. Only you will know. And you are the only one who needs to know.

We have been systematically and culturally conditioned to not look inward for answers. And on the rare occasions we do, we have been conditioned not to trust what we find there. This is not an accident. Disempowered people are easier to manage. It has been so thorough and so consistent that most of us don't experience it as conditioning. We experience it as common sense.

The result: you want to look outside yourself for an expert in what is happening inside yourself. You want someone with credentials to tell you if you are doing you correctly. Read that again slowly.

That is not a reasonable request. It is a trained helplessness.

The capacity to assess your inner landscape is not a rare gift. It is a skill. Every person reading this has it. It has been suppressed, not destroyed. And the thing that makes integration both demanding and elegant is that learning to assess your own experience is not just necessary for the work. It is the work. The practice builds the instrument. You cannot develop it from the outside looking in, and nobody else can do it for you.

This is what increased agency actually looks like. Not freedom from difficulty. The ability to trust your own experience as data, and to act from that trust.

Psychedelics don't fix you. They return the controls. What you do with them is the integration.

## The Movie

When you are not present, you are fully in the movie. The story is everything. You are the character, the plot is happening to you, and the possibility that any of it could be otherwise doesn't exist because there is no vantage point from which to see it.

When you are present, you have stepped outside the story far enough to recognize that it is a story. That the role you are about to play was written a long time ago, under circumstances that no longer exist, and that playing it right now is a choice.

That recognition is the finish line. Not enlightenment. Not the permanent dissolution of anything. The reliable capacity to step outside the movie long enough to remember you have a choice, and then to make one.

## When Is It Working

You will know it is working when you notice yourself noticing. Not in the post-game analysis, reconstructing what happened an hour later. In real time. The half-second gap between stimulus and response where something new has opened. The old pattern caught mid-motion, before it has fully taken over.

That gap is small at first. It grows.

Here is the other signal, and it is underappreciated: a significant portion of the change integration produces happens below your own awareness. Below even your paying-attention awareness. The nervous system reorganizes in ways that don't always announce themselves consciously.

Guess who notices.

The people who know you well are running detailed models of who you are, built from years of proximity. They know how you walk into a room, how you respond when you feel cornered, what your face does right before you say the thing you always say. When you change, the model no longer fits. They feel it before you can see it.

This is not an invitation to ask them what they notice and use the answer to narrate your own transformation. That is the ego finding a new source of confirmation. What it is an invitation to do is pay attention to how they react to you. The small recalibrations. The moment their script doesn't quite land. The conversation that goes differently than it always has. When their model stops fitting, you will feel it in the interaction. That is your data.

Use every resource available. Your own inner observation. The reactions of the people around you. All of it is signal. The work is learning to read it honestly, without letting the ego edit the results.

## When Is It Finished

You will know when you are done when you know. If you don't know, you're not done.

The same internal authority that tells you the work is working will tell you when this chapter has closed. Not that there is nothing left. That the specific thing you were integrating has found its place, has become part of the landscape rather than a disturbance in it.

There is no bell. No timeline I can give you that applies to your nervous system, your history, your particular experience. The shift from working-through to integrated is gradual, and you will likely realize in retrospect that it happened before you noticed.

## One More Thing

You will find, or create, or be handed some version of what success is supposed to look like. And you will use it to measure your progress. And you will come up short.

That is not integration failing. That is the old narrative doing what it always does. Compare with the unobtainable. Find fault. Repeat. It has been running your whole life and it is exceptionally good at disguising itself as honest self-assessment.

The work is not to hit the benchmark. The work is to notice when you are running that loop. To step outside it long enough to see it operating.

You are present. You saw it.

That is the work working.
