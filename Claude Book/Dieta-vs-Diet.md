# Dieta vs. Diet

*Working Draft | Healing with Psychedelics: A Physician's Perspective*

Your ceremony begins long before you drink.

This is the part that most people underestimate, and the part that explains more about the quality of their experience than almost anything else. What you do in the weeks before you sit with Grandmother Ayahuasca is not preamble. It is preparation in the same sense that a surgeon's hands are preparation: it determines what is possible before the work begins.

There are two related concepts here that often get conflated, and the conflation costs people something. The first is the traditional Dieta. The second is diet in the broader sense I want to use it. They are not the same thing, and understanding the difference matters.

## The Traditional Dieta

The Dieta, with a capital D, is an ancient practice. It is not primarily a food protocol. It is a technology for attunement: a structured period of isolation and simplification designed to quiet the noise of ordinary human life so that something subtler can be heard.

In traditional practice, a person undergoing a Dieta retreats from ordinary life for a defined period, which can range from days to months to years. The food is simple and ritually significant: plantain, manioc, certain fish and jungle animals, foods that sustain without overstimulating. What is avoided is equally specific: sugar, salt, chilies, pork, fermented foods, alcohol, acidic fruits, excessive sun, rain, fire, unpleasant smells. Sexual activity, conversations about the concerns of the outside world, and proximity to illness are all set aside. The social world is reduced to almost nothing.

The purpose is not deprivation. The purpose is subtraction. By removing the layers of cultural and sensory static that ordinarily occupy every available channel of attention, the Dieta opens the practitioner to guidance and intelligence from the natural world. The plants can only speak as loudly as the noise around them allows. The Dieta turns the noise down.

This is what the maestros practice before cooking medicine. It is what they practice before ceremony. It is what they have practiced for generations. There is a reason.

## Diet as Everything You Consume

Now the second concept, which operates on the same logic but at a more practical scale.

In ordinary usage, diet means what you eat. I want to use it more precisely. Diet is everything you take in. The food and the drinks, yes, but also what you read, what you watch, what you listen to, who you spend your time with, and what you allow those people to bring into your field. Every one of these things is an input. Every input has a quality. And the quality of your inputs shapes the state you arrive in.

We consume an extraordinary range of chemicals in the course of ordinary modern life, far more than any previous generation of humans. Some of these interact with the medicine in ways that range from uncomfortable to dangerous. The biochemical reality here is straightforward: ayahuasca contains MAO inhibitors, compounds that block the enzyme responsible for breaking down certain substances in the body. Under ordinary circumstances that enzyme is quietly neutralizing a long list of things you encounter daily. When it is inhibited, those things accumulate. The results can include severe blood pressure changes, serotonin excess, and in serious cases, medical emergencies.

This is not fearmongering. It is pharmacology. And it is why attending to the physical dimension of diet in the weeks before ceremony is not optional if you want to work safely.

The recommendations you will find if you go looking vary enormously, from the ultra-conservative to the dismissive. Sorting through them is its own task, and I provide a detailed breakdown elsewhere. What I will say here is that erring toward caution is not timidity. It is respect. For the medicine and for yourself.

## Grandmother's House

Here is a way to think about preparation that I find more useful than a list of restrictions.

You have been invited to have tea with Grandmother Ayahuasca in her home.

If you have attended to your preparation, if you have simplified your diet, reduced your chemical load, quieted the noise, shown up with care and intention: you will be welcomed in through the front door. You will be seated in the formal dining room. Your tea will be served in the finest china. The conversation will be unhurried, substantive, and precisely as long as it needs to be. You will leave knowing something you did not know before.

If you have not prepared: you will still be invited in. Grandmother does not turn people away. But you will come in through the back. You will be seated at what is clearly the kids' table, in the mudroom, and your tea will arrive in a chipped mug that looks like it last saw soap some time ago. The conversation will be short and difficult to follow. The tea will not sit well. Before you know it, you are being ushered out, and you are not entirely sure what just happened.

In both cases, you had tea with Grandmother Ayahuasca. Beyond that the experiences have almost nothing in common.

The invitation to sit in ceremony is not common. Most people never receive it. Of those who do, many are never in a position to accept. If you find yourself holding that invitation, the preparation you bring to it is the first and clearest signal of what you actually intend. After all, if you are not willing to commit to the preparation, what makes you think you will be willing to commit to what she shows you?

## After the Ceremony

The same logic that governs preparation governs what comes after.

In the days and weeks following ceremony, the walls are down. Not metaphorically: the neural and psychological structures you have spent a lifetime constructing to buffer the impact of the world around you have been significantly loosened. This is, in fact, part of what the medicine does. It is a feature, not a side effect. But it means that what ordinarily registers as minor irritation can land with the force of something much larger. What normally rolls off you will not roll off you for a while.

This is not a problem. It is information. It is the nervous system, newly open, doing exactly what it should. The same attention you brought to your diet before the ceremony belongs in the days after it. The same care with inputs. The same reduction of noise. The same selectivity about who and what you allow into your field.

Ceremony is not an event you attend and then leave. It is a threshold you cross. The crossing matters. So does what you do once you are on the other side.
