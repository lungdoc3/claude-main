# Getting Marge Home
## Part 4 — Closing Synthesis

---

This section was never meant to be a prohibition framework. Nothing in these pages exists to tell Marge from Ohio that the door is closed. The goal has been the opposite: to open the door more safely, for more people, by giving everyone in the room the information they need to do this well.

What that information adds up to is a preparation framework. Not a checklist, not a contraindication wall, not a clinical gatekeeping exercise. A framework built around a simple premise: the adverse outcomes that happen at the intersection of western pharmacology and plant medicine are, in the overwhelming majority of cases, preventable. The pharmacological mechanisms are understood. The dangerous combinations are known. The warning signs have been documented. The question has never been whether the information exists. It does. The question has always been whether the right people have access to it at the right time.

Three things need to be true for someone to sit safely in this work. The participant needs to know their own pharmacological picture, honestly and completely. Not just the prescription bottles on the nightstand but the supplement stack, the sleep aids, the herbal teas, the things they take so routinely they forget to mention them. The facilitator needs enough clinical fluency to know which of those things warrant a pause and which ones require a physician's eyes. And a physician needs to be in the loop, one who knows this territory, who can evaluate a medication list against the specific medicine being used, and who will not simply refuse engagement because the request falls outside the standard of care they were trained in. When all three of those things are true at the same time, the safety margin increases dramatically. When any one of them is missing, the gap returns.

The field is changing. This information is increasingly findable. Physicians who understand psychedelic pharmacology are not unicorns anymore. Research programs, clinical training pathways, integrative medicine practitioners, harm reduction organizations, and books like this one are moving this information out of the underground and into circulation. Marge can find a prescriber who will talk with her about this. She can find facilitators who screen seriously and refer when they should. She can find the support that closes the loop her healthcare system left open. It takes more effort than it should, and that is a gap the field still has to close. But the gap is smaller than it was.

Marge from Ohio packed her bags because something in her knew she needed this. That knowing deserves to be honored. It deserves the full weight of what we understand about how these medicines work, how they interact with the chemistry she is already carrying, and what she needs to do before she sits down. It deserves preparation rather than improvisation, information rather than hope alone. She came this far. Getting her there safely is the whole point of everything that preceded this page. I wrote it for her. I hope she reads it first.
