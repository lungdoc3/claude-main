# Wider Than You Think
## Part 1 — Target Population Standalone Piece

---

*[Note: Standalone piece positioned between the four foundations and the five stages. ---

Let me be clear about something before we go further.

Psychedelics are not the magic pill that fixes everything. They do not dissolve a lifetime of conditioning in an afternoon, and they do not work for everyone. We will get to that second point shortly.

But the assumption most people carry about who benefits from this work — that it is for people with serious, formally diagnosed conditions — is wrong. And it leaves an enormous number of people out of a conversation that belongs to them.

The mechanism we described in Foundation 4 does not stop at the diagnostic threshold. The story-becomes-identity process, the narrator running unchallenged, the grooves worn so deep they feel like the floor — this is not a feature of serious illness. It is a feature of being human in a culture that is extraordinarily good at writing stories into people and extraordinarily bad at helping them question those stories.

Consider who is actually sitting in those grooves.

The person who has never had a neutral relationship with food. Who has organized more mental energy around what they did and did not eat than around almost anything else in their life. Who does not have a clinical diagnosis, has never seen a therapist for it, and would not describe themselves as having an eating disorder. The story about the body is still running. It is still the bus driver.

The person whose relationship with their physical appearance was quietly shaped by years of comparison, comment, and a culture that has very specific ideas about what a body should look like. Body dysmorphia is in the DSM. What is in nearly everyone is some version of the story that the body is a problem to be managed rather than a home to be lived in.

The person who opens their phone first thing in the morning and spends the next twenty minutes measuring their life against a curated stream of other people's highlights. Social media industrialized the adequacy story. It gave the comparison engine an infinite feed, a dopamine loop, and an algorithm optimized to keep that engine running as hot as possible. No diagnosis. No clinical presentation. Just a story, running constantly, that this is not enough and neither are you.

As a physician, I can tell you: the target population for psychedelic healing is not the formally diagnosed. It is anyone who recognizes the story. Anyone whose narrator has been running without their full consent. Anyone who has felt the gap between who they are and who they might be, and has run out of conventional tools to close it.

That is not a narrow group.

We all carry tendencies. We all have grooves worn into the story by experience we did not choose. The question is not whether the mechanism applies to you. It is how deeply it is running, and whether you are ready to look at it.

---

That question leads directly to the next one.

Not everyone should do this work. The same mechanism that makes psychedelics powerful — the loosening of the story, the opening of the filter, the suppression of the narrator — makes them genuinely dangerous for people whose relationship with reality is already fragile. There are conditions that represent real contraindications, and there are circumstances where the timing is wrong, and there are people for whom this particular door is not the right one.

We will address the clinical detail in Part 4. For now, the short version: this work asks something of the person doing it. It asks for psychological stability that can withstand the ground shifting. It asks for a container strong enough to hold what surfaces. It asks for the capacity to return.

The door is wide. It is not open to everyone.
