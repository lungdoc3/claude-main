# The Update

*Working Draft | Healing with Psychedelics: A Physician's Perspective*

Every electronic device I own is obsessed with its own update. My phone. My laptop. My television. The thermostat. They notify me, remind me, plead with me, offer to handle it automatically while I sleep so I never have to think about it. The entire architecture of modern technology is built around the assumption that software becomes outdated and that outdated software causes problems, and that therefore the highest priority of any responsible system is to stay current.

Guess which device has never once offered me an update.

The one that needs it most.

The brain is running code that was written for a world that no longer exists, shaped by experiences that are decades old, filtered through a predictive model that was largely completed before you were old enough to have any say in its construction. The anxiety, the depression, the loops, the patterns, the persistent sense that something is wrong that you cannot quite locate or name: these are not character flaws. They are output problems. The operating system is outdated and nobody told you there was a newer version available.

Psychedelics are the update notification the brain forgot to send.

They do not rewrite the system by force. They create, temporarily, the conditions under which the system becomes capable of rewriting itself: the same window of plasticity that allowed the original code to be written in the first place, briefly reopened. New information reaches levels of the architecture that have been inaccessible since childhood. Patterns that have been running below the reach of insight or intention become, for a window of time, available for revision.

I have read the terms and conditions on every software update I have ever installed. I have not read a single one. I click agree and move on because the update is worth whatever they are asking.

I will agree to any terms and conditions to get the auto-update option for my brain.
