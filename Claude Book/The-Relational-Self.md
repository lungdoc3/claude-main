# The Mirror Is Other People

*Working Draft | Healing with Psychedelics: A Physician's Perspective*

Before we go further into the phases, we need to establish something that will make everything that follows make sense.

The self does not exist in isolation. Not poetically. Literally.

We know who we are through contrast and comparison, through how others respond to us, through the roles we play and the roles we refuse. Identity is not something you carry around inside you like a stone in your pocket. It is something that happens between you and the world, continuously, in every interaction you have. It is constructed and reconstructed in real time, through relationship.

Even when you are alone, you are in relationship. You rehearse the conversation you should have had. You imagine how someone will react to what you are about to do. You define yourself against some internal version of another person, argue with them, justify yourself to them, seek their approval or brace for their criticism. The argument you have with your partner while standing in the shower, three days after the actual argument, is not a neurotic quirk. It is the architecture of identity doing what it always does: working out who you are in relation to someone else.

This is why the people in your life are not the context for your integration. They are the substance of it.

The changes that happened in ceremony happened in a particular kind of space. Stripped of ordinary social context. Removed from the roles and relationships that ordinarily define you. In that space, something shifted. A new configuration became available. It is real. But it is not yet real in the world, because the world is relational, and the self is relational, and a change that has not been tested in relationship has not yet fully landed.

Integration is what happens when the new version of you walks back into the rooms that were built for the old version. The Tuesday afternoon meeting. The dinner table. The text from the friend who knew you before. The moment your partner says the thing they always say and you feel, for the first time, that you do not have to respond the way you always have.

That half-second is not a small thing. That is the work becoming real.

And here is the consequence that flows directly from this: your healing does not stay private. It cannot. The self that changes in ceremony changes in relationship to every self it encounters, whether you say a word about it or not. The people in your orbit will feel the delta between the version of you they have been carrying and the version that is now showing up. Some will feel it as relief. Some will feel it as a gift. Some will feel it as a threat and reach instinctively for the old version of you to reestablish what felt like solid ground.

None of these responses are about you, exactly. They are about the system adjusting to a change in one of its parts.

My healing is our healing. When the work is genuine and the integration is sustained, the people closest to you benefit. Not because you tell them about it. Because you show up differently, and differently is better, and they feel it. Children notice before anyone. Partners feel it in the quality of your attention. Friends feel it in the depth of what becomes possible in conversation.

But the adjustment is not always smooth. The growing pains are real. And the way you navigate them, across the different relationships in your life and in roughly the order you re-engage with them, is what the next section is about.

Your relationships are your classroom. They are also your report card.

This is your life. All of it. Right here.
