# The Ego in the Wild

*Working Draft | Healing with Psychedelics: A Physician's Perspective*

The enlightened ego does not behave the same way in every room. It reads its audience. It always has — that is what the ego does, it adapts to what the situation requires — and the version of itself that it presents will depend almost entirely on who is in front of it and what they already know.

This matters, because the risk it presents is not uniform. It depends on the audience.

## The People Who Knew You Before

Your significant other has been watching you your entire relationship. They have a detailed, continuously updated, load-bearing model of who you are. They know what you look like when you are genuinely moved versus when you are performing being moved. They know the specific quality of your attention when you are actually listening versus when you are waiting for your turn. They know, in their body before they can say it in words, when something is off.

When the enlightened ego comes home, the significant other feels it before they can name it. The warmth is there but it has a quality to it. The love is present but it is being offered from a slight elevation. Conversations that used to be exchanges have become transmissions. They are being talked at from somewhere they cannot quite reach, by a person who is using all the right words and producing, underneath all of it, a low-grade loneliness.

When they try to name this, the enlightened ego has an answer ready. You don't understand yet. You are not where I am. Your resistance is your fear. Your skepticism is your conditioning. Whatever the formulation, the message is the same: the problem is your limitation, not my performance.

Friends feel a version of the same thing, with less proximity and therefore less precision. The friendship starts to feel asymmetrical. The conversations flow in one direction. The friend becomes an audience rather than a peer, a witness to the transformation rather than a participant in a relationship. Some friends will name it. Most will simply start to drift, unable to articulate why but certain that something has changed, and not in the way they were hoping for.

Family is complicated by history. Some family members will be genuinely relieved if the change appears positive — calmer, more open, less driven by the old compulsions. They may not see the inflation underneath the presentation, because what they were living with before was its own kind of difficult. Others will notice immediately that the person has simply found a new way to be the same person, with a different vocabulary and a different set of justifications for the same fundamental orientation toward the world. These are the ones who are most likely to say something. They are also the ones most likely to be dismissed as not understanding, not being evolved enough, not being ready for this conversation.

Coworkers see a narrower slice. They may not register the change at all, or register only that the person has become slightly harder to read, slightly more distant, occasionally prone to saying things that don't quite fit the register of the conversation. The enlightened ego in a professional context tends to leak rather than announce itself. A reference to a recent experience that wasn't invited. A certainty about how things should work that has no clear basis in the evidence. A subtle condescension toward the ordinary business of the day.

## The Little Boy

In the story, it was a child who said it. Not because children are wiser than adults, but because the child had no stake in the pretense. No relationship to protect. No fear of the emperor's response. No history of investment in the belief that the emperor's taste in clothing was worth deferring to.

The people who knew you before your ceremony are in a similar position. They have a reference point. They remember the old version, in detail, and they can feel the gap between that version and what is currently being presented to them. They are better equipped than anyone to see through the act.

What they often lack is the courage or the framework to name it. To tell someone who has just returned from a profound experience, glowing with what appears to be genuine love and openness, that something about the performance is not landing — that takes a particular kind of honesty and a willingness to absorb the response. Because the response, from the enlightened ego, will not be gratitude. It will be some version of you are not yet able to see what I have seen.

The people who love you enough to say it anyway are doing you the greatest possible service. Let them.

## Strangers

Here is where the real vulnerability lives.

Strangers have no alternative version for comparison. They encounter the post-ceremony persona as the only version that exists. There is no model running in the background, no history to check the performance against, no felt sense of the gap between then and now. What is presented is accepted, because there is nothing else to accept.

The enlightened ego knows this, not consciously but functionally. It gravitates toward new audiences. New communities. Retreat circles, spiritual networks, online spaces populated by people who are themselves searching, who are open, who are primed to receive wisdom from someone who appears to have found it. These audiences do not push back. They reflect. They validate. They ask for more. And the enlightened ego, in this environment, flourishes.

This is how spiritual communities sometimes become closed systems. The people who knew the original version of the person are no longer in the room. The people who are in the room have accepted the presented identity as real and have built their own sense of belonging around it. To question the teacher becomes, in this context, a threat not just to the teacher but to the entire community that has organized itself around them.

The newcomer, the seeker, the person who just had their first ceremony and is looking for someone who has been further along the path — this person is the most vulnerable of all. They have no reference. They cannot see the gap. They experience the certainty of the enlightened ego as authority and the warmth of the performance as genuine connection. They are, in the truest sense, under a spell.

Not because they are naive. Because they have nothing to compare it to.

## What This Means

The antidote to the enlightened ego is the people who knew you before. Not because those relationships are automatically healthy or because the past is always the correct reference point. But because they hold a version of you that the ego cannot fully overwrite, and because their discomfort — when they feel it — is information worth taking seriously.

If the people who know you best are pulling back, something worth examining is happening. If the people who are most enthusiastic about your transformation are exclusively people who met you after the ceremony, that asymmetry is worth noticing.

The work of integration is not about finding an audience that accepts the new version. It is about being the new version in rooms where the old version is remembered. That is where the real test lives. And that is where genuine change either proves itself or doesn't.

The people who tell you the truth are the ones worth keeping close.
