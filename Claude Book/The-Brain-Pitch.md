# The Pitch

*Working Draft | Healing with Psychedelics: A Physician's Perspective*

Imagine you are a venture capitalist. You have seen a lot of pitches. You have funded some extraordinary things and passed on some things you should not have passed on, and you have developed, over the years, a reliable instinct for the difference between genuine innovation and an impressive slide deck.

Today's pitch is different.

The founders have been working on something they call The Brain: a vertically integrated signals processing and control unit with, in their words, an interface so intuitive the user never has to read the manual. They have been at this for a while. Their development timeline is, to put it diplomatically, non-standard.

---

**The Reptilian.** Their first release. Lean, fast, extraordinarily efficient. Core functions only: threat detection, hunger, temperature regulation, the basics of survival. No unnecessary features. The UI is minimal but the response times are remarkable. Still in production. Still running in every unit they have ever shipped.

**The Mammalian.** A generational leap. They kept everything from v1 and added a full emotional processing suite, social bonding architecture, memory consolidation, the early scaffolding of what will become learning. Substantially more complex. Substantially more powerful. The tradeoff, they acknowledge, is that the emotional processing layer occasionally overrides the core functions in ways that are not always optimal. They are working on it.

**The Human.** This is what they are here to talk about. Their most advanced release. Built on the full Reptilian and Mammalian stack, which means every legacy feature is still running underneath. New in this version: language. Abstract reasoning. Imagination. The capacity to model futures that do not yet exist and pasts that no longer do. Humor. Art. The ability to ask why.

The flagship feature of The Human is a premium add-on unit called the PFC: the Prefrontal Cortex. Executive function. Impulse regulation. Long-term consequence modeling. The capacity to override the older systems when their instincts are no longer appropriate to the situation.

There is, they mention, one small note about the PFC.

It does not activate at install. It comes online gradually over the first two decades of operation, reaching full functionality somewhere in the mid-twenties. During this window, the unit ships and operates on the older stack. The founders consider this a feature. It allows for environmental calibration during the formative period. The investors write something down.

The product demo is extraordinary. The Human does things no prior version could do. The addressable market is, literally, everyone. The founders are visibly proud and not without reason.

Then someone in the back of the room asks the question.

*What does ongoing maintenance look like? What is the update pathway?*

The founders glance at each other.

There is no auto-update.

The room is quiet.

They explain: the architecture is set largely during the early operational period. The formative years. Whatever the unit encountered during that window, the system incorporated. Efficiently. Permanently. The code that was written then is still running. Users cannot access it directly. It operates below the level of the conscious interface. It is not that updates are impossible. It is that the standard user has no mechanism for installing them independently.

*So how do users update the system?*

Third-party support, the founders say. The unit can be updated, but it requires external intervention. Talk therapy can reach the software layer. Significant life experience can sometimes prompt a patch. There are contemplative practices that, over years of consistent application, can make incremental changes.

And then there is one category of third-party tool that operates differently from all the others. Tools that temporarily reopen something close to the original installation conditions. The developmental window, briefly restored. During which the deeper code, the stuff written into the architecture during those early formative years before the user had any say in what was being installed, becomes accessible for revision.

These tools are not new. They have been in use, in various forms, for as long as The Human has been in production. For most of that time, in the most recent era of development, they have been formally unavailable, misclassified, their access restricted.

The investors are writing faster now.

One of them looks up.

*So you are telling me there is an existing, field-tested, third-party update tool that reaches the OS level, has a safety profile developed over thousands of years of human trials, is currently underutilized, and for which there is an enormous and almost entirely unmet demand?*

The founders nod.

*And the product has never once notified the user that this option exists?*

They nod again.

The meeting runs long.

---

The brain is the most sophisticated information processing system ever produced. It is also shipping, right now, in hundreds of millions of units, running code that was written during a window that closed decades ago, in circumstances the user did not choose, with no built-in mechanism for review or revision.

The update exists.

It has always existed.

We just forgot to tell people about it.
