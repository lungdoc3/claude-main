# Myth Buster: "Natural = Safe; Synthetic = Dangerous"
## Part 1 — Myth Buster Callout (follows "Safety Is Relational")

---

*[Note: Tight myth-buster. Dismantles the natural/synthetic safety dichotomy. Spine: safety is relational, not pharmacological. The origin of the molecule tells you almost nothing. Randy's voice. First-pass draft.]*

---

The natural equals safe assumption is one of the most dangerous things a person can carry into this work.

Ayahuasca is entirely natural. It is also a monoamine oxidase inhibitor that can produce a fatal hypertensive crisis when combined with the wrong medications, a fact the vine does not advertise. Peyote is a plant that has been used ceremonially for thousands of years. It also places significant cardiovascular demand on the body and carries real risk for people with certain heart conditions. The Amazon rainforest is full of compounds that will kill you. Natural is a statement about origin. It is not a safety rating.

The inverse assumption fails just as completely. A synthetic psilocybin compound administered in a well-screened, properly prepared clinical context by a skilled guide is, by any reasonable measure, safer than drinking an uncharacterized ayahuasca brew in an unvetted ceremony with no intake screening and a facilitator whose credentials are a compelling Instagram account. The context, the screening, the relationship, and the preparation made it safe.

This is the spine of everything in this section. Safety is not a property of the compound. It is a property of the conditions surrounding it. Source matters far less than set, setting, screening, and the quality of the container. A person who walks into ceremony having done serious preparation, with a skilled guide, in a well-held space, working with a synthetic compound, is safer than a person who walks in unprepared, unscreened, and poorly supported, working with a plant that grew in the ground.

The molecule does not know you. The relationship does.
