# Phase One: Coming Home

*Working Draft | Healing with Psychedelics: A Physician's Perspective*

The snow globe has been shaken. Hard. The snow is still in the air. You can make out shapes through it now, the outlines of the familiar landscape, but nothing is where you remember it being, and what looks like a clear path may not be.

This is navigating the blizzard. Proceed accordingly.

The brain, returning to ordinary reality after a significant psychedelic experience, does something predictable: it attempts to reestablish order. Predictive machinery that was temporarily disrupted begins to reassert itself. Emotional salience is heightened. Everything feels significant because, to the nervous system right now, everything is. Interpretive urgency is high. The mind wants to make meaning out of what just happened, to organize it, to know what it means and what to do about it. Identity itself may feel fluid in ways that are disorienting even when they are not unpleasant.

None of this means something is wrong. It means the work happened and the system is recalibrating.

Your job right now is not to interpret. It is to stabilize.

## Stabilize Before You Symbolize

Sleep. Nutrition. Water. Routine. Safety. These are not secondary concerns to be addressed after you have figured out what the experience meant. They are the primary concerns.

Premature meaning-making is one of the more reliable hazards of this phase. The urgency to understand, to capture, to translate the experience into a story that can be carried forward, is real and it is not entirely misplaced. But meaning made in this state has a tendency to lock in distortions. The lens you are looking through right now is not your ordinary lens. The insights that feel most urgent, most certain, most in need of immediate action are exactly the ones that most need time to settle before you touch them.

Give the snow time to fall before you try to read the landscape.

This applies to the big things, decisions, confrontations, realizations about your relationship or your career or your life's direction, and it applies to the small things too. The same routine creates the same person. You came back from wherever you went because something in you wanted to be different. Honor that by changing small things now and large things later, when you are standing on solid ground.

## You Are Wide Open. That Cuts Both Ways.

Here is something that does not get said often enough: the openness that makes ceremony so powerful does not close the moment ceremony ends. You are still open. Wide open. The filters through which you ordinarily process information are still loosened. Your normal skepticism is dialed down. Your risk assessment is operating below its usual threshold. Your bullshit detector, ordinarily one of your more reliable instruments, is taking a break.

This is beautiful in one direction. Everything feels luminous. Love is accessible in a way it usually is not. The world has a quality of meaning and aliveness that ordinary consciousness rarely touches.

It is also a vulnerability.

Ideas that your ordinary mind would examine carefully, you may now accept whole. Conspiracy theories become more interesting. Esoteric frameworks feel more plausible. The person who catches you in this state with a compelling story, about what your experience meant, about what you should do next, about who you should become, has unusual access. This is not the moment to be receiving those transmissions.

What you bring in during this window matters. Think of it as a post-ceremony dieta.

Before ceremony you restrict what you consume, physically, emotionally, digitally, to prepare the vessel. The same principle applies afterward. The opening that ceremony created is still present, and what flows in through that opening will have unusual influence. Old habits, numbing behaviors, toxic environments, algorithmic rabbit holes, the ambient noise of news and social media: all of it lands differently now, and not in a good way. The phone in your pocket is not neutral. Every scroll is a transmission from the old paradigm, arriving through an open channel into a system that is still trying to figure out what it is.

This is not about purity. It is about not pouring muddy water into a clean vessel.

## Coming Home to the People Who Waited

You are walking back through your front door. The people on the other side of it have been living their lives while you were away. Ordinary, real, unaltered lives. They are glad to see you. They are also, if they are honest, a little uncertain about who exactly is coming through that door.

They have been running the old version of you in your absence. The model they carry is detailed and load-bearing and it was built around someone who has, in some meaningful way, just changed. They will feel that before they can name it. The space between their model and the actual you standing in front of them is information for both of you.

What they do not need, in the first moments, is the full download.

The love you are feeling is real. It may be the most real thing you have felt in years. That does not mean they are equipped to receive it at the intensity at which you are currently generating it. What feels to you like a gift may feel to them like a weather event. Come home as a person who loves them. Not as an emissary from another dimension.

Ask about them first. What happened while you were away? What was hard? What was good? Let yourself be genuinely interested in the answer, because something always happened and it mattered even if it does not feel mythologically significant compared to what you just went through. Their reality was entirely real during your absence. Treat it that way.

The things you most need to say can be said in a few days, when you have both feet on the ground and the words are yours and not the ceremony's.

## The Integration Diet

Protect the window. Not forever. For now.

Stay away from work if you possibly can. Give yourself a few days. The demands of professional life, the inbox, the deadlines, the interpersonal dynamics of the office, pull hard toward the old operating system. Every role you play at work is a role the system built around the old version of you, and stepping back into that role right now, when your identity is still fluid enough to be shaped, is an opportunity that deserves more intention than most people give it.

Stay off social media. The algorithmic machinery is exquisitely calibrated to capture attention and generate emotional response. It will find you wherever you are vulnerable. This is not the moment to hand it access.

Be cautious about what ideas you let in. Not permanently. Right now. Some ideas, encountered at the right moment of openness, embed in ways that are very hard to dislodge. The shamanic traditions have a word for uninvited things that take up residence in an open mind. You do not need the word to take the warning seriously.

Move your body. Spend time outside. Sleep as much as you need. Let the ordinary world be ordinary for a few days before you ask it to help you interpret what happened somewhere extraordinary.

## Your Relationships Are Already Changing

They were changing before you got home. They will continue to change whether you intend it or not. Every relationship you are in exists in a system that was calibrated around the version of you that existed before the ceremony, and that version has shifted.

Some of what changes will be immediately and obviously good. People close to you may notice something different before you have said a word about where you have been. Children, especially, are extraordinarily sensitive to the quality of parental presence. Partners who have been watching you struggle with something may feel a loosening. These reflections are real and they are worth receiving.

Some of what changes will be harder. Systems seek equilibrium. When one part of a system changes, the rest of the system applies pressure to restore what it recognizes as normal. The people in your life will, without any conscious intention to do so, reach for the old version of you. They will say the things that always worked, make the jokes that always landed, expect the reactions that always came. The pull to step back into the familiar character will be strong, because it relieves the tension and returns everyone to known ground.

Resist it quietly. You do not need to announce that you have changed. You do not need to explain or justify or defend it. You only need to be who you actually are, steadily and without drama, and let the system adjust to that.

The adjustment will not always be comfortable. That is not a sign that something is wrong.

It is a sign that something is real.
