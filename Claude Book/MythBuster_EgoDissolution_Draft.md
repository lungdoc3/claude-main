# Myth Buster: "Ego Dissolution Is the Goal"
## Part 1 — Myth Buster Callout

---

*[Note: Tight myth-buster paragraph. The myth: ego dissolution is what you're aiming for. The reality: it happens sometimes, it can be profound when it does, and treating it as a target actively works against the process. Physician's perspective included. Randy's voice. First-pass draft.]*

---

Ego dissolution is real. It can be one of the most significant experiences of a person's life. It is not a key performance indicator.

The moment you make it a goal, it becomes an obstacle. The ego trying to dissolve itself is a contradiction in terms. You have just sent the narrator on a mission to eliminate the narrator. What you get instead is performance anxiety, which is precisely the kind of effortful self-monitoring that keeps the story running at full volume. In thirty-five years of medicine, I have never seen a patient achieve a better outcome by gripping harder. The sessions that produce the deepest access are the ones where the person surrendered the agenda entirely.

Ego dissolution, when it happens, is a byproduct of going deep enough. It is not the mechanism of healing. It is one possible expression of it. Contact with real material at real depth produces change. That contact does not require a full dissolution of the self. It requires honest engagement with what is actually there.

Chase the process. Let the outcomes arrive.
