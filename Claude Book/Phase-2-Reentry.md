# Phase Two: Re-entry

*Working Draft | Healing with Psychedelics: A Physician's Perspective*

When a massive object changes, every other object in its gravitational field feels it. The closer the orbit, the stronger the perturbation. Objects at the outer edges may barely register the shift at all. Objects in close orbit feel it immediately, in ways that can be disorienting even when the change itself is entirely for the better.

You are the massive object. You have changed. And everyone in your field is about to feel it, in proportion to how close they are.

This is not a warning. It is physics. The disruption that the people closest to you experience is not a measure of how fragile they are or how wrong you have been to do this work. It is a measure of proximity. Understanding that distinction will help you navigate what comes next with more patience and less guilt.

We move outward from the center.

## The Innermost Orbit: Partner and Children

Your partner occupies a unique position in this. Nobody else carries as detailed a model of you. Nobody else has as much load-bearing investment in who you are and how you behave. Nobody else is running as many daily processes organized around the version of you that existed before ceremony. We have covered this territory already, and we will return to it. For now: the disruption your partner feels is proportional to how close they are, and closeness here is measured in years and in the accumulated weight of shared life. Give them room. Give them honesty. Give them time.

Children are a different kind of sensor entirely.

They do not process change the way adults do. They do not have the cognitive apparatus to name what they are noticing or to construct a narrative around it. What they have is something more immediate and more accurate: they feel the quality of your presence. They register attunement, or the absence of it, before a word has been spoken. They know, at a level that bypasses language, whether the person who just walked in the door is actually there.

This means two things. First, children will often register genuine positive change before any adult in your life does. If something real shifted in ceremony, your kids may reflect it back to you unprompted, in the offhand comment or the willingness to climb into your lap, before you have told anyone where you have been. Second, they will also register the disruption, the fluidity, the not-quite-landed quality of the early integration period, and they will feel it as instability even if they cannot say so.

Land before you parent. The same instruction applies here as everywhere in Phase One. Stabilize first. Your children need you present, not profound.

## Close Friends

Here is where the picture gets more complex and, for many people, more surprising.

Some friendships will deepen. The person who could only meet you in a certain register (lighter, more defended, more performed) may find that the new version of you creates space for something more real. Conversations that always stayed at a certain level may suddenly be capable of going further. This is one of the quiet gifts of this work that does not get talked about enough. Genuine change in one person can unlock something in the people around them that was always there but had no opening.

Some friendships will reveal themselves as thinner than they appeared.

This is harder. What often happens is not that the friend has changed; it is that you have, and the friendship was calibrated to a version of you that no longer fits as comfortably. The group you always went out with, drank with, complained with, laughed with: it may still be the same group. What has changed is your tolerance for a certain quality of connection. Time feels more finite. Superficial suddenly feels like an actual texture rather than just the default. The gap between what the conversation is and what it could be becomes harder to sit with.

Some of those friends will feel the change and push back. They may name it directly or they may simply apply pressure, through humor or through the familiar dynamics that always brought you back into line. You have changed. They will not always like it. Some will feel threatened by it without being able to say why.

A word of caution here. The impulse to accelerate this process, to curate your social world aggressively in the first weeks after ceremony, to cut people off because they no longer match the frequency: resist it. Give it time. The judgment that feels like clarity right now may look different from six months out. The friend who seems suddenly intolerable may be a person you love who is simply in a different place, not a person you need to leave behind.

The relationship changes. Let it change at its own pace. Stay awake to what it becomes.

## Work and Colleagues

Your career is a relationship. It is also, for many people, a significant part of their identity, and identity is exactly what this work disturbs.

The doctor who is the doctor, the lawyer who is the lawyer, the executive whose entire sense of self is organized around what they do and how well they do it: when the identity starts to loosen, the career can feel like a costume that no longer fits. The role, the title, the daily rituals of professional life, the way colleagues interact with you as the person they have always known you to be — all of it is a casting, and the casting is based on the old version.

Some things will start to rub differently. Smalltalk gets heavier. The meetings that always felt like the texture of a normal day start to feel like an enormous investment of energy for an unclear return. The priorities that organized your professional ambition may seem, if not wrong, then at least worth examining in a way they never were before.

This is real information. It deserves to be taken seriously and not acted on immediately.

Change your relationship with your career before you change your career. This is the more precise and usually the more useful intervention. What you are actually in relationship with is not the job title but the meaning you derive from the work, the identity you have built around it, the way it structures your time and your sense of self. Those things can shift without the job changing at all. And the shift may reveal that the work itself is more aligned with who you are becoming than it appeared to be when you were looking at it through the old lens.

If, after the initial period has settled and the snow is on the ground rather than in the air, the career still does not fit — then you have information worth acting on. Not before.

## A Specific Warning

The medicine path will call to some of you. The retreat center in Costa Rica. The integration coaching practice. The ceremony space. The decision to leave whatever you were doing and devote yourself to carrying this work into the world.

Sometimes this is real. Sometimes a person's genuine gifts and genuine calling converge with this work and something true is being recognized.

More often, at least in the immediate aftermath of a significant experience, it is the enlightened ego finding a new costume. The career change is the same move as the spiritual inflation, wearing professional clothes. The identity that was loosened by the medicine needs something new to organize around, and the medicine path offers a narrative that feels simultaneously humble and exceptional: I am giving up my old life to serve something greater.

This is worth examining with great honesty and considerable time before acting on it. Genuine calling does not evaporate if you wait six months. If it is real, it will still be real when you are standing on solid ground. If it dissolves when the snow settles, you have learned something important about the difference between inspiration and instruction.

The medicine does not need more retreat centers. It needs more people doing the quiet, unglamorous, daily work of actually integrating what they found.

## The Pattern Across All of It

As you move through the relationships in your orbit, from the innermost to the outermost, the same dynamic appears at every level. The people who knew you well will feel the change most acutely. Some will grow with it. Some will resist it. Some will eventually update their model. Some will not be able to.

What you are doing, in each encounter, is the same thing you have been doing since you walked back through the door: showing up as who you actually are, in rooms that were designed for someone slightly different, and staying there.

The rooms will adjust. It takes longer than you expect. And it is, without question, the work.
