# No Purge, No Bueno

*Part 2: The Substances — Ayahuasca chapter, aside*

I have one client I've served many times over the years. After an unusually strong night of ceremony, she found me afterward looking distressed. Her first question skipped past what she'd actually experienced and went straight to what was wrong with the medicine, because she hadn't purged.

I stayed quiet and let her keep talking. Silence does more work in that conversation than questions do, and people usually fill it with exactly the information you need. She filled it with conviction. The medicine had to be off, or the batch had to be weak, because in her experience the real work only started once her body emptied itself onto the floor of the maloca. No purge, no bueno, her words exactly.

I asked what the night had actually felt like without it. The night itself, by her own account, had been calm. Nothing dramatic in her body, nothing to point to. But the belief that calm meant nothing had happened was loud enough to bring her to me in distress an hour later.

She got to the real insight herself, which is usually how it works in that room. The rule running her reaction, things have to look a certain way to count, had already been operating in her marriage, her work, and the private scorecard she kept for whether a day had gone well. The ceremony simply put it in front of her clearly enough to finally see it.

She shows people what they need to see before they understand why they needed to see it. That night, what she needed to see lived in the question she'd already decided the answer to before she found me, not in her body.

Purging is plumbing, not proof.
