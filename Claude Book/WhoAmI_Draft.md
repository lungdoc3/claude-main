Why should you trust a physician on this subject?

Honestly, you probably shouldn't. Most physicians are remarkably uninformed about psychedelics. The training doesn't cover it. The culture discourages it. The typical physician who writes about plant medicine has read the research, attended a conference or two, and formed opinions from a careful distance. That distance is exactly the problem.

I am not that physician.

I have spent over thirty-five years in medicine, most of them in critical care. Real medicine. Lung transplants. Families in waiting rooms at two in the morning. The kind of work where you understand very quickly that competence without presence is just mechanics. That part of my background matters here, but not for the reason you might think. It matters because it taught me to recognize the difference between managing something and actually healing it. We were extraordinarily good at the first. We were not good at the second.

The other part of my background is the one most physicians in my position keep quiet. I don't.

My primary medicine is ayahuasca. I have been working with it for well over a decade. In ceremony, as a participant. As a guide and facilitator for groups and individuals. I have trained directly with indigenous practitioners. I have served the medicine in settings where the stakes were real and the responsibility was mine. I have done the same depth of work with psilocybin and San Pedro. This is not research from the outside. This is not clinical curiosity. This is a practice, sustained and serious, that has shaped everything I understand about how healing actually happens.

Very few physicians have practiced serious medicine in both worlds. I have. That is why I wrote this book.
