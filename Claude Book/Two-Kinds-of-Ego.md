# Two Kinds of Ego

*Working Draft | Healing with Psychedelics: A Physician's Perspective*

I trained at Harvard. If you want to find the largest collection of outsized egos on the planet, that is a reasonable place to start looking.

I have worked alongside surgeons who were, by any ordinary social standard, absolute assholes. Arrogant, imperious, constitutionally incapable of admitting uncertainty. The kind of men and women you would not choose to have dinner with under any circumstances.

I referred my patients to them anyway. I told my patients plainly: I am not sending you to find a new friend. I am sending you to the person most likely to get you out of that operating room alive.

I meant it. I still mean it.

There is something in the outsized medical ego that is not just tolerable but genuinely useful. Think about what the job actually requires. To go toe to toe with death, you have to believe, on some level, that you can win. That belief is not rational. Death has an undefeated record. The surgeon who has looked honestly at the odds and retained the conviction that they are going to beat them anyway is, in some sense, operating from a productive delusion. The ego is what makes that possible. The ego that says: not today. Not this patient. Not on my watch.

Does it lead you astray? Of course it does. Ego distorts risk estimation. It can make you attempt what should not be attempted and call failures a learning experience and keep going when you should have stopped. These are real costs and they produce real harm. But I will put my money on the big ego to get you out of trouble in the OR. I have seen what the alternative looks like and it is not better.

Here is the crucial thing about the medical ego, though: it does not pretend to be something else.

The asshole surgeon is identifiable as an asshole. The ego is visible, legible, and honest about what it is. Everyone in the room knows who the ego belongs to and roughly how large it is. The patient may not like it. The nurses definitely do not like it. But no one mistakes it for humility. No one is confused about what they are dealing with.

The ego and the patient also want the same thing. The surgeon's need to win and the patient's need to survive are pointed in the same direction. The ego's hunger for victory is, in that context, the patient's best asset.

## A Different Animal Entirely

The enlightened ego is not a bigger version of the medical ego. It is a different species.

It does not look like arrogance. It looks like depth. It speaks the language of surrender, of dissolution, of love as the organizing principle of reality. It has done the work. It has been in ceremony more times than it can count. It has sat with the medicine in places you have not been and received things you have not received, and it would very much like to share all of that with you.

From the inside, it does not feel like ego at all. It feels like service. It feels like an obligation to give back what was given. It feels, often, like genuine compassion, and some of it is. That is what makes it so difficult to see and so difficult to name.

The medical ego wants to defeat your disease. The ego and the patient are allies in the same direction.

The enlightened ego wants to be the instrument of your healing. That is a different relationship entirely. In that version, you are not the protagonist of your own story. You are the occasion for theirs.

In conventional medicine, we have structures designed to manage the ego's known pathologies: informed consent, peer review, morbidity and mortality conferences, licensing boards, liability. These systems exist because we know that ego distorts judgment and we have decided, as a field, to build external checks. They are imperfect. They fail. But they exist.

The psychedelic space has almost none of this. The practitioner meets you in your most open and impressionable state, often in legal gray zones that make formal oversight impossible, with no standardized credentialing and no external accountability. What you have is the guide's own character and the culture of the communities they come from.

Into that vacuum, the enlightened ego moves freely. And because it wears the face of wisdom rather than arrogance, the ordinary defenses that might catch an obvious ego are not deployed. You are not looking for it. You are not expecting to need to.

## The Particular Danger

The surgeons I trained with who were technically brilliant and personally insufferable were, at their worst, bad colleagues. Difficult to work with. Occasionally harmful to the people around them. But inside the operating room, pointing at the thing that was trying to kill you, their ego was oriented correctly.

A guide with an enlightened ego meets you when you are not in danger. You are in a ceremony. You are open, impressionable, and extending trust. What you need, in that moment, is someone who has genuinely gotten out of the way, who is holding the space for your experience rather than having one of their own at your expense.

The guide whose ego has wrapped itself in spiritual authority cannot fully do that. They are too present in the room. Their need to be the instrument of your healing is too loud. The ceremony becomes, in subtle ways, about them: their perception, their intervention, their wisdom, their relationship with the medicine.

You may not notice. The ceremony is not a context in which you are running your usual defenses. You may come out of it convinced you were in exceptional hands. The enlightened ego is skilled at producing that conviction.

This is not a small risk. This is the risk.

The medicine is powerful and the container it is served in matters enormously. Choosing a guide is not like choosing a surgeon, where the ego's functional value can sometimes outweigh its interpersonal costs. It is more like choosing a therapist: the relationship itself is the treatment, and the character of the person holding it determines everything.

The question to bring with you is not whether your guide is impressive. It is whether your guide has genuinely learned to get out of the way.

Those are not the same thing. In this space, they are almost opposites.
