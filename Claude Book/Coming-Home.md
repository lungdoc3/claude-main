# Coming Home

*Working Draft | Healing with Psychedelics: A Physician's Perspective*

I am, without question, the luckiest man alive. I know this with a certainty that did not come easily, and I know it because of the work. I also know that I did the lifting. The ceremonies, the preparation, the integration: that was mine to do and I did it.

And I know, with equal certainty, that I could not have done any of it without my wife.

Which is why I want to speak directly to something that integration guides often gloss over and that nobody talks about honestly: coming home to a partner after ceremony. Because the stakes here are higher than anywhere else, and how you manage this particular transition matters more than most people realize until they get it wrong.

## The Model Problem

We have already established that everyone carries their own version of you. A working model assembled from accumulated experience, weighted toward the recent and the memorable, updated only when new information arrives that is different enough from the existing model to demand revision.

Your partner's model of you is the most detailed one in existence. Built from more data points than anyone else has access to, refined over years of daily proximity, and deeply load-bearing: the relationship is structured around it. Everything she knows about how to be with you is organized around that model.

You left as a particular version of yourself. She has been running that version in your absence. As long as the you that walks back through the door is close enough to the version she has been carrying, the system adjusts smoothly. The greater the delta between her model and the real you who comes home, the more friction there will be: until the model updates, or until you start acting more like the old you. How you manage that friction is part of your integration. A significant part.

## What She Was Doing While You Were Away

Here is the thing that took me a long time to fully absorb: while I was off in Peru or wherever the work was taking me, my wife was at home. Going to work. Taking care of her daughter. Walking the dogs. Living her life. Content in the way that people are content when they are doing what they are doing and not in the middle of something world-rearranging.

Not singing medicine songs about how love is all there is content. Just content.

That matters. Her reality during your absence was entirely real, entirely valid, and entirely different from yours. She was not on a journey. She was at home, holding everything down, probably missing you, possibly worried about you, definitely not having her understanding of reality gently rearranged by Grandmother Ayahuasca. To walk in the door and expect her to match your frequency is not just unrealistic. It is a failure to extend to her the same respect for her experience that you are asking her to extend to yours.

There is also, on her side of this, something that deserves acknowledgment: the fear. Of being left behind. Of missing out on something she cannot quite access. Of feeling, however briefly and irrationally, like second place to an experience she did not have and cannot fully understand. These feelings are real and they are not unreasonable and they will not be helped by your immediate and enthusiastic download of everything that just happened to you.

## Coming Home Wide Open

You walk in the door in a particular state. You are glowing, yes. There is a quality of openness and love that feels enormous and that you desperately want to share with the person you love most.

But you also have a look in your eyes like you have seen some things.

Your brain has been destabilized. The filters through which you normally process incoming information have been loosened. Which means two things are true simultaneously: you are more sensitive than usual to everything in the environment, and you are less accurate than usual in interpreting what you are receiving. Your nervous system, trying to reorient in familiar territory, is scanning everything. The old signal-reading systems are running hot and getting it wrong. What was once a knowing smile in response to an inside joke can register, in your current state, as the grimace of distaste you have always found so difficult. That is not her. That is your destabilized system doing its best to assign meaning to ambiguous information.

Clarify before you assume. This is good advice at any time. Right now it is essential. You owe it to both of you.

And when you try to share the experience: lower your expectations substantially. Not because she does not love you or does not care. Because it is not a spectator sport, what you just went through. Trying to describe it is like trying to describe a fever dream to someone who was not feverish. Vacation pictures are bad enough. Give her the feeling rather than the content. Tell her you went somewhere important and you are glad to be home and you will need some time to land. That is enough for now. The rest can unfold over days and weeks.

## The Most Dangerous Animal in the Jungle

There is one more hazard that nobody in this space talks about candidly enough, and I am going to name it directly because I have seen it damage relationships and I have felt its pull myself.

The enlightened ego.

You go into ceremony and leave your ego at the door, or at least you try to. Something real happens. Something opens. You return carrying genuine insight, genuine love, genuine change. And then, faster than you would believe possible, the ego that you thought you had left behind reappears, dusts itself off, and proceeds to take full credit for everything.

There is no more dangerous animal in the jungle than the enlightened ego. It combines the worst of both worlds: the ego's need to be special and superior, now wrapped in the unassailable authority of spiritual experience. It knows more than you. It has been places you have not been. It has done the work and it would like you to know that.

Do not be that person. Not with your partner, not with your friends, not with anyone. The work you did was yours and the insights you returned with are real. None of that requires an audience. None of it is improved by being performed. The most powerful demonstration of genuine change is not talking about it. It is being different in ways that people notice without being told to look.

## Learning to Come Home

It took me a long time to learn how to do this well. I came home wrong more times than I am proud of, and my wife extended a patience and grace that I did not always deserve.

What I learned, slowly: land before you try to connect. Give the nervous system time to reorient. Let the open state settle rather than performing it. Ask about her experience before launching into yours. Be genuinely interested in what happened at home while you were away, because something always does and it mattered even if it does not feel mythologically significant.

Come home as a person who loves her. Not as an emissary from another dimension.

The love you returned with is real. Let it show up as presence, as attention, as someone who is genuinely glad to be back. That lands. Everything else can wait.
