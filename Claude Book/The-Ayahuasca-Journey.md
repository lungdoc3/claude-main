# The Ayahuasca Journey

*Working Draft | Healing with Psychedelics: A Physician's Perspective*

Nobody throws a space launch together last minute.

Months of engineering. Weeks of systems checks. Days of countdown protocols. By the time the engines ignite, an enormous amount of work has already happened. The launch is not the beginning. The launch is the moment when everything that has been building finally releases.

A ceremony night with Grandmother Ayahuasca works the same way. If you have prepared correctly, the experience already exists before you drink. It has been assembling itself through the dieta, through the intention work, through the quieting of ordinary noise and the gradual orientation of your attention toward something larger. You are not arriving at a starting line. You are arriving at a threshold that has been slowly constructing itself around you for weeks.

You can feel this in the air.

It is not quite heaviness. Not quite seriousness, though seriousness is there. It is more like the quality of air before weather: charged, expectant, alive with something that has not yet declared itself. The people around you feel it too. Those who have sat in ceremony before carry a particular kind of stillness that is not calm but readiness. Those for whom this is new carry something else: a brightness in the eyes, a quality of energy that has nowhere yet to go. There is wildness behind the surface. There is risk in the room, and everyone present knows it. That knowledge is not frightening exactly, though fear is part of it. It is more like the feeling of standing at the edge of something real.

You are about to climb into a one-man space capsule atop engines of almost incomprehensible power.

## The Countdown

The ritual holds the space. The songs, the prayers, the ceremonial opening of the space: these are not decoration. They are the final systems check. Each element of the ceremony is a verification that everything is in place, that the conditions are right, that you have done what was asked of you and are prepared to receive what is offered.

You check your talisman. The small object you have carried here, the thing that tethers you to something specific and known. It is in place.

Now is the time for prayer. For gratitude. For a clear and honest statement of intention that you make not to the room but to something larger than the room. You are on the cusp of something that cannot be predicted, controlled, or fully prepared for, and you know it. You do not know where this is going. You know only that you are going to leave and that you are going to come back. Everything else is unknown. More than that: everything else is unknowable. And you drink anyway.

The cup is smaller than people expect.

The taste is its own introduction. Dark, bitter, earthy, with a depth and density that has no easy comparison. You will taste this medicine before it works, and the taste alone is a kind of signal. Something in the body recognizes it before the mind has time to decide what to make of it.

## Ignition

The onset is not subtle. It does not creep up on you the way some medicines do. It announces itself.

In the thirty to sixty minutes after drinking, the body begins to communicate. There may be nausea: genuine, unmistakable, the kind that cannot be willed away. The purge, if it comes, is not something that happens to you. It is something you do, something the body does in your name, releasing what it no longer needs to carry. The traditions have always understood the purge as medicine in its own right. In my experience that understanding is correct. What comes up is not only physical.

The physical sensations deepen. A heaviness in the limbs. A heightening of the skin's sensitivity to temperature and texture. The body begins to feel more than usually present, more than usually real, while simultaneously losing its ordinary borders. You become very aware that you are in a body and that the body is in a space and that both are subject to something larger than either.

The engines are building thrust.

The visual field shifts. With eyes closed, patterns emerge: geometric, architectural, luminous, moving with a logic that feels both entirely alien and somehow familiar. The Shipibo-Conibo describe the medicine's visual dimension as a language, and this is exactly right. You are not seeing things. You are being shown things. Whether you can read what is being shown is a different question.

The shaking, when it comes, is deep. Not the surface trembling of cold or fear but something that seems to originate in the middle of the body and radiate outward. It is not unpleasant. It is the body processing a magnitude of input it was not designed to handle in ordinary states. The sound of the icaros moves through the shaking the way a hand moves through water: the medicine and the songs are not separate things. You can feel the songs on your skin.

The noise builds. The vibration intensifies. You are somewhere in the middle of it, no longer tracking individual sensations but lost in the totality of them, until:

The silence.

Not the absence of sound. Something more precise than that. The silence that creates the sounds. The ground beneath all of it from which everything else rises. The moment when the capsule clears the atmosphere and the engines that were the entirety of your experience a moment ago are simply gone, and what remains is a quality of stillness that contains everything.

That moment. That silence.

You have left.

## What Happens There

I will not describe the journey in specific terms, because there are no specific terms that hold across people or across nights. What I will tell you is that the range is real and the range is wide.

It is not always beautiful. It is not always comfortable. It is not always even close to what you had hoped for or imagined. It can be terrifying, until it isn't. It can be grievous, until it lifts. It can be boring in a way that has its own teaching. It can be the most extraordinary experience of your life. It will almost certainly be things you do not have language for afterward, which is not a failure of language but a recognition that you have been somewhere language does not reach.

Here is what I used to think was a disclaimer and now understand to be the deepest truth I know about this medicine: it will be exactly what you need.

Not what you want. Not what you expected. Not what the person next to you got. What you need. And nobody knows what you need better than the you underneath all the accumulated armor and story and strategy: the you that has been waiting patiently under all of that for exactly this kind of access. Grandmother takes you where you need to go. She does not take you where you want to go. She does not take you where you think you should go. She takes you where you need to go, and she is not interested in your objections.

The extra baggage you insist on bringing will alter the flight characteristics. It will not ground you, but it will complicate the trajectory in ways you will feel and not enjoy. This is another argument for the preparation. You cannot leave your history at the door, but you can reduce the load.

## The Return

The return is gradual. The visual dimension softens. The body reasserts itself, its weight, its temperature, its familiar signals. The room becomes a room again. The people around you resolve back into individuals. The icaros continue and the ceremony holds you in the space between where you have been and where you ordinarily live, which is exactly where you need to be held for a little while.

There is, in the hours after ceremony, a quality of clarity that is different from ordinary clarity. Things that were complicated feel simple. Not because the complications have gone away but because you are seeing them from somewhere that is no longer inside them. This does not last indefinitely. The ordinary mind reasserts itself. The walls come back up, gradually, over days and weeks. But something has changed. Something always changes.

You have come back.

The question that matters is not what happened during the journey. It is what you do now that you have returned.
