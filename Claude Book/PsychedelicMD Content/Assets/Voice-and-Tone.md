# Voice and Tone Reference

## The On-Camera Persona

Physician first. The credential is not decoration — it is what earns the audience's permission to take these ideas seriously. The videos should feel like sitting across from a doctor who has thought very carefully about something and wants to tell you what he has learned.

Not a guru. Not an advocate. A physician who follows the evidence, including the evidence that challenges the models he was trained in.

---

## Tone Markers

**What it sounds like:**
- Direct and confident, not aggressive
- Intellectually serious, occasionally funny
- Personal without being confessional
- The voice of someone who has been in ceremony and in the clinic and has reconciled those two things

**What it does not sound like:**
- A pitch
- A testimonial
- An advocate trying to convince you of something
- A disclaimer-heavy legal document

---

## The Central Reframe (use in every video, in some form)

We are not talking about recreational drug use. We are talking about how the brain actually works, what healing actually requires, and why the tools that have been used for thousands of years are only now being taken seriously by Western medicine. The physician's job is to follow the evidence. This is where the evidence is pointing.

---

## Language Notes

- "Medicine" not "drug" when referring to plant medicines
- "Ceremony" not "trip" or "session" in traditional contexts
- "Grandmother Ayahuasca" or "Grandmother" is acceptable in context — explain it the first time
- Avoid "psychonaut," "tripping," "getting high"
- "Integration" should always be explained briefly — it is not universally understood
- Spell out plant names fully on first use: Psilocybin (magic mushrooms), Ayahuasca (the vine and leaf brew), Wachuma (San Pedro cactus)

---

## The One Rule

If a reasonable person watching the video would describe it as "a doctor talking about how the brain works and why medicine has been treating the wrong things," the video is right. If they would describe it as "a doctor pushing psychedelics," pull back.
