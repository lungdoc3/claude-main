# The Wachuma Journey

*Working Draft | Healing with Psychedelics: A Physician's Perspective*

If ayahuasca is a space launch, Wachuma is a long walk into open country as the sun comes up.

No countdown. No capsule. No engines building toward an explosive release. You step outside and the world is already there, already lit, already doing what the world does: shimmering quietly with a life that was always present and that you simply were not, until now, sufficiently available to notice.

This is a daytime medicine. It belongs to open sky and sunlight the way ayahuasca belongs to the jungle at night. Where one takes you inward with force and purpose, the other takes you outward with warmth and patience. Where one closes around you like a deep current pulling you under, the other opens like a landscape resolving into clarity as the light improves.

Same category. Entirely different territory.

## Before

The air before a Wachuma ceremony does not carry the charged weight of an ayahuasca night. There is no wildness behind the surface, no palpable sense of risk and uncontrolled velocity. What you feel instead is something quieter: a gentle forward lean, an opening of the chest, a quality of attention that is less like readying yourself for departure and more like making yourself available for arrival.

You are not about to leave. You are about to become more present than you ordinarily manage to be.

The preparation still matters. The intention still matters. But the atmosphere is different. Where the night ceremony asks you to sit still and be taken, Wachuma extends an invitation. Come outside. Walk. Touch things. Look at what is actually in front of you. Be with the people around you. He is a social medicine, one of the few that actively wants you to turn toward the world rather than away from it.

The cup, when it comes, is larger than expected. The taste is green and bitter and alive, nothing like the dark density of ayahuasca, more like drinking something that is very directly and unapologetically a plant.

## The Onset

The onset is slow. Not the thirty-minute countdown of ayahuasca but a gradual, unhurried building that can take several hours to make itself fully known. This is not a medicine in a hurry. It does not announce itself so much as it accumulates, like the way warmth builds in a room that has been getting more sunlight all morning without your noticing until suddenly it is warm.

The body is the first to register it clearly. Not nausea, usually, not the gut-level reckoning that ayahuasca sometimes demands, but a softening. The chronic low-level tension most of us carry in our shoulders, our jaw, our chest: it loosens. The body begins to feel inhabited in a way it ordinarily does not. Not heavy but present. Not sedated but genuinely relaxed in a way that is different from ordinary relaxation because it is not effortful.

The senses sharpen. Colors become more themselves. The green of a leaf is not simply more vivid: it is more precisely green, more fully what green actually is, as if ordinary perception had always been operating through a slight film and the film has quietly been removed. Sound deepens. The texture of things under your fingers becomes worth attending to. The world, which had seemed like backdrop, becomes foreground.

Everything shimmers with life.

This is not a metaphor and it is not a visual hallucination in the clinical sense. It is closer to an accurate perception: the recognition that the world is, in fact, alive in every part of it, and that ordinary consciousness is simply too narrowed and too busy to register this most of the time.

## What Wachuma Does

Here is the essential distinction between these two medicines.

Ayahuasca will show you what you need to see. She is a surgeon, and she works in the dark and the deep, and she will expose what needs to be exposed with a precision and a force that does not ask whether you are comfortable with what is being revealed. She is not cruel. She is thorough. The wounds she finds have been there a long time, often unknown to the person carrying them, and her work is to bring them into the light where they can finally begin to heal.

Wachuma helps you feel what you need to feel.

This is the work that comes after surgery. The scar tissue has been loosened. The wound, finally visible after years or decades of armor and avoidance, is tender and real. What it needs now is not more excavation. It needs warmth. Connection. The felt sense of not being alone in it. The visceral, bodily understanding that love exists and is available, that compassion is not a concept, that you belong to the world and the world to you.

He is a medicine of the heart in a direct, physical sense. Not the heart as metaphor but the heart as the organ in the chest that opens and closes and whose opening you can feel when it happens. When Wachuma works, you feel it there first.

The clinical language for what he does includes words like entactogenic, pro-social, somatically integrative. These are accurate. What they describe is the experience of boundaries softening until it becomes genuinely difficult to say where you end and the person beside you begins. Not in a frightening way. In the way that feels like remembering something true that you had forgotten: that the separateness we experience as ordinary reality is a useful construction, not the whole story.

Where ayahuasca is busy, relentless, pressing, filling every available channel with content that demands to be processed, Wachuma creates space. Space in the body. Space between thoughts. Space in the relational field between people. And in that space, things that were too close to be seen can finally be seen. Things that were too painful to be felt can finally be felt. Not because the pain has lessened but because you are no longer carrying it alone.

## The Duration

Wachuma stays. Where an ayahuasca journey typically runs four to six hours, Wachuma can accompany you for ten, twelve, sometimes longer. This is not excess. It is the nature of integrative work. The surgeon operates quickly. The healing takes time.

The long tail of the experience is not incidental. It is the medicine giving you time to work with what has opened. To walk through it. To sit with it in the sunlight. To talk about it with the people beside you who are in their own version of the same process. Ceremony with Wachuma is less a contained event and more an extended, mobile conversation between you and the world, and the medicine holds that conversation open long enough for something to shift.

## What Remains

You do not return from a Wachuma ceremony the way you return from ayahuasca. Ayahuasca brings you back to the ground after having taken you somewhere else entirely. Wachuma does not take you elsewhere. It makes where you already are more available to you.

The return is less a landing and more a gradual dimming of the light, the world settling back into its ordinary register while retaining, at least for a while, some of the aliveness that became visible during the day. The tenderness in the chest. The ease in the body. The residual warmth toward the people around you.

The question that remains is the same one that follows any real ceremony.

Now that you have felt it: what are you going to do with that?
