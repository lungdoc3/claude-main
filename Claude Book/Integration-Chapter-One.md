# What Is Integration

*Working Draft | Healing with Psychedelics: A Physician's Perspective*

To understand a process that is already underway, you should be able to do four things. Define it. Explain what it is trying to accomplish. Know whether it is working. Know when it is finished.

That is the minimum. And it is where we will start.

Here is a definition.

Integration is the process through which real, tangible change is achieved.

You did this for a reason. Something about your experience wasn't working and you wanted it to be different. The medicine gave you access to the tools you needed to make that happen. That is where its job ends and yours begins.

Integration is yours.

That covers the first two questions. What it is, and what it is for. The goal of integration is not insight. Not understanding. Not a profound experience to carry around. The goal is an actual, lived difference in how you move through your life. That is the only finish line worth pointing at.

## The Inside and the Outside

Questions three and four are harder. How do you know if it is working, and how do you know when it is finished. The answer depends, in part, on who is asking.

From the outside, measurement matters. Researchers, clinicians, and institutions need to be able to compare before and after, treated and untreated. These are the people responsible for understanding this work at a population level and making the case for it in the language that policy and medicine require. That requires objectifying a subjective experience, which is an imperfect process with real limitations that the field is actively grappling with. Those are important conversations. The one thing worth saying here is that what is being measured is not the thing. The map is not the territory. Keep that in the room whenever the numbers come out.

From the inside, none of those conversations are relevant to you. You do not need to translate your experience into something measurable. You need to know whether your life is actually changing.

How you know, and how you know when it is done, that is the next chapter. It deserves the room.

## What Comes After That

What I cannot give you is a detailed, step-by-step guide for how to do this. Not because I am withholding it. Because it does not exist. Integration is not a protocol. It is a process that is unique to the person doing it, shaped by what they experienced, what they carried in, and what their life looks like on the other side.

What does exist is a remarkable and growing community of gifted therapists, somatic practitioners, integration guides, and coaches who have developed real expertise in supporting people through this process. They have done the hard work of learning what helps. They speak from experience and from dedication to this work, and they are the right people to help you develop your specific practice. That is their wheelhouse. I am not going to pretend it is mine.

What I also cannot give you is a timeline. The process is non-linear, non-discrete, and shaped by too many variables that are specific to you. Anyone who hands you a schedule is handing you their schedule, or an average that belongs to no one. Either way it becomes one more thing to fall short of.

What I can give you is the lay of the land.

The challenges in integration are predictable in their type, even when they are not predictable in their specific form for you. The immediate post-experience period has its own particular character. Coming home to the people in your life has its own. Returning to work, to your social world, to the systems and roles that were built around the version of you that went into ceremony, that has its own. You will move through these roughly in that order, and each one has recognizable features.

What makes examples useful here is the same thing that makes integration itself possible: pattern recognition. You cannot give someone a map of territory that is unique to them. But you can show them what certain kinds of terrain look like, so that when they encounter it they recognize it rather than simply being lost in it. That recognition, that half-second of "oh, this is one of those" — is the exact capacity the work is trying to build.

The examples that follow are not prescriptions. They are mirrors. If you see yourself in them, that is the point. If successful navigation around those moments looks a certain way, it is not because that way is the only way. It is because it has features worth recognizing when they show up in your own version of the same situation.

That is what comes next.
