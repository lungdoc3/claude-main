## Meaning & society

*Existential and meaning level*
- Psychedelics increase access to questions of meaning and purpose.
- They destabilize previously unquestioned worldviews.
- They can reorganize value hierarchies.
- They intensify encounters with mortality, connection, and responsibility.
- They make meaning unavoidable.

*Societal level*
- Psychedelics challenge prevailing medical and cultural models.
- They expose limitations of symptom-based care.
- They resist standardization.
- They amplify both wisdom and misuse within systems.

## Experience & change

*Experiential level*
- Psychedelics induce non-ordinary states of consciousness.
- They increase perceived meaning and significance.
- They often produce experiences described as mystical or transcendent.
- They amplify context, intention, and expectation.

*Clinical level*
- Psychedelics act as therapeutic catalysts rather than treatments.
- They compress therapeutic timelines.
- They shift trajectories rather than manage symptoms.
- They expose underlying dynamics rather than masking them.
- They increase both opportunity and risk.

*Behavioral level*
- Psychedelics can interrupt maladaptive behavioral loops.
- They increase openness to change.
- They reduce behavioral rigidity.
- They enhance motivation for alignment.

## Mind, emotion & self

*Cognitive level*
- Psychedelics loosen fixed beliefs and assumptions.
- They reduce certainty in habitual narratives.
- They increase cognitive flexibility.
- They allow previously excluded interpretations to surface.
- They disrupt automatic meaning-making.

*Emotional level*
- Psychedelics increase emotional access and intensity.
- They reduce emotional avoidance.
- They allow emotional processing outside habitual defenses.
- They increase emotional lability without directing its content.

*Self and identity level*
- Psychedelics soften or dissolve the sense of a fixed self.
- They destabilize identity narratives.
- They reduce self-referential dominance.
- They allow alternative self-models to emerge.
- They expose identity as a constructed process.

*Learning and plasticity level*
- Psychedelics increase sensitivity to learning.
- They reopen periods of psychological plasticity.
- They facilitate extinction of maladaptive patterns.
- They accelerate reframing and insight.
- They make change easier, not inevitable.

## Perception & body

*Mind-body interface*
- Psychedelics blur the functional separation between psychological and somatic processes.
- Emotional processing is frequently accompanied by measurable physiologic change.
- They may reduce inflammation indirectly by altering perception, meaning, and stress appraisal.
- Subjective insight and biological change are often temporally linked.

*Perceptual level*
- Psychedelics alter sensory processing and perceptual inference.
- They reduce perceptual filtering.
- They increase salience attribution.
- They blur boundaries between internal and external perception.

## Brain & body systems

*Systems neuroscience level*
- Psychedelics reduce the rigidity of large-scale brain networks.
- They weaken top-down predictive control.
- They increase communication between normally segregated regions.
- They increase neural entropy and signal variability.
- They temporarily destabilize entrenched neural patterns.

*Biological and physiological systems level*
- Psychedelics modulate inflammatory signaling within the central nervous system.
- They are associated with reductions in pro-inflammatory cytokines in both neuro and systemic contexts.
- They influence microglial activation states.
- They appear to shift immune signaling toward anti-inflammatory profiles.

*Neuroplasticity and neurogenesis level*
- Psychedelics increase expression of neurotrophic factors, including BDNF.
- They promote dendritic spine growth and synaptogenesis.
- They enhance structural and functional plasticity in cortical circuits.
- These changes occur rapidly and can outlast the acute subjective effects.
- Neurogenesis appears to be context- and region-dependent rather than global.

*Autonomic and stress-response level*
- Psychedelics transiently alter autonomic balance.
- They can reduce chronic sympathetic dominance.
- They influence hypothalamic–pituitary–adrenal axis signaling.
- They may recalibrate stress reactivity rather than blunt it.

## Molecules & receptors

*Molecular level*
- Psychedelics bind to specific receptors without directly replacing endogenous signaling.
- They alter intracellular signaling cascades rather than turning pathways on or off.
- They act transiently, with effects that outlast their presence in the body.
- They do not accumulate with repeated use.

*Receptor and pharmacologic level*
- Psychedelics activate the 5-HT2A receptor in a context-dependent manner.
- They bias receptor signaling toward non-ordinary downstream effects.
- They modulate rather than suppress neural activity.
- They increase sensitivity to both internal and external inputs.
- They shift how signals are weighted rather than what signals exist.
