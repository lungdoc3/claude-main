# Who This Book Is For — And Who It Is Not
## Book Introduction Section

---

*[Note: "Who it's for / not for" section. The "not for" is written to pull in the audience, not exclude them. Two distinct readers acknowledged. Randy's voice. First-pass draft.]*

---

You probably already know that psychedelics have value. You are not here to be convinced.

This book is not a sales pitch. It is not an advertisement. It is not an argument for why plant medicines deserve a seat at the table. That argument has been made, and if you are holding this book, you have already heard it and found it persuasive. We are past that.

What you are here for is something more specific. You want to understand what is actually happening — in the brain, in the body, in the healing process — from someone who has been in both rooms. You want the physician's perspective, not because you need permission, but because the information matters. You want to do this well.

This book is for you.

More precisely, it is for the person who has sat in ceremony at least once, or is seriously preparing to, and is ready to go deeper than the first layer. It is for the person who has moved past curiosity and into commitment, and wants preparation and understanding to match that commitment. It is for anyone who has felt the gap between what the mainstream medical world says about this work and what they have actually experienced — and wants a physician who has stood in that gap to speak honestly about it.

It is also for the professional in this space. The facilitator who wants the medical safety framework not as a credential to display but as a foundation to work from. The therapist, the guide, the practitioner who is already doing the work and wants the clinical depth to do it more safely. Part 4 of this book was written with you specifically in mind.

---

Now for who this book is not for. Read carefully, because this part is not meant to send you away.

This is not the Level 0 book. If you are starting from zero — if you have never encountered this material, are skeptical that psychedelics have any legitimate therapeutic value, or need to be walked through the basics before anything else lands — there are better starting points than this one. This book assumes a foundation. It builds on it.

This is not a how-to manual. It does not tell you where to go, what to take, or how much. It does not provide a protocol, a recipe, or a roadmap to a specific experience. What it provides is understanding — of the mechanisms, the preparation, the risks, the integration, and the arc of healing. What you do with that understanding is your decision.

This is not medical advice, and it does not create a physician-patient relationship between you and me. The clinical information in this book, particularly in Part 4, is offered for education and informed decision-making, not as a substitute for care from a physician who knows your specific history. If you have serious medical conditions or complex medication needs, that context matters. The book addresses exactly why it matters.

This book will not tell you that psychedelics are for everyone. They are not. It will not tell you that they are safe under all conditions. They are not. What it will do is give you the real picture — the mechanisms, the risks, the preparation that makes the difference — so that you can make the most informed decision possible about your own path.

That is the only sales pitch here. Information, honestly delivered.

If that is what you came for, keep reading.
