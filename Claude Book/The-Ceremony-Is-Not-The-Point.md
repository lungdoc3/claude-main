# The Ceremony Is Not The Point

*Working Draft | Healing with Psychedelics: A Physician's Perspective*

America has a billion dollar wedding industry and no marriage industry.

We pour staggering resources into the dress, the venue, the flowers, the cake, the photographer. We spend almost nothing on what comes after: the daily, unglamorous practice of actually being married. The wedding is the visible thing. The marriage is invisible, continuous, and resistant to being photographed.

Anyone who has been married for more than a few years knows which one actually determines the outcome.

We have all encountered a Bridezilla. So consumed by the perfection of the day that the marriage itself becomes an afterthought. The seating chart gets more attention than the relationship. The color of the napkins matters more than the conversation about how to navigate conflict. The wedding is the point. The marriage is what happens when the party is over.

The psychedelic community has its own version.

There is a particular kind of seeker for whom the ceremony has become the destination rather than the departure point. The next retreat. The next medicine. The next facilitator, country, compound. A growing collection of peak experiences and a life that, examined carefully, has not changed in proportion to the investment. The medicine keeps opening the door. They keep standing in the doorway.

A psychedelic experience is like a wedding. What happens inside the ceremony can be genuine, profound, and true. None of that is in question. But the ceremony is not the marriage. It is the beginning of one.

The insights that surface do not integrate themselves. The window that opens does not stay open. What determines whether the experience becomes a transformation or a story you tell at parties is entirely what happens after the ceremony ends.

Integration is the marriage. Daily, invisible, unglamorous. The different decision at the familiar crossroads. The old pattern noticed before it takes over. The relationship approached with new eyes. None of this is dramatic. None of it photographs well.

All of it is the point.

The ceremony is where it starts. Everything that matters comes after.
