# Digital Detox and Noble Silence

*Working Draft | Healing with Psychedelics: A Physician's Perspective*

The goal of what we are doing here, stripped to its most fundamental form, is the interruption of a pattern. Something about your experience is not working. You came here because you want it to be different. That pattern, whatever it is, is not something that happened to you from the outside. It is something you built. Your brain built it. The you that you walk around being every day built it, one reinforced loop at a time.

Which means the thing we need space from, more than anything else during this work, is that you.

Your phone is a direct line back to it. Every notification, every scroll, every message, every reflex to check the feed: these are not neutral acts. They are invitations for the old version of you to reassert itself. And it will take every invitation you offer, because it is desperate right now. When a pattern is threatened, it does not go quietly. It reaches for anything familiar. It calls the number it has always called. Your phone is that number.

Put it away. Not as ritual. Because it is a portal, and what comes through that portal is the exact thing we are here to interrupt.

Noble silence operates on the same principle and gets less credit for it.

A conversation requires a "you" to show up. Not philosophically. Practically, concretely: the moment you are in a social exchange, something has to occupy the role of the person having it. Your brain fills that role automatically and without asking permission, and what it reaches for is the most practiced version available. The practiced version is the old one. The one that knows how to be you in conversation, that has all the patterns and reflexes and self-presentations ready to deploy.

Even eye contact is enough. A glance that lands and holds: now there is a "you" looking back.

Here is the part that is less obvious. Noble silence is not only protection for your own process. It is a gift to the people doing this work alongside you.

Everyone in that room is trying to get distance from the same thing. Their version of it, their own constructed self, their own practiced character with its own reflexes and defenses and needs. They came here for exactly what you came here for. The moment you initiate, a look held a beat too long, a word, a small social gesture that opens a door, you are not just inviting your old self back. You are inviting theirs. You are the troublemaker. Not out of malice. Out of ordinary human habit, which is exactly the problem.

Honor your process. Honor theirs. The two are not separate.

You do not need to explain this. You do not need to announce it, to smile apologetically at someone across the room and telegraph that you are not actually an antisocial person, just a person who has read the instructions. The explanation requires a "you" to deliver it. You know how this goes.

Just do it.

Or don't. That is also information.

People don't try to change. They either change or they don't.
