**Healing and Curing Are Not the Same Thing**

This book uses the word "healing" constantly and deliberately. It is worth spending thirty seconds on why.

Curing is mechanical. A broken bone is set. An infection is treated. A tumor is removed. The body returns to function. Medicine is extraordinarily good at curing things, and curing things matters enormously.

Healing is something different. It addresses what lives underneath the symptoms — the psychological architecture, the unresolved patterns, the places where the self got organized around pain or fear or loss and never found its way back. You can cure a body that has not healed. You can stop the bleeding without touching what caused it.

When this book talks about what psychedelics do, it is talking about healing. That distinction is not semantic. It is the whole argument.

---

**A Word About These Medicines**

For most of the past fifty years, writing about psychedelics fell into one of two categories. Criminalized and dismissed, or passionately defended by the people who believed in them deeply enough to risk saying so. Neither produced honest literature.

The stigma was real and damaging. It drove serious research underground, kept physicians from asking honest questions, and made anyone who spoke positively about plant medicine sound like a true believer with an agenda. When the entire mainstream culture has decided something is dangerous and deviant, the people who know better argue loudly in the other direction. That is what happened. It is still happening.

The pendulum is shifting now. The research is accumulating. The cultural conversation is opening. The field has not always been honest about what it found. This book is that attempt.

This book is that attempt.

These substances produce genuine, documented healing in conditions that have been resistant to everything else medicine has tried. That is real. It is not hype. The research behind it is serious and growing.

They also carry real risk. Small when the work is done seriously. Not zero. Never zero.

Here is the thing that tends to get lost between the stigma and the enthusiasm. These medicines do not adjust the chemistry slightly. They do not take the edge off or smooth things out. They alter the fundamental structure of how you perceive reality, how you experience yourself, and how the self relates to everything around it. They reach into the deepest layers of who you are and reorganize the foundation. When that goes well, it can be the most profound and useful experience of a person's life. When it goes poorly, the consequences are real.

These are not to be trifled with. They are not supplements. They are not a wellness trend. They are powerful, ancient, and serious. They deserve to be approached that way.

That is my physician's promise in this book. Not a sales pitch. Not advocacy. An honest account of what these medicines are, what they do, why they produce healing in some conditions and risk in others, and what you need to know before you sit with them.

The truth lives between the stigma and the hype. That is where we are going.
