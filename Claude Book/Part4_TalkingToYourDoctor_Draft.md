# Talking to Your Doctor About Plant Medicines
## Part 4: Short companion paragraph

Most people feel a flicker of anxiety before they say the word psychedelic out loud to their physician. That is not irrational. Whatever shape it takes (shame, suspicion, the simple dread of being lectured), it keeps people quiet at exactly the moment they should be speaking up. Taking ownership of your healing starts before the ceremony. For some it starts in the exam room, with the decision to say the true thing out loud.

Talk to your doctor. Remember that your visit is about you. You can set the agenda. You can ask whatever questions you may have. Be honest. If you want to discuss the risks and/or the potential benefits of psychedelics in your situation, do it. It's your dime.

What's the worst thing that could happen? Maybe your provider knows less about the subject than you do. Maybe it feels a bit awkward when the usual dynamic of the relationship gets turned upside down. Want to know who is more uncomfortable than you at that moment? Your doctor. Doctors hate to not know. How your doctor handles that moment will tell you a lot. If your doctor is the kind who can recognize the gap between what they know and the information you are looking for as an opportunity for THEIR growth, then it becomes a "let's learn this together" kind of moment. If you are met with pushback, remember that you are dealing with a medical ego as we described earlier in the book. They can be brusque, no doubt. They don't always have the best manners. Remember that whatever poor behavior choice they may make is a response to their inner turmoil. It's not about you. I'm not excusing bad ego behavior. Sometimes understanding who owns what is important.

Whatever the reason you've been avoiding it, it isn't a good enough one. Your doctor does not need training in plant medicine to keep you safe. They need an accurate list of every prescription, every over-the-counter product, and every supplement currently in your system, including the ones that do not look like medications. That list is what protects you, not their approval.

A few questions carry more weight than the rest of the conversation combined. Ask directly: "Is anything I am currently taking, prescription or over the counter, serotonergic?" If you take an SSRI or SNRI, ask how long a safe taper takes and what it would entail. If you use supplements that don't feel like medication (melatonin, St. John's Wort, 5-HTP, a nightly cough syrup), name them by their actual names rather than waving them off as "just vitamins." None of these questions require your doctor to endorse psychedelics, only to share accurate information in both directions.

If your doctor is unwilling to address your concerns for whatever reason, simply ask for help finding a provider who is both capable and comfortable having those conversations with you. Then offer to come back and share what you learn. That's the power move.
