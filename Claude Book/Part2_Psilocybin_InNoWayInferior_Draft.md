# In No Way Inferior

*Part 2: The Substances — Psilocybin chapter, aside*
*Status: Draft, Critic-reviewed (Excellent)*

---

At low doses, psilocybin earns every bit of its fun-and-giggles reputation. Light visual shimmer, a loosened sense of humor, music that suddenly sounds better than it did an hour ago. That playful read is accurate as far as it goes, but it stops short of where the dose can take you.

Push the dose up and the same medicine turns into something else entirely. High-dose psilocybin produces reality-shattering, ego-dissolving experiences that are in no way inferior to the most intense night of ayahuasca ceremony. Same mushroom, same active compound, a completely different category of experience depending entirely on how much of it you take.

Calling mushrooms a trickster, or niños santos, the holy children, was always more accurate than the label gets credit for. Tricksters in every mythology earn that name by being underestimated right up until the moment it costs someone something. The same playful reputation that makes psilocybin approachable at a low dose is exactly what makes people walk into a high dose without the respect it actually requires.

Here's a piece of chemistry most people never learn, and I remember being genuinely startled by it myself the first time I did. Psilocin, the compound your body actually converts psilocybin into, has a real name, 4-hydroxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine. Strip away the prefix and what's left is DMT, the same molecule doing the visionary work in ayahuasca. Psilocin is DMT with one hydroxyl group attached, not a distant pharmacological cousin.

It's also worth knowing that DMT, psilocin, and serotonin all belong to the same tryptamine family. What gets handed to your nervous system at a high dose is a close relative of a molecule it already produces every day, not a stranger arriving from outside the system. That may be part of why the body seems to know exactly what to do with it.

Light and fun at a low dose, in no way inferior at a high one, same molecule doing what tryptamines have always done once you give them enough room to work.
