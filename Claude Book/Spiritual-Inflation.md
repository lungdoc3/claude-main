# Before We Go Further

*Working Draft | Healing with Psychedelics: A Physician's Perspective*

Before we go further, we need to discuss what I believe represents the greatest risk associated with these medicines. It deserves to be spoken to directly, and it deserves to be revisited more than once.

Let us be clear about something first. There are no such things as side effects. There are only effects. Some of those effects are deemed beneficial, others not so much, and that determination is highly dependent upon the circumstances and the agenda of whoever is making it. Language matters here, and "side effect" is a word that lets us pretend something is peripheral when it is not.

As a physician, I can tell you that the potential untoward medical risks associated with these substances are few, predictable, and largely preventable. Cardiovascular considerations, drug interactions, contraindications for certain psychiatric histories: these are real, they are known, and they are manageable with appropriate screening and oversight. They belong on the list and they deserve attention.

The same cannot be said with the same degree of confidence about their effects on the psyche. Not because those effects are necessarily more dangerous, but because they are less predictable, less well understood, and far less visible. A cardiac event announces itself. What happens to a person's sense of self after a significant psychedelic experience does not always announce itself at all. Which is exactly what makes it worth talking about.

## Spiritual Inflation

The risk I want to name is this: spiritual inflation, and the false certainty that travels with it.

Here is what it looks like. During the ceremony, the sense of self temporarily dissolves or expands or disappears into something much larger. This is, to be clear, completely normal. It is one of the known effects of these medicines and, in the right container, it is part of what makes them so powerful. A temporary dissolution of the ordinary self creates the conditions for seeing what the ordinary self usually obscures.

The problem is what happens after.

The mind, confronted with the relief of that dissolution, the sudden absence of the insecurity and striving and self-protection that consume so much ordinary energy, sometimes does something very human. It converts the relief into a new identity. The experience becomes not something that happened to you, but something that revealed who you really are. The momentary dissolving of self becomes evidence of a permanent new self. The glimpse of something universal becomes proof that you are universally significant.

Before the ceremony: driven, competitive, relentlessly self-focused. Maybe a successful entrepreneur, a deal-closer, an influencer. The engine running hot on the fuel of status and acquisition.

After the ceremony: called. Chosen. Here to carry the medicine, to serve, to heal. The engine running just as hot, on exactly the same fuel, with a completely different story attached to it.

Same ego. New costume.

This happens because of something specific about the psychedelic experience that is worth understanding. Researchers call it the noetic quality. What it means is this: insights and perceptions that occur during a psychedelic experience carry an unusual sense of certainty. They feel truer than true. More real than ordinary reality. The brain tags them with a marker that says: this is not speculation, this is not opinion, this is not interpretation. This is fact.

That marker is not always accurate. Noetic does not mean correct. It means the brain has flagged something as certain. The experience of certainty and the fact of truth are not the same thing, and in the aftermath of a ceremony they can be very difficult to tell apart.

This is what makes spiritual inflation so hard to catch from the inside. It does not feel like inflation. It feels like clarity. It feels like finally seeing what was always true. It feels, often, like the most important thing you have ever understood.

And some of it may be. That is what makes this complicated.

You touched something real. The experience was genuine. The opening was genuine. The question is what the mind does with it when it comes back online and starts looking for a story to organize around what just happened.

A genuine opening leaves you humbled. It leaves you with more questions than you arrived with, more awareness of how much you do not know, more tenderness toward the difficulty of being human. It does not leave you with a new title.

Felt-truth is not fact-truth. The integration of a genuine experience requires time and behavior to confirm it. Not the feeling of certainty.

## The Shapeshifter

The ego is a shapeshifter. That is its fundamental nature. It has been whatever the situation required for as long as you have been alive, and it is exceptionally good at its job.

In ceremony, it encountered something it could not outmaneuver. The medicine dissolved the narrative. The lights went out on the story the ego had been running. And the ego, to its credit, did not fight it. It sat there in the dark and watched.

Then the lights came back on.

And the ego, which had been in the room the entire time, which had witnessed every moment of what just happened to you, which knew perfectly well that none of it was its doing, stood up, smoothed its robes, and took a bow.

This is stolen valor in its most elegant form. The ego did not earn what happened in that ceremony. The medicine earned it. Something opened that the ego did not open. But the ego was there, and presence is enough for a shapeshifter. It watched. It took notes. And now it wears the experience like a uniform it did not earn, with full knowledge that it did not earn it, and zero shame about any of it.

That is the chimera. Real feathers. Real fire. Assembled into something that does not exist in nature and should not be trusted in the wild.

The tell is the certainty. A genuine opening leaves you humbled. The enlightened ego leaves you certain. Certain that you have been somewhere special, that you have seen what others have not seen, that you were chosen for this particular revelation at this particular time. That certainty is the ego's signature. It cannot help itself. It demands to be The One even when it sat through an experience that showed, definitively, that there is no The One.

It knows this. It was there.

It does not care.

This creature has a name. And it shows up everywhere.
