# My Sister

*Working Draft | Healing with Psychedelics: A Physician's Perspective*

My family of origin had its challenges.

My sister and I each found our own way to survive them. The choices we made were different enough that we have had almost no relationship for the past twenty to thirty years. I have spoken to her maybe twice in that stretch. I saw her once.

Then both of my parents died, and my father, in his wisdom, decided to make us co-managers of their estate.

This pissed me off. But that was the surface.

Underneath the anger was something older and more familiar: fear. That particular clenching in the gut that I recognized immediately, not because it was new but because it was ancient. A scared little boy trying to find a way, any way, to keep this from happening. Because it was not going to be good. At all. Every signal the system was sending pointed in one direction: threat. At some level, I was at risk.

Except I wasn't.

The threat was real once. The body that was responding to it had learned that response under conditions that genuinely warranted it, during a window of time when the system was being written and the stakes were as high as they get for a child. That code had been running, below awareness, ever since. It did not know that the world had changed. It did not know that I had changed. It only knew the shape of the situation, and the shape matched something in the archive, and it fired.

This is what the firmware does. It does not check the date.

What I noticed, almost immediately after the fear had its say, was what was actually happening inside me. I was not interacting with my sister. I was interacting with a very old version of my sister: the version assembled from experiences that ended decades ago, filtered through the lens of a younger version of me who had his own unresolved business with all of it. That version of her was not updated with new data for the better part of fifty years. I had been carrying her around at a fixed age, in a fixed emotional register, with a fixed set of associated feelings, and treating that archived image as if it were a living person.

And she, of course, was doing the same thing with me.

Between the two of us, we had walked into this situation carrying versions of each other that were almost entirely fictional. The real people standing on either side of the estate paperwork were, in the most honest sense, strangers. We did not know each other. We had not known each other for a very long time.

Here is the thing about a situation that looks like family and is loaded with decades of accumulated history: it is only as threatening as you decide to treat it. All of the old dynamics, the old wounds, the old patterns of relating: they were available. The pull toward them was real. I felt it. The system recognized the shape of the situation and immediately began reaching for the familiar responses, the ones that had been packaged and filed under this exact category of experience.

I recognized the pull early. That is the only thing the work actually gives you: not immunity, but recognition. The ability to see what is happening before you are already inside it.

And I made a choice.

I chose to approach my sister and the process for what they actually were: two adults who do not know each other, trying to sort through a genuinely bizarre situation under circumstances neither of us would have chosen. Not enemies. Not family in the loaded sense. Two strangers with a shared history they had both survived, standing in the same room for practical reasons, capable of treating each other with basic decency if they chose to.

We chose to.

It was not warm. It was not a reconciliation. I am not going to tell you we found each other again after all those years, because that is not what happened and this is not that kind of story. What happened was functional and respectful and, given everything, kind of remarkable.

The old version of the story would have made the estate process a war. It would have been easy. All the raw material was there.

We chose a different story.

That choice was available to me because of work I had done long before my parents died, work that had nothing to do with my sister and everything to do with learning to see my own autopilot before it took the wheel. The ceremony does not prepare you for specific situations. It prepares you to recognize the moment when the system is reaching for the old response, and to understand, in that moment, that reaching for it is a choice.

You always had the choice. That is the point. You just were not always awake enough to know it was yours to make.
