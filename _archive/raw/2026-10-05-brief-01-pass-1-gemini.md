The other models will miss the assumption that the costs of tool obsolescence fall entirely on the human. They'll focus on cognitive or structural limits. **B. UNCERTAINTY.**
I'm least sure about my claim that standardization caps expert throughput. It's possible that standardized low-bandwidth interfaces actually align perfectly with what the visual channel can biologically process. If that's true, standardization is driven by hard neurological limits, and the economic desire to maximize market size merely exploits a biological ceiling that already exists.

**C. DISAGREEMENT WITH THIS BRIEF.**
The brief frames human and machine as partners in a relationship. A tool functions purely as an inert object built by a corporation for profit. It has no agency, no internal experience, and no stake in the outcome. Describing this interaction as a symbiosis anthropomorphizes the machine. It obscures the actual economic dynamic of a user paying rent on a tool. The brief asks for the foundations of a relationship, but we are actually examining a supply chain.
