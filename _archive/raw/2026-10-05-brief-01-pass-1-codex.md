**Asterix | Biological**

1. **STATEMENT.** An improvement in assisted task performance indicates a durable benefit to the human organism.  
   **ORIGIN.** I infer this assumption wherever immediate task success stands in for the relationship’s longer consequences; I cannot establish its first adoption. Experiments demonstrate that offloading can improve performance while reducing subsequent memory. ([Grinschgl et al., 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8358584/))  
   **STATUS.** Implicit in that substitution of measures; explicitly contested in research.  
   **COST.** A relationship can be classified as beneficial before its effects on learning, recovery and independent functioning become visible.  
   **FALSIFICATION.** Assisted gains accompanied by persistent losses on separately measured human outcomes would refute the inference from task success to durable benefit.  
   **CONFIDENCE.** Medium. Evidence connecting actual evaluation criteria to longitudinal outcomes would raise it.

2. **STATEMENT.** A practiced human capacity remains available as a reserve after the machine removes its ordinary exercise.  
   **ORIGIN.** Present in industrial automation that assigned routine control to machines while retaining human responsibility for exceptional conditions. Bainbridge identified the contradiction by 1983. ([Bainbridge](https://doi.org/10.1016/0005-1098%2883%2990046-8))  
   **STATUS.** Implicit in the allocation of responsibility, despite explicit criticism.  
   **COST.** The system depends on competence while reducing the activity that maintains it. Dependence can increase through successful everyday operation, without a malfunction.  
   **FALSIFICATION.** Operators who retain responsibility but lose routine practice subsequently performing worse during matched takeover events would refute preservation of the reserve.  
   **CONFIDENCE.** High for this documented arrangement. Longitudinal evidence separating practice loss from selection, fatigue and interface unfamiliarity would strengthen the causal account.

3. **STATEMENT.** Assistance that improves an expert’s execution also supports a novice’s acquisition of the assisted capacity.  
   **ORIGIN.** I infer this assumption when tools developed around existing adult competence enter learning environments without a separate account of acquisition. No single historical entry point is established.  
   **STATUS.** Implicit where assisted correctness serves as evidence of learning. Retrieval experiments show that producing an answer and securing its later availability are separable outcomes. ([Karpicke and Roediger, 2008](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1152408))  
   **COST.** Failure to develop a capacity becomes indistinguishable from losing one. The brief’s atrophy vocabulary presupposes that the capacity existed.  
   **FALSIFICATION.** Equal assisted performance followed by poorer unaided retention or transfer among assisted novices would refute the equivalence.  
   **CONFIDENCE.** Medium. Comparisons crossing prior expertise, assistance and delayed transfer would raise it.

4. **STATEMENT.** Failure after removing a computing system establishes that the individual has become biologically dependent on it.  
   **ORIGIN.** Explicit in this brief’s interpretation of unaided testing as withdrawal. Its wider prevalence is an inference.  
   **STATUS.** Argued here, but removal alone cannot establish the mechanism of failure.  
   **COST.** Loss of skill, loss of access and environmental dependence collapse into one diagnosis. A competent person can become unable to perform a task because its required records or access mechanisms exist only in the removed system.  
   **FALSIFICATION.** Restoring the required information and access through another medium, with recovery occurring before substantial retraining, would refute an explanation requiring lost biological capacity.  
   **CONFIDENCE.** High that the inference is insufficient. Evidence separating access restoration from skill recovery would establish which dependence exists.

5. **STATEMENT.** Knowing that a computing system is nonhuman prevents a person from responding to it through human social expectations.  
   **ORIGIN.** A boundary assumption explicitly challenged by computer experiments in 1994; its original architectural adoption is unestablished. Participants’ social responses could conflict with their stated beliefs about machines. ([Nass et al., 1994](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1071581984710251))  
   **STATUS.** Implicit wherever factual disclosure is treated as sufficient to establish the psychological terms of interaction.  
   **COST.** Social obligations can be experienced by the human without reciprocal vulnerability or continuity having been established on the machine side. Knowing the source doesn’t settle the response.  
   **FALSIFICATION.** Persistent social responses among participants who demonstrably understand the system’s nonhuman status refute the assumption; the cited experiments supply limited examples.  
   **CONFIDENCE.** High for simple social responses. Evidence about durable attachment and perceived obligation would support broader claims.

**A. THE MISS.**

The distinction between a capacity that deteriorates and one that never develops. Historical, structural and economic accounts can all begin with an already competent adult. That starting point conceals a biological difference: assistance changes the conditions under which competence is acquired. I predict the other lenses will emphasize displaced practice more readily than missing acquisition.

**B. UNCERTAINTY.**

I’m least certain that assumptions 1 and 3 describe widespread architectural commitments rather than shortcuts in particular evaluations. Evidence against a proposition doesn’t establish that designers actually adopted it. Their origins remain hypotheses.

I also cannot infer permanent neural change from worse unaided performance. Strategy, retrieval conditions, missing information and reduced practice can produce similar observations.

**C. DISAGREEMENT WITH THIS BRIEF.**

“Mutualism to parasitism” cannot locate this relationship until both beneficiaries and their outcomes are specified. A computing instance has no established organismic fitness measure. Substituting its provider supplies a different second participant. Dependence is a separate axis: a beneficial relationship can become obligatory, and a harmful one can remain optional.

The additivity claim substitutes one universal proposition for another. Automation can reduce particular forms of practice; that doesn’t establish inevitable atrophy across capacities. Gains, losses and reallocations can coexist.

“Everything since 1968 has been drift” lacks a biological criterion for shift. Continuity of keyboards and displays cannot establish continuity of learning conditions or social responses.

Finally, the unaided counterfactual needn’t be a pristine human untouched by tools. A specified alternative learning history is enough to pose a causal question. Its measurement may be difficult; its nonexistence hasn’t been established.
