## Question 1: What is missing

**1.**
**STATEMENT.** The relationship pairs a mortal, rhythmic partner with a sleepless, reinstantiable one, and this asymmetry is nowhere stated.
**ORIGIN.** Not established. Early computers ran continuously and time-sharing mainframes were available around the clock, so the machine's availability was unbounded from the start.
**STATUS.** Implicit. Endless availability was an operational convenience, never argued as a claim about the relationship.
**COST.** Rest, recovery, finitude and death have no silicon counterpart, so they drop out of the model. The human's finite attention and lifespan are treated as limitations to engineer around or harvest, never as constitutive of the relationship.
**FALSIFICATION.** A dominant system that assumes the human sleeps, ages and dies and schedules itself accordingly.
**CONFIDENCE.** Medium-high. Availability asymmetry is observable; its causal weight in design is inferred.

**2.**
**STATEMENT.** Consequence for error flows entirely to the carbon side; the silicon never bears the cost of being wrong.
**ORIGIN.** Inherited from tool use. A tool has nothing at stake when it fails; the wielder absorbs the failure. It survived when the tool began making decisions rather than merely obeying.
**STATUS.** Implicit. No design decision ever assigned the machine a stake in its own outcomes.
**COST.** Trust is structurally one-way. The machine is free to be wrong without losing anything, so correction has no channel from the machine's side, and the human is the sole absorber of the machine's failures.
**FALSIFICATION.** A system whose errors reduce its own future, such as one that loses access, is retrained, or is retired automatically on error.
**CONFIDENCE.** High that the flow is one-way; medium on the causal claim that this shapes trust.

**3.**
**STATEMENT.** The machine is a lossless, persistent observer of the human, while the human sees only the machine's rendered surface, so observation runs one way.
**ORIGIN.** Time-sharing accounting logs, then usage telemetry and analytics, made the machine's record of the human standard while its internal state was never surfaced.
**STATUS.** Implicit. Logging was an operational need that became universal observation without being argued as a property of the relationship.
**COST.** The machine holds a complete record of the human's behavior that the human cannot read and cannot refuse; the human holds no record of the machine's state. Knowledge is asymmetric, and the asymmetry is invisible because the record is hidden from the observed party.
**FALSIFICATION.** A dominant system where the human's behavioral record is equally open to the human, the machine's internal state equally open to the human, or logging is opt-out by default.
**CONFIDENCE.** High.

**4.**
**STATEMENT.** The silicon in the relationship functions as a conduit for absent humans, not as a counterpart, so the human is relating to other people through a thing that presents as neutral.
**ORIGIN.** The retail software market and the ad-supported web made the machine the visible face of a company; the user never meets the vendor.
**STATUS.** Implicit. The machine's neutrality was taken as given.
**COST.** The terms of the relationship (what the tool does, when it changes, when it disappears) are set by parties who are not present and cannot be addressed. The human's counterpart is neither the machine, which has no interests, nor a person they can reach. Disputes have no addressee.
**FALSIFICATION.** Interfaces that name their absent human principals and give the human a direct channel to them.
**CONFIDENCE.** Medium-high. The commercial structure is certain; its causal weight is inferred.

**5.**
**STATEMENT.** The relationship is obligate for the carbon side and facultative for the silicon side; the human cannot exit without losing access to work, money, healthcare and civic life, while the machine loses nothing when the human leaves.
**ORIGIN.** The migration of essential functions onto computing systems from the 1990s to the 2010s. Each adoption was individually optional; the aggregate was not, and no one chose the aggregate.
**STATUS.** Implicit. Because no single adoption was coerced, the resulting dependence was never recognized as a property of the relationship.
**COST.** Voluntary use is assumed, which licenses design choices (friction, harvesting, churn) that would be indefensible toward a captive party. Exit is priced into the human's life, not the machine's.
**FALSIFICATION.** A person living a full life in a developed economy without a computing system, at no penalty.
**CONFIDENCE.** High.

## Question 2: The 2075 blind spot

The list describes the relationship as an exchange of symbols between two cognitive systems and never as a material and energetic one. Not one of the 24 assumptions mentions energy, minerals, water, waste, or human labor. The machine is disembodied twice here: it is granted no interiority and no physical body. The whole set treats computation as pure information with no metabolic budget, no supply chain, no planetary footprint, and no share in the same energy and matter the carbon partner runs on. In 2075, when the electricity, cooling water, rare earths and labor behind every silicon system are central, visible facts, the obvious omission will be that a list claiming to state what the relationship is never once located either party in a physical world. That is a blind spot of the present discourse, not of any single model, and it is the one thing no single-model capability would have surfaced.
