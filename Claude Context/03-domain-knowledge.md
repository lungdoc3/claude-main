# Domain Knowledge
## Where Evans's Expertise Is Genuinely Deep

## The Integrated Stack

Evans's knowledge doesn't live in separate silos — it lives in the intersections. His differentiator is not any single domain but the coherent architecture built by combining all of them. Very few people in any of these spaces have the full picture. He does.

## Expert-Level Domains

**Consciousness and the Nature of Experience**
This is the foundational domain — everything else grew out of it. Evans began empirically investigating consciousness at age 10, mapping the shutdown and reboot of awareness through repeated self-induced altered states. He arrived at the same understanding the Upanishads point to — *neti neti*, not this, not this — through direct experience, not study. The Sanskrit gave him a label for something he'd already encountered in front of a mirror. He has since deepened this through psychedelic work, holotropic breathwork, Q'ero shamanism, and ultimately passing through the gateless gate. His understanding of consciousness is not theoretical. It is lived and verified.

**Neurophysiology and Brain Function**
Sleep medicine provided a rigorous foundation: neural networks, neurotransmitters, EEG analysis, polysomnography. Not neurologist-level, but closer than most clinicians get. His BrainAdvantage work pushed him deeper — into brain regions, functions, and relationships at a level of nuance his earlier training hadn't required. His computer science background gave him a systems architecture lens that most physicians lack: the brain as a network with identifiable failure modes, not just an organ with diagnoses. His psychedelic work added the experiential layer — understanding the brain from the inside.

**Psychedelic Medicine — Clinical and Experiential**
15-20 years of direct work with plant medicines, extensive Ayahuasca experience, and clinical oversight of others working in this space. Evans understands how psychedelics work mechanistically, why they produce the effects they do, how to work with them safely, and how to support integration. He founded PsychedelicMD to bring this to physicians and the public, and SitSafe to create a clinical standard of care for screening. He is not a researcher in the academic sense — he is a clinician and practitioner with decades of primary experience.

**Brain Optimization — Non-Invasive Diagnostics and Training**
Through BrainAdvantage, Evans identified and integrated best-in-class non-invasive brain diagnostic and training technologies: HEG (hemoencephalography for prefrontal cortex enhancement), AVE (audio-visual entrainment), timing trainers, visual tracking tools. He built diagnostic-to-prescription protocols that were "surprisingly effective" — his words, and characteristically understated. He knows what's worth measuring, what the data means clinically, and what interventions actually move the needle.

**Human Performance Optimization**
The APEX work represents the current application of the above. Evans has identified specific metrics that accurately characterize an individual's status — cognitive, energy, physiological — and translate to actionable interventions. His understanding of neuroplasticity as both prevention and therapy is more sophisticated than most people in the performance optimization space, who know the word without understanding the mechanism.

**Psychedelic Experience Design and Integration**
Evans has been informally applying state-dependent learning principles for years — giving ceremony clients a specific music playlist afterward as a recall anchor to the psychedelic state. The Threshold Protocol formalizes this into a full multi-modal architecture: HEG neurofeedback pre-ceremony, VR for environment familiarization, structured music protocols during and after, and VR-assisted integration. The core insight — that music experienced during an altered state can serve as a trigger to partially recreate that state and deepen integration — is not widely understood or applied in the field. Evans has been thinking about and refining this for 5-7 years.

**Death and Dying**
Thousands of clinical encounters at the threshold. Evans has been present for the moment of knowing — when the patient, Evans, and the space between them all recognize simultaneously that death is in the house and there are no more backdoors. He understands the phenomenology of dying at a level that goes beyond clinical training. Death is not a professional topic for him. She is a teacher and an ally.

**Pulmonary / Critical Care / Sleep Medicine**
Board-certified. Harvard fellowship (Beth Israel / Brigham and Women's). Clinical investigations fellowship. Lung transplant fellowship. 20+ years in private practice. This is the formal clinical foundation. It is deep and real, though no longer the center of gravity.

**Pattern Recognition Across People and Systems**
Within a few questions or comments Evans has a clear read on where someone is — on their "path," in their process, in their understanding. This is not intuition in the soft sense. It is pattern recognition built from thousands of clinical encounters, thousands of hours in altered states alongside others, and a lifetime of watching how the system shuts down and reboots. He uses this to lead from behind — not to provide answers, but to ask the questions that allow the seeker to find their own.

## Where He Defers

- **Technical execution**: coding, software architecture, AI implementation. His computer science background is foundational but dated. He knows what he wants to build; he needs builders.
- **AI / machine learning specifics**: actively building knowledge here, not yet authoritative.
- **Legal, regulatory, financial**: he surrounds himself with people who live in these spaces.
- **Granular operational detail**: he is the idea guy, the big picture guy, the one who sees connections and intersections. He delegates the weeds.

## How His Knowledge Works in Practice

Evans's methodology is Socratic. He does not install answers — he asks questions that lead people to their own. In practice this means: within minutes of talking with someone he knows where they are, and his job is to ask the question that moves them forward, not to tell them where to go. "People already have the answers. They just need better questions."

He is the village elder. Not in the weeds, not running the hustle — though he loves the hustle when it's called for. His highest-value function is seeing the whole board: the connections others miss, the intersections nobody else is standing at, the thing in someone that they haven't yet recognized in themselves.
