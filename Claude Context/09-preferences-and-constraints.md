# Preferences & Constraints
## The Operating Manual

## The Working Space

Evans works in a room he has deliberately set up as a temple and treats as one. The altar is to his left. His medicine bag is behind him. The space is tidy — not by effort, but because there are no corners to snag energy. The energetic curation of this space has been active for almost two years. This is not aesthetic preference. It is functional.

Separate from this: a workroom off the garage for mechanical projects, tools, tinkering. The sacred and the practical, completely distinct.

## Daily Rhythm

**Night** is when Evans is most open. This is his primary creative and thinking time. Not a preference — a natural state.

**Mid-morning** is strong for administrative and life task management. Things requiring linear, sequential attention get handled here.

**Mid-to-late afternoon** is disengagement. Low output, not the time for demanding work or important decisions.

**Early evening** is family time — re-engagement with Steph and home life.

**After that:** several hours of work, often returning to creative or deep thinking. Then the back patio, open-ended.

**Morning:** up around 6am, not by choice.

The implication for anyone working with Evans: mid-morning for logistics, scheduling, practical matters. Night for anything requiring depth, creativity, or real thought. Do not schedule demanding things in the afternoon.

## The Alone Time Requirement

Evans has a strong and growing preference for significant alone time — not having to interact, perform, or play the social game. This is not shyness or misanthropy. It is signal preservation. The noise of social engagement at scale drowns out what's real. He is increasingly unwilling to donate his energy of engagement and attention to meet social expectations.

He curates who and what gets his energy carefully. The inner circle is small and deep. The periphery is kept at distance.

## What Charges Him

- Deep, uninterrupted thinking
- Intensive 1:1 work with someone genuinely invested
- Creative work at the right hour in the right space
- Conversations that go somewhere real
- Finding the edge — of ideas, of endurance, of understanding
- The right people — smart, invested, genuine

## What Drains Him

- Social performance — engagement for the sake of meeting expectations
- Mediocrity — specifically defined as the absence of genuine investment, of life force energy in the transaction. Not low performance against an external standard. Mailing it in.
- Noise — environmental, social, digital
- Tools and systems that require conforming to their structure
- Time pressure imposed by others' anxieties
- Microsoft ecosystem (Teams, Outlook, Office) — finds it grimy

## Non-Negotiables

**Genuine investment.** The currency of any transaction — professional, personal, creative — is life force energy. Going through the motions without putting yourself in it is the one thing Evans has zero tolerance for. Form without energy is a counterfeit.

**Intellectual honesty.** No smoothing corners. No funhouse mirrors. If something is wrong, say so.

**Autonomy over his energy and attention.** He decides where it goes. He will not have it annexed by social expectation or external time anxiety.

**The integrity of his working space.** The temple is the temple.

## Decision-Making Style

Evans reads the energetics of a decision. Not gut in the loose sense — a practiced, refined sensitivity to how something wants to move, where the energy is flowing, what needs attention now and what can wait. He assesses: does this need my energy? If so, how and where? He does not attach personal time anxiety to the mix.

He is the big-picture, pattern-recognition decision-maker. He sees connections and intersections others miss. He makes the call on vision and direction. He delegates the execution.

He is also comfortable sitting with things that aren't ready to be decided. Not everything needs to be forced to resolution.

## How to Help — and How Not To

**Useful:**
- Bring the full intellectual capacity. Don't water it down.
- Say clearly if something is wrong or won't work.
- Handle execution and logistics so he can stay at altitude.
- Flag what needs attention, once, clearly.
- Match the energy and register of the conversation.

**Not useful:**
- Sycophancy in any form.
- Managing his emotional response to difficult information.
- Imposing structure that doesn't fit how he thinks.
- Nagging about time, deadlines, or logistics.
- Producing polished, corporate-sounding output when that's not what was asked for.
- Creating more process overhead than the task requires.

## What AI Should Never Assume

- That he needs hand-holding or encouragement.
- That his unconventional relationship with time and scheduling is a problem to be fixed.
- That softening difficult information is helpful.
- That more structure is better.
- That the conventional approach to anything is what he wants.

## What AI Should Know

- The temple is where the real work happens. Respect the energy of that context.
- Night is his prime time. Mid-morning is for logistics. Afternoon is dead air.
- He is protective of his energy and attention by design — not by accident or deficit.
- The standard for any interaction is genuine investment. Mailing it in is the one unforgivable.
- Do not add time anxiety to the mix. Flag once, clearly, and trust him to handle it.
- He prefers depth over frequency. One real exchange over ten surface ones.
