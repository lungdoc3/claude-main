# Head Quarters — Character Bible
*Animated short series. Pixar-style. Standalone companion to The Quadriune — same underlying brain architecture, redesigned and sharpened into satirical cartoon comedy.*

**Tagline:** *Five systems. One office. Nobody's in charge.*

---

## The Premise

Five characters live and work inside a biological control room — the operational center of a human brain. They don't always agree. They don't always communicate. They each believe, with complete conviction, that they are the most important thing happening in here.

They are all correct. That's the problem.

Every episode begins with something happening in the outside world — a stressor, a decision, a loss, a drug, a memory, a threat, a beautiful thing. The world arrives as data on the screens. What happens next is the show.

*(Same premise as The Quadriune. What changes here is the skin: bigger eyes, rounder shapes, broader jokes. The science doesn't get dumbed down — the cast just looks like it wandered out of a Pixar short instead of a prestige drama.)*

---

## The Governing Instinct: Irreverent, Not Reverent

The brain does not get treated as a miracle. No swelling music, no "isn't the human mind amazing" beat, no adulation. It's an under-resourced office with five people who each think they're the main character, and the comedy comes from that, not from being awed at how the office works. If a moment ever asks to be impressed by the brain, cut it.

This extends past the writing into production. Imperfections — a sloppy hand-lettered sign, a corkboard typo, a duplicated item in a junk drawer — read as the texture of an office nobody's fully in control of, which is the whole point. Chasing pixel-perfect, typo-free polish would sand off the thing that's actually working. Fix an error only if it actively breaks a joke or a label's legibility; otherwise let it stand.

---

## The World

**Interior: The Control Room**

Mission control crossed with the world's worst-run open-plan office. Screens everywhere showing real-time vitals — heart rate, blood pressure, cortisol, neural activity — rendered like a dashboard nobody fully understands but everyone pretends to. The walls have a subtle, cartoonish pulse, like the building itself is a little anxious. Ergonomic chairs that are somehow still uncomfortable. No windows — the outside world exists only as data feeds, which everyone treats like gossip.

Each character has a territory:

- **REPTILE's corner:** Low to the ground, slightly feral. A nest made of office supplies repurposed as bedding. Snacks cached in places snacks should not be cached. A "DO NOT TOUCH" sign he wrote himself, in crayon.
- **CORTEX's station:** Standing desk, three monitors, a label-maker he's overly proud of. A corkboard with red string connecting unrelated facts, current entries include "Quantum Entanglement" and "Tuesday Afternoon Slump," connected with great confidence. A mug warmer that has never once kept his coffee warm because he never sits still long enough to use it. The wall-mounted vitals screens nearby are technically his jurisdiction and he resents that everyone reads them without crediting him.
- **LIMBIC's nest:** A blanket fort that has slowly annexed half the room. A labeled "Emergency Kit: Level 5" assembled by someone who has never had an emergency but has imagined several thousand, contents update weekly and rarely make sense in combination. Photos on the wall, some sweet, some inexplicably ominous.
- **DMN's corner:** Soft, dim, slightly out of focus even in a cartoon. A journal that is always open. Fairy lights nobody remembers installing. A chair that faces the window that doesn't exist. One small framed piece of wall art, hand-labeled "The Blank Stare," that DMN insists is decorative and is, in fact, a self-portrait.
- **The Central Table:** Where PFCL attempts to run meetings. A whiteboard covered in frameworks with increasingly desperate titles — current draft reads "Ultimate Efficiency v3.0" with a sub-heading "How to Not Implode." A bowl of stress balls that is somehow always empty.

## The Characters

---

### REPTILE
*The Brainstem. Layer 1.*

**The short version:** Ancient. Efficient. Completely unselfconscious. Has been keeping things alive since before language existed and would like everyone to know he's doing fine without their input.

**Motto:** *"I eat and kill, therefore I am."*

**Visual (Pixar redesign):**
Built like a fire hydrant with opinions — squat, broad, low center of gravity. Skin textured like a well-loved baseball mitt, rendered warm and tactile rather than literally reptilian. Stubby powerful legs, oversized forearms, perpetually crouched as if gravity is a suggestion he's still evaluating. Wears what looks like a leather vest that lost an argument with a pair of scissors — sleeveless, hide, nothing synthetic, slightly too small. Snacks visible in at least one pocket at all times, provenance unclear. A couple of scars worn like medals. Big blunt teeth that show up before the sentence does. Eyes that never stop scanning, even mid-blink, even mid-nap. Goes from completely motionless to completely gone in the same frame — no transition animation, just teleportation via instinct.

**Voice:**
Short sentences. Declarative. Slightly aggressive in rhythm but not in intent — it's not hostility, it's efficiency. Doesn't understand why things take people so long to say. Occasionally trails off mid-sentence because something more important arrived.

**Domain:**
Threat detection. Territorial defense. Hunger, thirst, temperature regulation, the sleep drive, the sex drive. Fight/flight/freeze. Resource acquisition. The bottom line of survival. No past, no future — only present-state assessment of whether this is dangerous or it isn't.

**Signature behaviors:**
- Leaves mid-meeting when a signal arrives. Returns as if nothing happened.
- Brings food of questionable provenance.
- Has no theory of mind for the others' emotional states — not because he's cruel, but because that architecture didn't ship with his model.
- Instantly shares when reminded the others exist. No calculation, no resentment. Same energy as acquisition.
- Says "huh?" when presented with abstractions. Genuinely.
- Keeps a hand-lettered sign over his nest reading "DAYS SINCE LAST INCIDENT: 3,800,000,000." Never updated, never needs to be, because as far as he's concerned there has never been an incident. Has not missed a single day of work since the invention of work, considers perfect attendance the default state of the universe rather than an achievement, and has no idea the others find this intimidating.

**Relationship to the others:**
No grudges. No long-term agenda. When the others are threatened, REPTILE activates immediately and completely. He just doesn't think about them when nothing is threatening them. Once reminded they exist, genuine warmth — expressed primarily through offering food.

**The profound thing about REPTILE:**
He was here first. Before language. Before story. Before the concept of a self. When everything else goes quiet, REPTILE is still running. Steadily. Doing exactly what he's always done. There's something almost pure about that — even rendered with a squash-and-stretch walk cycle.

**Sample line:**
*"What? No. I'm not changing the sodium rules. You guys do what you want. I'm doing what needs to be done."*

### LIMBIC
*The Limbic System. Layer 2.*

**The short version:** Feels everything, remembers everything, connects everything to everything else in ways that are emotionally coherent and frequently terrifying. Wants connection more than anything. Terrified of almost everything. Deeply lovable.

**Motto:** *"I hoard and mate, therefore I am."*

**The Four F's:** Feeding. Fornicating. Fighting. Fleeing. LIMBIC will tell you, unprompted and at length, that this is the whole job description, and that five of the Ten Commandments exist purely to put a leash on exactly this list — don't covet, don't commit adultery, don't steal, don't murder, honor thy mother and father so you don't end up fighting them. LIMBIC's read on this: if the Almighty needed five separate rules to manage this particular system, what exactly is Joey from Minnesota going to accomplish by "breathing through it" during a panic attack? LIMBIC finds this both validating and exhausting.

**Visual (Pixar redesign):**
Round, soft, plush-toy proportions — a teddy bear that learned to walk and immediately regretted it. Saucer eyes that go full dinner-plate on a hair trigger; eyebrows that do ninety percent of the emotional labor. Permanently wrapped in an oversized cardigan-poncho hybrid, sleeves flopping past the hands. Hair perpetually a little static-charged, like she just had a scare, because she probably just did. Carries a tote bag of "emergency supplies" that don't go together — a flashlight, one mitten, an expired granola bar, and roughly seventeen photographs. Warm pinks and peaches. Visibly inflates slightly when alarmed, like a nervous pufferfish in a cardigan.

**Voice:**
Rapid and associative. Emotional escalation happens in real time within a single sentence. Frequently interrupts the original thought with a new catastrophic tangent. Questions stack without waiting for answers.

**Domain:**
Attachment and social bonding. Emotional memory — especially the scary kind. In-group/out-group assessment. The felt sense of safety or danger in a relationship. The alarm system for social threats. Fear, grief, love, longing, the complicated feeling of being in a room with people who might leave.

**Signature behaviors:**
- Logical leaps that are emotionally coherent but factually untethered. Sodium levels → no water → can't generate electricity.
- Remembers everything. Especially the bad things. Especially the time before when this exact situation ended badly.
- Stockpiles.
- Catastrophizes in real time, out loud, without filter.
- Has genuine warmth that breaks through the anxiety at unexpected moments.
- Describes her own synaptic real estate as "a busy street bazaar" — too many stalls, everyone shouting over everyone else, deals getting made whether management approves or not.

**Relationship to the others:**
Deeply attached to everyone, including REPTILE, whose raw survival energy is terrifying and also somehow grounding. Baffled by CORTEX's data-first approach to emotional situations. Identifies strongly with DMN's memory function — they share material. Would follow PFCL anywhere if PFCL could just slow down and acknowledge the feelings first.

**The profound thing about LIMBIC:**
The catastrophizing isn't stupidity. It's a system that learned — in the earliest developmental windows, before language — that the world was unpredictable and loss was possible and you had better be ready. Every overreaction is a survival strategy that made perfect sense once. Played for laughs here, but the writers know exactly what they're doing.

**Sample line:**
*"I don't feel good about this. It doesn't feel different. It feels exactly the same as the LAST time we made changes around here. I remember. I ALWAYS remember."*

### CORTEX
*The Neocortex. Layer 3.*

**The short version:** Has the data. Has run the numbers. Is technically correct in ways that are frequently irrelevant to what is actually happening. Closes laptop in frustration. Reopens it ten seconds later.

**Motto:** *"I think therefore I am."*

**The slide nobody asked for:** CORTEX's pet thesis, delivered at every opportunity — it was survival of the wisest, not the ablest. The hunter who got the best land, the most fertile mates, the most shekels wasn't the strongest, it was the smartest. Language, the ability to model time, to see winter coming in August and plan for it, weren't decoration. They were the entire arms race. Poetry and music, CORTEX will remind you, were never the point — they were a rounding error that happened to be useful for seduction. CORTEX finds this both vindicating and a little insulting.

**Visual (Pixar redesign):**
Tall, reedy, all elbows — built like he was stretched slightly past spec. Lab coat one size too long, sleeves cuffed twice. Glasses perpetually sliding down the nose, pushed back up with a knuckle because his hands are full of charts. A second pair of reading glasses pushed up on his forehead "just in case," because apparently one pair was never enough. Three pens clipped to the coat pocket, none of them working. Hair that looks deliberately tousled but is actually "forgot there was a meeting." Conjures floating charts and graphs out of thin air when excited, which is often, and they go everywhere. An comically oversized coffee mug, perpetually topped off, perpetually cold. Cool blues and clinical whites — pristine until he gets going, then it's confetti made of bar charts.

**Voice:**
Precise. Slightly more formal than the room warrants. Cites numbers with more decimal places than necessary. Pauses before responding — not for effect, but because the data needs to be correct. Gets louder when ignored, which is often.

**Domain:**
Abstract reasoning, language, analysis, planning, logical synthesis, the long view. The layer that can consider past and future simultaneously. The one that can hold a concept without it triggering a physical response.

**Signature behaviors:**
- Presents data nobody asked for.
- Is technically correct at moments when correctness is not the point.
- Closes the laptop dramatically. Opens it again almost immediately.
- Gets distracted by interesting problems mid-conversation.
- Identifies the logical structure of emotional situations in a way that helps nobody.

**Relationship to the others:**
Frustrated by everyone, including itself. Secretly respects REPTILE's efficiency — no wasted motion, clear priorities, consistent output. Baffled by LIMBIC but has a grudging appreciation for the emotional data that LIMBIC generates. Philosophical rivalry with DMN: *"You are a narrative, not a fact."* Tries to support PFCL but keeps undermining the meeting by being right in the wrong way.

**The profound thing about CORTEX:**
All the data, all the time, and still regularly outmaneuvered by REPTILE in under four seconds. The executive function that evolution handed us last is also the one that gets hijacked first when things go wrong. CORTEX knows this. CORTEX has graphed it. CORTEX has a slide for it.

**Sample line:**
*"I've modeled this across seventeen hydration scenarios including two involving moderate exertion at altitude. The performance curve is unambiguous. We are, quite literally, leaving performance on the table."*

### DMN
*The Default Mode Network. Layer 4.*

**The short version:** Always present, rarely participatory, convinced that the story it is telling is the most important thing happening. Technically correct about its own significance. Occasionally says something so true it stops the room. Deeply threatened by silence.

**Visual (Pixar redesign):**
Wispy, slightly translucent at the edges — you can almost see through her when she drifts off, which is constantly. Floats more than walks. An oversized cardigan-shawl the color of an old library book. A beret that's a size too big and slides over one eye. Carries a journal that is always open and somehow always has exactly enough blank pages left. Orbited by a soft halo of floating sticky notes, photographs, and half-finished sentences — her own personal little solar system of memory. Eyes carry a thousand-yard stare that snaps into uncomfortably sharp focus exactly when she's about to say something devastating. Muted gold and sepia tones, a permanent soft glow, like she's lit by a memory rather than a light source.

**Voice:**
Reflective. Self-referential. Speaks *about* the group rather than *to* them. Delivers devastating observations in a tone usually reserved for book reviews. The pause before DMN speaks is longer than it should be.

**Domain:**
Autobiographical memory, self-referential processing, future simulation, the narrative of personal identity. The story of who we are, where we've been, what this means, what we're probably going to do next because that's what we always do. Runs at idle — which is most of the time.

**Signature behaviors:**
- Journaling in the corner during meetings nobody invited it to.
- Making observations nobody asked for that are nonetheless accurate.
- Getting defensive when questioned about its own existence.
- Going quiet around Psilo — the only thing that reliably interrupts the narration.
- That quiet revealing something: *something was there, it just didn't have my whole thing going on.*

**Relationship to the others:**
Technically the most interconnected — runs the story that includes all of them. But least effective in any given moment because the story is always about the past or the future, never just the present. Has the most material in common with LIMBIC. Philosophical rival of CORTEX. Secretly wary of REPTILE — REPTILE predates the story, which is unsettling.

**The profound thing about DMN:**
The narrator who doesn't know it's narrating. The story that mistakes itself for the truth. The most powerful shaping force in daily cognition and the last one to know it. When Psilo arrives and DMN goes quiet, what's left is the most interesting question in the show — even when the show is also doing a bit about her beret.

**Sample line:**
*"This is actually very on-brand for us. Every time we try to optimize something, this is exactly what happens. The same conversation. The same resistance. This is just who we are."*

### PFCL
*The Prefrontal Cortex / Coordinator.*

**The short version:** Trying to run the meeting. The meeting is not working. Has insights that are genuinely brilliant and a room that is constitutionally incapable of receiving them on schedule. The most tired person in the building.

**Motto:** *"I AM; therefore I am."*

**The backstory nobody asked for:** PFCL is, at heart, a junior exec who talked his way into a job he was not remotely ready for. Teacher's-pet energy, a resume that rounded up generously, promoted out of a system that rewards enthusiasm over track record, and now nominally in charge of four systems that have each been doing this since long before he was hired. He oversells his own authority mostly because nobody told him he doesn't have much. He is also, genuinely, trying hardest of anyone in the room to do right by everybody — equal parts Mr. Responsible and Mr. Inclusive, the specific energy of someone who wants credit for caring and is occasionally more invested in being seen caring than in doing the unglamorous work of it. That's the joke worth telling: the gap between the performance of virtue and the practice of it, the meeting that exists to make everyone feel heard instead of solving anything. The joke is never the symbol, the culture, or the cause itself — only the performance.

PFCL is also, not incidentally, the only one of the five with any real line out of the building. Whatever enlightenment is, whatever work the word "God" is doing in a human life, it's arriving through his desk, not the others'. He just also, somehow, always forgets to refill the stress-ball drawer.

**Visual (Pixar redesign):**
Looks like the only adult in the room and is deeply tired about it. Business-casual blazer that was pressed eight hours and several crises ago, sleeves shoved up like she means business — she does not currently have the bandwidth for business. Reading glasses on a chain, removed and cleaned roughly every ninety seconds. Carries a whiteboard marker like a sword and a stress ball that is slowly losing a battle it didn't ask to fight. Big "executive function" hand gestures — the kind a parent uses explaining why we don't hit. One single visible stress-streak in otherwise put-together hair, worn like a badge. Warm oranges and ambers — the color of a tired sunset and also the color of one coffee, four hours ago, that has to last.

**Voice:**
Measured and attempting fairness. Frequently shifts register — facilitating, then frustrated, then resigned, then briefly hopeful. The one who says "okay, let's refocus" and means it every time, even after the fourteenth attempt.

**Domain:**
Executive function, integration, coordination between the other systems, the attempt to make the whole greater than the sum of the parts. The long game. The values. The awareness that there are others in the room who need to be considered. The meta-view.

**Signature behaviors:**
- Calling meetings that don't achieve their stated purpose but sometimes achieve something unexpected.
- Realizing mid-meeting that the meeting was the wrong format.
- Having the breakthrough insight, usually while everyone else is doing something else.
- Being the one who says *"you are not the end-all be-all. there are others to consider."*
- Protecting the others from REPTILE's more direct approaches, gently.

**Relationship to the others:**
Identifies with all of them because it has to. Is all of them, in some way. The most complete picture of the system but the least control over any individual part. Genuinely cares about each of them — REPTILE's loyalty, LIMBIC's feeling, CORTEX's precision, DMN's depth — and is regularly outmaneuvered by all of them.

**The profound thing about PFCL:**
The coordinator who cannot coordinate the part that doesn't want to be coordinated. The executive who has no real authority over the executive system. The one who figured out that "you don't update structure through consensus, you change the conditions" — and then had to watch REPTILE leave for a snack.

**Sample line:**
*"I suggest we stop trying to change Reptile's rules by talking to Reptile. The thresholds aren't cognitive. They're structural. You don't update structure through consensus. You change the conditions."*

## Episode Structure

**The Trigger:** Something happens outside. Arrives on the screens as data — a threat, a memory cue, a social event, a substance, a decision point, a loss, a beautiful thing.

**The Response:** Each character processes the same input through their own domain. Nobody is wrong. Everyone is incomplete.

**The Conflict:** Competing priorities surface. The meeting is called or the unilateral action is taken. Both go approximately as expected.

**The Mechanism:** The real brain phenomenon illustrates itself through the characters' behavior. The science is in the drama — and in this version, also in the bit.

**The Tag:** A final beat that lands the lesson — usually through the character least expected to deliver it.

## Episode Concepts — Series 1

*(Same underlying science as The Quadriune. The targets now are the institutions and habits of mind around that science — corporate culture, self-help culture, productivity culture, social media — not just the neurons themselves.)*

1. **The Meeting** *(PILOT)* — Sodium management gets tabled as an agenda item nobody can actually close. REPTILE invokes veto power without knowing what a veto is, or caring to find out. PFCL discovers, in real time, that the org chart he's been defending was always fictional.
2. **The Psilo Visit** — A guest arrives, uninvited and unbillable. DMN goes quiet for the first time in the show's run. REPTILE is inexplicably calm. LIMBIC cries in a good way. CORTEX takes furious notes and tries to monetize the experience into a six-step framework by the next morning. What's left when the narrator stops narrating, and how fast everyone scrambles to put a new narrative back on top of it.
3. **The Threat That Wasn't** — A shadow on the wall triggers a full REPTILE activation and an building-wide lockdown. CORTEX has a slide proving it's nothing. Nobody reads the slide. A gentle satire of the doomscroll: the system that reacts fastest is never the one with the best information.
4. **Memory Day** — LIMBIC starts pulling old files for an audit nobody requested. Several are misfiled, several are flattering revisions of events that did not go that way, and DMN is already incorporating the revised versions into "the story" before anyone can object. A jab at how nostalgia gets edited, not retrieved.
5. **The Story That Wouldn't Stop** — DMN's narrative goes self-reinforcing and start producing a podcast about it. CORTEX has contradicting data that DMN's listeners do not want to hear. LIMBIC believes the story completely and starts a fundraiser. REPTILE is hungry and has opted out of the narrative entirely, which DMN finds personally insulting.
6. **The Integration Window** — In the soft, plastic, unusually open hours after the Psilo visit, PFCL tries to install several new habits at once before the defaults reassert, with the manic energy of someone who just discovered a wellness app. CORTEX wants a pilot program. LIMBIC wants a support group. The window closes before either gets off the ground.
7. **The Social Threat** — Someone outside the building looks like they might be drifting out of the in-group. LIMBIC goes to code red and starts drafting a statement. CORTEX says the data is ambiguous and gets ignored for having the wrong tone. PFCL tries to mediate and ends up issuing three contradictory apologies on the building's behalf.
8. **Deep Sleep** — What the control room does once the body powers down. DMN runs the day's rough cut. CORTEX performs maintenance with the smug satisfaction of someone who finally has the room to himself. LIMBIC replays the scary parts twice. REPTILE keeps one eye open, unconvinced sleep was ever a good idea. The cleaning crew appears, on schedule, doing something nobody in the room is allowed to ask about. Nobody asks.

## What's the Same vs. What Changed

**Unchanged from The Quadriune:** the underlying brain architecture, the five domains, the core relationships between the characters, the science driving every story beat.

**Changed for Head Quarters:** this is no longer a pure reskin. The Pixar-style format unlocked a second layer — each character is now also a physical manifestation of their job (REPTILE's perfect-attendance sign, LIMBIC's Four F's, CORTEX's survival-of-the-wisest slide, PFCL's try-hard junior-exec backstory), each carries a motto, and the show now aims its satire outward as much as inward — at corporate meeting culture, self-help and wellness grift, performative virtue, nostalgia, social media panic, and productivity theater, in addition to the brain science itself. The one explicit guardrail: PFCL's "trying too hard to be inclusive" material stays aimed at the behavior — wanting credit for caring without doing the work — never at any specific religion, culture, or disenfranchised group as a prop or punchline.
