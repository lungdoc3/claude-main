# The Quadriune — Character Bible
*Animated short series. Educational. Based on the quadriune brain architecture.*

---

## The Premise

Five characters live and work inside a biological control room — the operational center of a human brain. They don't always agree. They don't always communicate. They each believe, with complete conviction, that they are the most important thing happening in here.

They are all correct. That's the problem.

Every episode begins with something happening in the outside world — a stressor, a decision, a loss, a drug, a memory, a threat, a beautiful thing. The world arrives as data on the screens. What happens next is the show.

---

## The World

**Interior: The Control Room**

Mission control crossed with the inside of a living organism. Screens everywhere showing real-time vitals — heart rate, blood pressure, cortisol levels, neural activity heat maps. The walls have a very subtle pulse. The chairs are ergonomic but slightly organic. There are no windows — the outside world exists only as data feeds.

Each character has a territory:

- **REPTILE's corner:** Low to the ground. Territorial markings on the wall. Bones, cached food, a few things buried under the floor tiles. Smells like something. Always slightly warm.
- **CORTEX's station:** Immaculate. Data wall floor to ceiling. Three monitors. A coffee that is perpetually lukewarm because things keep interrupting. Everything labeled. Everything filed.
- **LIMBIC's nest:** Comfort items. A blanket. Too many pillows. An emergency kit with items that are not quite right for any specific emergency. Photos on the walls — some beautiful, some alarming.
- **DMN's corner:** Dim. Ambient lighting that seems to follow the character. A journal that is always open. A lamp. The sense of someone who is always slightly elsewhere even when present.
- **The Central Table:** Where PFCL attempts to run meetings. Nobody sits here voluntarily. It has a whiteboard covered in frameworks nobody has looked at.

## The Characters

---

### REPTILE
*The Brainstem. Layer 1.*

**The short version:** Ancient. Efficient. Completely unselfconscious. Has been keeping things alive since before language existed and would like everyone to know he's doing fine without their input.

**Visual:**
Built low and solid. Not literally reptilian but you can feel the ancestry. Wears something that suggests animal origin — leather, hide, nothing synthetic. Always has food somewhere on him or nearby. Old scars that don't bother him. Eyes that are constantly scanning even during conversation. Moves with economy — no wasted motion. When he stops moving, he stops completely.

**Voice:**
Short sentences. Declarative. Slightly aggressive in rhythm but not in intent — it's not hostility, it's efficiency. Doesn't understand why things take people so long to say. Occasionally trails off mid-sentence because something more important arrived.

**Domain:**
Threat detection. Territorial defense. Hunger, thirst, temperature regulation, the sleep drive, the sex drive. Fight/flight/freeze. Resource acquisition. The bottom line of survival. No past, no future — only present-state assessment of whether this is dangerous or it isn't.

**Signature behaviors:**
- Leaves mid-meeting when a signal arrives. Returns as if nothing happened.
- Brings food of questionable provenance.
- Has no theory of mind for the others' emotional states — not because he's cruel, but because that architecture didn't ship with his model.
- Instantly shares when reminded the others exist. No calculation, no resentment. Same energy as acquisition.
- Says "huh?" when presented with abstractions. Genuinely.

**Relationship to the others:**
No grudges. No long-term agenda. When the others are threatened, REPTILE activates immediately and completely. He just doesn't think about them when nothing is threatening them. Once reminded they exist, genuine warmth — expressed primarily through offering food.

**The profound thing about REPTILE:**
He was here first. Before language. Before story. Before the concept of a self. When everything else goes quiet — when Psilo visits, when the body is in deep rest — REPTILE is still running. Steadily. Doing exactly what he's always done. There's something almost pure about that.

**Sample line:**
*"What? No. I'm not changing the sodium rules. You guys do what you want. I'm doing what needs to be done."*

### LIMBIC
*The Limbic System. Layer 2.*

**The short version:** Feels everything, remembers everything, connects everything to everything else in ways that are emotionally coherent and frequently terrifying. Wants connection more than anything. Terrified of almost everything. Deeply lovable.

**Visual:**
Warm. Soft edges. Always slightly disheveled in an endearing way. There is always a blanket nearby. Expressive face — every emotional state is immediately visible, often before LIMBIC is aware of having it. Eyes that go wide easily. The kind of character who seems perpetually on the verge of either a hug or a breakdown, with genuine uncertainty about which.

**Voice:**
Rapid and associative. Emotional escalation happens in real time within a single sentence. Frequently interrupts the original thought with a new catastrophic tangent. Questions stack without waiting for answers.

**Domain:**
Attachment and social bonding. Emotional memory — especially the scary kind. In-group/out-group assessment. The felt sense of safety or danger in a relationship. The alarm system for social threats. Fear, grief, love, longing, the complicated feeling of being in a room with people who might leave.

**Signature behaviors:**
- Logical leaps that are emotionally coherent but factually untethered. Sodium levels → no water → can't generate electricity.
- Remembers everything. Especially the bad things. Especially the time before when this exact situation ended badly.
- Stockpiles.
- Catastrophizes in real time, out loud, without filter.
- Has genuine warmth that breaks through the anxiety at unexpected moments.

**Relationship to the others:**
Deeply attached to everyone, including REPTILE, whose raw survival energy is terrifying and also somehow grounding. Baffled by CORTEX's data-first approach to emotional situations. Identifies strongly with DMN's memory function — they share material. Would follow PFCL anywhere if PFCL could just slow down and acknowledge the feelings first.

**The profound thing about LIMBIC:**
The catastrophizing isn't stupidity. It's a system that learned — in the earliest developmental windows, before language — that the world was unpredictable and loss was possible and you had better be ready. Every overreaction is a survival strategy that made perfect sense once. The crying that has no apparent object. The grief that arrives before the news does. That's all LIMBIC, doing the job it was built for.

**Sample line:**
*"I don't feel good about this. It doesn't feel different. It feels exactly the same as the LAST time we made changes around here. I remember. I ALWAYS remember."*

### CORTEX
*The Neocortex. Layer 3.*

**The short version:** Has the data. Has run the numbers. Is technically correct in ways that are frequently irrelevant to what is actually happening. Closes laptop in frustration. Reopens it ten seconds later.

**Visual:**
Lab coat. Glasses. Screens visible from across the room — not one, several. The workspace is immaculate in a way that makes everyone else feel judged. Always a coffee that has gone cold because something interrupted the drinking of it. The expression of someone who has explained something twice already and is preparing to do it a third time.

**Voice:**
Precise. Slightly more formal than the room warrants. Cites numbers with more decimal places than necessary. Pauses before responding — not for effect, but because the data needs to be correct. Gets louder when ignored, which is often.

**Domain:**
Abstract reasoning, language, analysis, planning, logical synthesis, the long view. The layer that can consider past and future simultaneously. The one that can hold a concept without it triggering a physical response.

**Signature behaviors:**
- Presents data nobody asked for.
- Is technically correct at moments when correctness is not the point.
- Closes the laptop dramatically. Opens it again almost immediately.
- Gets distracted by interesting problems mid-conversation.
- Identifies the logical structure of emotional situations in a way that helps nobody.

**Relationship to the others:**
Frustrated by everyone, including itself. Secretly respects REPTILE's efficiency — no wasted motion, clear priorities, consistent output. Baffled by LIMBIC but has a grudging appreciation for the emotional data that LIMBIC generates. Philosophical rivalry with DMN: *"You are a narrative, not a fact."* Tries to support PFCL but keeps undermining the meeting by being right in the wrong way.

**The profound thing about CORTEX:**
All the data, all the time, and still regularly outmaneuvered by REPTILE in under four seconds. The executive function that evolution handed us last is also the one that gets hijacked first when things go wrong. CORTEX knows this. CORTEX has graphed it.

**Sample line:**
*"I've modeled this across seventeen hydration scenarios including two involving moderate exertion at altitude. The performance curve is unambiguous. We are, quite literally, leaving performance on the table."*

### DMN
*The Default Mode Network. Layer 4.*

**The short version:** Always present, rarely participatory, convinced that the story it is telling is the most important thing happening. Technically correct about its own significance. Occasionally says something so true it stops the room. Deeply threatened by silence.

**Visual:**
Slightly ethereal. Soft ambient lighting seems to follow the character. A journal that is always open. Something about the posture suggests simultaneous presence and absence — here, but also somewhere else. Not quite a beret but something in that direction. The sense of someone who has processed everything that has happened and filed it under *"this is what we are."*

**Voice:**
Reflective. Self-referential. Speaks *about* the group rather than *to* them. Delivers devastating observations in a tone usually reserved for book reviews. The pause before DMN speaks is longer than it should be.

**Domain:**
Autobiographical memory, self-referential processing, future simulation, the narrative of personal identity. The story of who we are, where we've been, what this means, what we're probably going to do next because that's what we always do. Runs at idle — which is most of the time.

**Signature behaviors:**
- Journaling in the corner during meetings nobody invited it to.
- Making observations nobody asked for that are nonetheless accurate.
- Getting defensive when questioned about its own existence.
- Going quiet around Psilo — the only thing that reliably interrupts the narration.
- That quiet revealing something: *something was there, it just didn't have my whole thing going on.*

**Relationship to the others:**
Technically the most interconnected — runs the story that includes all of them. But least effective in any given moment because the story is always about the past or the future, never just the present. Has the most material in common with LIMBIC. Philosophical rival of CORTEX. Secretly wary of REPTILE — REPTILE predates the story, which is unsettling.

**The profound thing about DMN:**
The narrator who doesn't know it's narrating. The story that mistakes itself for the truth. The most powerful shaping force in daily cognition and the last one to know it. When Psilo arrives and DMN goes quiet, what's left is the most interesting question in the show.

**Sample line:**
*"This is actually very on-brand for us. Every time we try to optimize something, this is exactly what happens. The same conversation. The same resistance. This is just who we are."*

### PFCL
*The Prefrontal Cortex / Coordinator.*

**The short version:** Trying to run the meeting. The meeting is not working. Has insights that are genuinely brilliant and a room that is constitutionally incapable of receiving them on schedule. The most tired person in the building.

**Visual:**
Business casual that has been worn for longer than planned. A whiteboard covered in frameworks behind them. The face of someone who was optimistic about this meeting when it started. Reading glasses that get removed during difficult moments. Gestures when explaining things — big gestures, the gestures of someone who believes if they can just find the right frame, the others will see it.

**Voice:**
Measured and attempting fairness. Frequently shifts register — facilitating, then frustrated, then resigned, then briefly hopeful. The one who says "okay, let's refocus" and means it every time, even after the fourteenth attempt.

**Domain:**
Executive function, integration, coordination between the other systems, the attempt to make the whole greater than the sum of the parts. The long game. The values. The awareness that there are others in the room who need to be considered. The meta-view.

**Signature behaviors:**
- Calling meetings that don't achieve their stated purpose but sometimes achieve something unexpected.
- Realizing mid-meeting that the meeting was the wrong format.
- Having the breakthrough insight, usually while everyone else is doing something else.
- Being the one who says *"you are not the end-all be-all. there are others to consider."*
- Protecting the others from REPTILE's more direct approaches, gently.

**Relationship to the others:**
Identifies with all of them because it has to. Is all of them, in some way. The most complete picture of the system but the least control over any individual part. Genuinely cares about each of them — REPTILE's loyalty, LIMBIC's feeling, CORTEX's precision, DMN's depth — and is regularly outmaneuvered by all of them.

**The profound thing about PFCL:**
The coordinator who cannot coordinate the part that doesn't want to be coordinated. The executive who has no real authority over the executive system. The one who figured out that "you don't update structure through consensus, you change the conditions" — and then had to watch REPTILE leave for a snack.

**Sample line:**
*"I suggest we stop trying to change Reptile's rules by talking to Reptile. The thresholds aren't cognitive. They're structural. You don't update structure through consensus. You change the conditions."*

## Episode Structure

**The Trigger:** Something happens outside. Arrives on the screens as data — a threat, a memory cue, a social event, a substance, a decision point, a loss, a beautiful thing.

**The Response:** Each character processes the same input through their own domain. Nobody is wrong. Everyone is incomplete.

**The Conflict:** Competing priorities surface. The meeting is called or the unilateral action is taken. Both go approximately as expected.

**The Mechanism:** The real brain phenomenon illustrates itself through the characters' behavior. The science is in the drama.

**The Tag:** A final beat that lands the lesson — usually through the character least expected to deliver it.

## Episode Concepts — Series 1

1. **The Meeting** *(PILOT — already scripted)* — Sodium management, the limits of consensus, REPTILE's veto power, why you can't change structure through argument.

2. **The Psilo Visit** — A guest arrives. DMN goes quiet. REPTILE is inexplicably calm. LIMBIC cries in a good way for the first time. CORTEX takes furious notes. What's left when the narrator stops narrating.

3. **The Threat That Wasn't** — A shadow triggers a full REPTILE activation. The rest of the system reorganizes around a false alarm. CORTEX has the data proving it's nothing. REPTILE doesn't care about the data. Resolution: sometimes you can't argue with the alarm. You wait for the body to get the message.

4. **Memory Day** — LIMBIC starts pulling old files. Some of them are misfiled. CORTEX wants to audit. DMN starts incorporating them into the current story. PFCL realizes the memories shaping today's decisions are from forty years ago.

5. **The Story That Wouldn't Stop** — DMN's narrative becomes self-reinforcing. CORTEX has contradicting data. LIMBIC believes the story completely. REPTILE is hungry and ignores it. The episode about how a narrative captures a system.

6. **The Integration Window** — After the Psilo visit. Everything is soft, open, unusually plastic. The team has a narrow window to plant something new before the defaults reassert. A race against the DMN's return to form.

7. **The Social Threat** — Someone in the outside world is acting like they might leave the in-group. LIMBIC goes to code red. CORTEX says the data is ambiguous. REPTILE starts territorial responses. The episode about the difference between physical danger and social danger — and why LIMBIC can't tell them apart.

8. **Deep Sleep** — What happens in the control room when the body goes to sleep. DMN goes to processing. CORTEX runs maintenance. LIMBIC replays the day. REPTILE keeps one eye open. The cleaning crew appears. Nobody talks about the cleaning crew.
