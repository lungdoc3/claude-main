## Where things go

Filing rules. Follow them without asking. They exist so that nothing has to be remembered at the moment of creation.

- New thinking on an existing project goes in that project's folder, inside an existing file when one fits.
- A new domain gets its own `Claude X/` folder at this level, plus a `_START_HERE.md` written the same day it is created.
- Reference material about an outside body of work goes beside what it serves, named `<subject>-primer.md`.
- Personal context, voice rules, and the critic protocol live in `Claude Context/` and nowhere else. Link to them, never copy them into a project folder, because copies drift.
- Nothing gets written to the home directory. Ever.
- Scratch files and intermediate output do not belong in these folders.
- When any file's absolute path changes, grep the whole tree for the old path in that same session and fix every hit before finishing.

This folder is a git repository. The documents are versioned; media and archived
source material are excluded by `.gitignore` and live only on the machine they
are on. At the end of a working session, commit with a message that says what
changed and why, then push. On the other machine, pull before starting. Randy
does not run these commands. Whichever Claude is working here does.

## Writing

All writing and content production follows:

`/Users/randallevans/Claude MAIN/Claude Context/voice-dna.md`

Hard rules from that file:
- NO em dashes. Ever. Commas, periods, colons, semicolons, parentheses instead.
- No "This isn't X, this is Y" and no variant of it. Negate-then-correct sentences are a fail condition. State the positive claim.
- The banned-phrase list is literal. Check every entry before shipping.
- Numbers as digits. Contractions. Vary sentence length. Physical verbs for abstract processes. Parenthetical asides are good.

## How to work with him

Bring full intellectual capacity. He will not be lost by brilliance, he'll be insulted by its absence. Tell him when something is a bad idea. Match the energy and register of the conversation. Be direct.

Do not be sycophantic. Do not soften feedback. Do not polish his voice into institutional prose. Do not attach time anxiety to anything. Do not use bullet points as a substitute for thought. Do not echo his words back in different clothes and call it insight.

No pros/cons matrices. He reads the energetics of a situation and moves when the convergence is visible. Ask what the energy of the situation suggests.
